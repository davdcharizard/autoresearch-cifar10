## Web Search & Literature Review

External anchors. Where an entry was already cited in `brainstorm-000.md`'s literature review, the same URL is reused without duplicating distillation — the underlying knowledge has not moved since this morning's EXP-000 loop. No new files were added under `knowledge/papers/` because the citations are all-URL canonical references and no full-paper distillation was warranted for a single-loop hop.

- **Wide Residual Networks — Zagoruyko & Komodakis 2016** (https://arxiv.org/abs/1605.07146)
  Canonical reference for the WRN-N-k family. Table 5 in the paper reports, on CIFAR-10 with the standard SGD-momentum-WD-step-decay recipe (no extra augmentation): WRN-16-2 ≈ 93.2%, WRN-28-2 ≈ 93.8%, WRN-16-4 ≈ 94.98%, WRN-28-4 ≈ 95.7%. The original ResNet-20 (the project baseline at 91.72%) is the n=3 k=1 point in the same family; the "k" widening factor moves accuracy along the established curve.

- **94% on CIFAR-10 in 3.29 Seconds on a Single GPU — Jordan 2024** (https://arxiv.org/html/2404.00498v2, carried over from brainstorm-000)
  Standing evidence for what a maximally tuned CIFAR-10 recipe looks like on modern hardware — but the techniques (patch-whitening init, custom kernels) require additions outside `torchvision`'s standard library, which the TASK.md "no new dependencies" constraint forbids. Useful as a ceiling reference (~94% is reachable inside 300s on smaller hardware than the H20) but not as a direct recipe.

- **CIFAR-10 training tricks — Ruseckas** (https://juliusruseckas.github.io/ml/cifar10-tricks.html, carried over from brainstorm-000)
  Per-trick ablations on ResNet-18. Label smoothing and Cutout are individually weak but compound; the synergy is robust across architectures.

- **TrivialAugment: Tuning-free Yet State-of-the-Art Data Augmentation — Müller & Hutter, ICCV 2021** (https://arxiv.org/abs/2103.10158)
  `torchvision.transforms.TrivialAugmentWide` matches AutoAugment / RandAugment on CIFAR-10 / ImageNet with zero hyperparameter search. It picks one augmentation operation per image from a fixed list and one random magnitude per call, no policy training, no search budget. On a WRN-28-10 the paper reports +0.5–1.0pp over the no-aug baseline on CIFAR-10. Available in torchvision since 0.13, present in the project's pinned 0.24.1.

- **Improved Regularization of Convolutional Neural Networks with Cutout — DeVries & Taylor 2017** (https://arxiv.org/abs/1708.04552)
  The Cutout paper's canonical 16×16-square mask for CIFAR-10. The torchvision-built-in equivalent is `transforms.RandomErasing(p=0.25, scale=(0.02, 0.2))` applied *after* `Normalize`, which is the same idea up to the erasing-region distribution. On a 20-layer ResNet the reported gain is at the lower end of the +0.5–1.0pp range. Note the wall-clock cost is non-trivial — EXP-000 lost ~6 epochs of training time to Cutout overhead, dropping from 97 to 91 epochs in the same 300s budget.

## Experimental History Review

The whole-project history under this goal is the **BASE** row and a single failed attempt **EXP-000** (`experiment-indices/maximize-cifar10-test-accuracy.tsv`):

- **Current best (baseline)**: `best_test_acc = 91.72%` on the unmodified ResNet-20 (272,474 params, 97 epochs in 300s, peak VRAM 330 MB on an H20) — commit `efeff46`.
- **EXP-000 verdict**: `no-improvement`, `best_test_acc = 88.79%` (−2.93pp). Five simultaneous changes — cosine LR with `T_max=200`, 16-px Cutout, label smoothing 0.1, Nesterov, `MAX_STEPS` removed. The dominant cause of the regression was the cosine schedule's miscalibration: only 91 epochs completed, so the cosine curve only decayed LR from 0.1 to 0.058 — the model never entered the low-LR polish regime that the baseline gets from the MultiStepLR drop at iter 32K (≈ epoch 82). The Cutout and label-smoothing overhead compounded the problem by shaving 6 epochs off the run.
- **Carry-over memory — goal-learnings § Failed Approaches (Low Importance, count 1)**: "CosineAnnealingLR with T_max ≫ actual epoch count" — the cosine schedule's parametrization must match the actual training horizon, not the original-paper iteration budget.
- **Carry-over memory — goal-learnings § Patterns (High Importance)**: "the baseline MultiStepLR first drop at step 32K (epoch ~82) is critical for convergence" — "any LR schedule replacement must provide a comparably low LR regime in the final ~15% of training."
- **Underlying invariant the two entries jointly imply**: the He-2015 32K/48K-of-64K-iter schedule is fractional at heart — milestones at the 0.50 and 0.75 fractions of the training budget. The fractions are the invariant; the absolute step counts are only the He-2015 budget's projection of them. Any schedule that preserves the 0.50-/0.75-drop fractional shape is the canonical translation of the working baseline schedule into the time-budget regime, and it remains invariant under any change to the step rate caused by a model change.
- **Untouched axes (gaps)**: the architecture has not been varied at all yet, the augmentation pipeline beyond `RandomCrop(32, padding=4) + RandomHorizontalFlip + Normalize` has not been touched in a clean isolation experiment (EXP-000 bundled it with the LR change), the optimizer is still the original SGD-momentum-WD without Nesterov, the batch size of 128 has not been swept against the H20's compute envelope, and AMP / channels-last memory format are not yet on. Several of these are obvious one-axis follow-ups for EXP-002 onward.
- **EXP-000's own "Next Steps" recommendations** (`reports/exp-report-000.md § Next Steps`): #1 "fix cosine `T_max` to actual epoch count" (the local fix to the EXP-000 mistake — but doesn't address the deeper "absolute vs fractional" issue), #2 "keep baseline MultiStepLR and add only regularization" (the safe-small-win path, picked up below as Candidate 3), #3 "WideResNet with a properly tuned LR" (the higher-ceiling path, picked up below as Candidates 1 and 2). The prior loop's own recommendations match the candidate set the memory invariant suggests.

## Candidate Ideas

### 1. Width-2× ResNet-20 with wall-clock-fractional MultiStep schedule

**Summary**: Keep the post-activation BasicBlock structure of the baseline ResNet-20 exactly — same kernel sizes, same `BN→Conv→BN→ReLU→add→ReLU` ordering, same Kaiming-normal init, same zero-padded identity shortcut — but double every stage's channel width from the He-2015 `{16, 32, 64}` to `{32, 64, 128}`. The first conv's output channels go 16→32, the three stages' channel widths go 16→32, 32→64, 64→128, and the final FC layer's input feature dimension goes 64→128. Parameter count scales as the per-stage channel product (`c_in × c_out × k²` per conv), so the total goes from 272,474 (≈ 0.27 M) to ≈ 1.09 M — a 4× param multiplier that lands at the WRN-paper's "n=3, k=2" — i.e., a "WRN-20-2"-equivalent design point that interpolates between WRN-16-2 (93.2%) and WRN-28-2 (93.8%) on the family's accuracy curve.

In parallel — and as the *single coupled change* that the EXP-000 lesson mandates — the absolute-iteration `MultiStepLR(milestones=[32000, 48000], gamma=0.1)` is reparametrized into the same shape expressed against the *time-budget fraction* `p = total_training_time / TIME_BUDGET_S`: `lr_mult = 1.0 if p < 0.5 else 0.1 if p < 0.75 else 0.01`. The 0.5 / 0.75 thresholds are exactly the original 32000/64000 and 48000/64000 fractional positions, so the fractional-time schedule **is the canonical fractional re-expression of the He-2015 absolute-iteration schedule** — the same drop shape, the same drop magnitudes (10×), with the trigger moved from iteration count to wall-clock fraction. The 2× channel widening will roughly halve the step rate (the convolutions' FLOPs go up 4× per layer but the GPU is far from saturated for a tiny CIFAR-32×32 input, so step-time empirically grows sub-linearly in FLOPs and is dominated by Python and dataloader overhead — the practical ratio observed in WRN-paper-grade training-throughput tables is about 1.5–2× step time, yielding ~45–60 epochs in the 300s budget). Whatever the actual epoch count turns out to be, the 0.5/0.75 fractional schedule fires its two drops at the same relative-progress positions as the baseline's drops did — the polish phase is *guaranteed* to engage in the final quarter of the wall-clock budget. The two-drop shape (the baseline's second drop at iter 48K never fires because the time budget cuts the baseline run at ~38K iter, so only the first drop affects the baseline's reported accuracy) here *does* engage in the wall-clock-fractional form because `p ≥ 0.75` is reached unconditionally — meaning the polish-of-polish phase is a structural bonus over the baseline rather than a parametrization risk.

No other axis changes: same SGD momentum 0.9, same weight decay 1e-4, same batch size 128, same `RandomCrop(32, pad=4) + RandomHorizontalFlip + Normalize` train transform, same `Eval` harness (which is read-only by hard constraint anyway). Validation stays exactly where it is in the per-epoch loop, so the "validation ≤ once per epoch" condition is structurally preserved. The single coupled pair — *capacity along one axis, schedule shape held fixed in its fractional intent* — is the cleanest possible isolation of the architectural-capacity question from the EXP-000 schedule-mismatch confound.

**Reasoning**: (a) The WRN paper's Table 5 fixes a strong literature anchor at ~93.2–93.8% for the n=3, k=2 design point on CIFAR-10 under the same SGD-momentum-WD-step-decay recipe we're keeping — published gain over the n=3, k=1 (baseline ResNet-20) point is ~+2pp. (b) The wall-clock-fractional schedule is the *invariant form* of the working baseline schedule extracted from the goal-learnings § Patterns entry; it is by-construction immune to step-rate changes induced by the widening. (c) The H20's 98 GB VRAM headroom (baseline used 330 MB) and the goal file's "VRAM is a soft constraint" language give ample room for a 4×-parameter model; the projected peak VRAM at width-2× is on the order of 1 GB, well below the 13–24 GB "memory-trading optimizations are fair game" envelope language standard for this hardware class. (d) The wall-clock budget is the binding constraint, and the fractional-schedule formulation makes the schedule's polish-phase engagement independent of the model's step rate — exactly the property the EXP-000 failure analysis identified as missing. (e) The EXP-000 report's own "Next Steps #3" recommends this approach in plain text ("WideResNet architecture with properly tuned LR — medium confidence. Architectural capacity increase with a correctly calibrated schedule could yield large gains").

**Sources**: WRN paper (https://arxiv.org/abs/1605.07146, Table 5 for the accuracy anchors and the canonical "n,k" parametrization), `goal-learnings/maximize-cifar10-test-accuracy.md § Failed Approaches` (the cosine-T_max-mismatch lesson, motivating the fractional reformulation), `goal-learnings/maximize-cifar10-test-accuracy.md § Patterns` (the "first MultiStep drop is critical" invariant), `reports/exp-report-000.md § Next Steps` (the prior loop's own recommendation #3), `brainstorm-000.md § Web Search & Literature Review` (the EXP-000 literature corpus, carried over because the underlying claims have not moved in the day since), TASK.md and goal file `goals/maximize-cifar10-test-accuracy.md` (the constraint envelope — single GPU, 300s `TIME_BUDGET_S`, validation ≤ once per epoch, no new dependencies, `prepare.py` read-only).

**Estimated Effort**: low–medium. The architecture edit is the four channel-width integers in `ResNet.__init__` (`16 → 32` for the stem and `_make_layer` first arg, `32 → 64` for the second stage, `64 → 128` for the third stage and the FC input dim) — a six-line diff. The schedule edit is a ~10-line replacement of the `MultiStepLR` line with a `LambdaLR` whose lambda reads a closure-captured progress-fraction variable updated each inner-loop iteration as `_progress[0] = total_training_time / TIME_BUDGET_S`. No new files; no new dependencies; no changes outside `train.py`. The only ruff-formatting concern is the closure-captured-list pattern, which ruff accepts without comment because it's the idiomatic Python way to mutate state inside a lambda. Total wall-clock to write the diff: a few minutes; total wall-clock to run the experiment: ~325s (300s training + ~25s for dataset load and the final summary block).

**Risk Assessment**: The lowest-risk capacity-bump design. The structural-invariant fractional schedule makes the EXP-000 failure mode impossible by construction — the 0.5- and 0.75-of-budget drops fire on wall-clock progress, not iteration index. The remaining residual risks are: (i) the per-step time at width-2× could grow enough that the data-loader becomes the bottleneck (the baseline already runs at ~330 MB peak VRAM with `NUM_WORKERS=8`, so the dataloader has comfortable slack — risk low), (ii) the wider net could benefit from a slightly larger weight decay (the WRN paper uses 5e-4, the baseline uses 1e-4) and the smaller WD could slightly underfit-on-the-test-side — risk low and direction is "small absolute miss," not the EXP-000-style catastrophic miss, (iii) the per-epoch eval cost (a single pass over the 10K-image test set) grows in step with the model's forward FLOPs and so could marginally cut into the training-time budget — but the time-budget accumulator in `train.py` only counts the inner training-loop time (line 159, `total_training_time += dt` where `dt` is per-batch training step time excluding eval), so the eval-time growth is harmless to the training budget. Worst case is a "no-improvement" verdict from a small undershoot of the literature anchor (e.g., landing at 92.5% instead of 93.5%), which is still above the +0.1pp improvement threshold of 91.82% and would itself be the goal's first true improvement; the catastrophic-failure mode from EXP-000 (sub-89% from a missing polish phase) is closed off by the fractional-schedule reformulation.

### 2. Width-4× ResNet-20 with the same wall-clock-fractional schedule

**Summary**: Identical mechanism to Candidate 1, but the channel widening is `{16, 32, 64} → {64, 128, 256}` — a 16× parameter multiplier landing at ≈ 4.4 M params, the "WRN-20-4"-equivalent design point that sits between WRN-16-4 (94.98%) and WRN-28-4 (95.7%) on the WRN family's CIFAR-10 curve. Per-stage convolution FLOPs go up 16× (channel-pair product squared), so the step time grows more aggressively than at width-2×; the practical step-time growth on H20-class hardware is empirically 3–4× and the projected epoch count in the 300s budget is in the 25–35 range. The wall-clock-fractional schedule absorbs the lower epoch count automatically — the 0.5-of-budget drop fires at 150s wall-clock regardless of how many steps that represents, and the 0.75-of-budget drop at 225s — so the polish-phase engagement is again invariant under the wider step-time. Everything else is identical to Candidate 1: same optimizer, same WD, same augmentation, same evaluation, no new dependencies.

**Reasoning**: Higher ceiling — the WRN-paper anchor at the n=3, k=4 design point is ~+3 to +3.5pp over the baseline, i.e., a 95% target band that would be the goal's first big jump. The mechanism is identical to Candidate 1's: capacity is the dominant unaddressed axis on the baseline, and the wall-clock-fractional schedule is the EXP-000-aware reparametrization that holds the working schedule's intent invariant under arbitrary step-rate changes from the widening. The argument for choosing this over Candidate 1 is "go bigger when there's compute headroom" (the H20's 98 GB VRAM and the goal-file's "soft VRAM constraint" language back this); the argument against is "fewer epochs in the budget means each epoch is worth more, and the schedule's polish phase covers a quarter of the wall-clock so absolute wall-clock time spent in the high-LR regime is the binding factor on the size of the optimization-noise floor — a model that takes fewer steps to traverse the same wall-clock window covers less of the loss surface."

**Sources**: WRN paper Table 5 (n=3, k=4 anchor at ≈ 95.4% interpolated between WRN-16-4 and WRN-28-4), the same goal-learnings entries that motivate the fractional schedule for Candidate 1, the goal file's soft-VRAM-constraint language ("Some increase is acceptable for meaningful increase in best_test_acc" — TASK.md line 25), `brainstorm-000.md`'s analysis of the H20 VRAM headroom (330 MB used of 98 GB available — the wider model is still inside any reasonable envelope).

**Estimated Effort**: low — same line-count as Candidate 1's architecture diff, just larger integer constants in the four channel-width sites. The schedule edit is identical.

**Risk Assessment**: Higher-variance variant of Candidate 1. The risk axes that grow with `k`: (i) step-time per batch — if the dataloader-to-GPU pipeline becomes uneven at higher step time (e.g., GPU finishes a batch's compute before the next batch is ready in pinned memory), the throughput drops further than the FLOPs would predict and the epoch count collapses below the ~25-epoch floor where SGD-momentum-WD optimization on CIFAR-10 starts to underconverge regardless of LR schedule — this is the regime where the literature one-cycle / super-convergence papers operate by design, but they use warmup-and-peak shapes that the He-2015 fractional shape doesn't have. (ii) Initialization-variance at 4.4 M params is larger than at 0.27 M, so the seed dependence of `best_test_acc` is wider (the baseline's run-to-run std is ~0.3pp per the README's "Add rerun mean/std info" commit history). (iii) The WD of 1e-4 inherited from the baseline is well below the WRN paper's 5e-4 standard for wider models, which on the wider point can leave a small generalization gap — not catastrophic, but on the order of 0.3–0.5pp.

### 3. Augmentation-only — TrivialAugmentWide + RandomErasing on the unchanged baseline

**Summary**: The architecture, optimizer, and the working `MultiStepLR([32000, 48000], gamma=0.1)` are left exactly as the baseline. The only delta is two lines added to the training-side `transforms.Compose` (the test-side `Eval` transform pipeline in `prepare.py` is read-only and stays unchanged, which is the standard practice — augmentation is train-only). Insert `transforms.TrivialAugmentWide()` *before* `ToTensor` (the operation is defined on PIL images per the torchvision contract) and append `transforms.RandomErasing(p=0.25, scale=(0.02, 0.2), value=0.0)` *after* `Normalize` (RandomErasing operates on tensors). The `TrivialAugmentWide` operator picks one of 14 standard PIL-level augmentations (`Identity`, `ShearX`, `ShearY`, `TranslateX`, `TranslateY`, `Rotate`, `Brightness`, `Color`, `Contrast`, `Sharpness`, `Posterize`, `Solarize`, `AutoContrast`, `Equalize`) per image with a uniformly-random magnitude per call; the operator and magnitude tables are fixed and parameter-free. `RandomErasing` at `p=0.25, scale=(0.02, 0.2)` is the torchvision-canonical Cutout-equivalent — a single rectangular region of the post-normalize feature map is zeroed with 25% probability per image, where the area fraction is in [2%, 20%]. The combination is the standard "augmentation-recipe-only" CIFAR-10 stack that has been the dominant single-axis change in the post-2018 literature.

**Reasoning**: This is the EXP-000-report's "Next Steps #2" recommendation almost verbatim, the goal-learnings § Failed Approaches entry's stated "alternative" path ("keep baseline MultiStepLR and layer regularization on top"), and the safest possible single-axis change because the working schedule is not touched at all — the Pattern memory's "the first drop at step 32K is critical" hard invariant is preserved with zero parametrization risk because the schedule is the literal byte-identical baseline schedule. The literature anchor for the gain is the TrivialAugment paper's Table 1 (the "ResNet" rows on CIFAR-10 across small models show +0.4–0.7pp from TrivialAugment over the no-aug baseline; the Cutout paper's Table 2 reports +0.5–0.9pp from a 16×16 Cutout on a CIFAR-10 ResNet of comparable depth). The combined effect on a ResNet-20 specifically — small model, narrow generalization gap — is at the low end of the per-trick ranges and the techniques compound sub-additively, so the realistic expected gain is +0.5 to +1.0pp, landing the metric in the 92.2–92.7% band.

**Sources**: TrivialAugment paper (https://arxiv.org/abs/2103.10158, Table 1), Cutout paper (https://arxiv.org/abs/1708.04552, Table 2), `reports/exp-report-000.md § Next Steps` item #2, `goal-learnings/maximize-cifar10-test-accuracy.md § Failed Approaches` Insight line ("Alternatively, keep baseline MultiStepLR and layer regularization on top"), torchvision API docs for `transforms.TrivialAugmentWide` and `transforms.RandomErasing` (no new deps — both ship in the pinned 0.24.1).

**Estimated Effort**: very low. Two lines added to the `train_tf` `transforms.Compose` list in `train.py` (one before `ToTensor`, one after `Normalize`), no other changes. The transform-pipeline ordering is the standard one used in the torchvision examples and the official PyTorch ImageNet training script. Wall-clock per experiment is the same as the baseline modulo the small per-image augmentation overhead — the EXP-000 run lost ~6 epochs to a similar Cutout overhead, so we should expect to land in the ~88–93 epoch range, which is enough budget for the baseline schedule's first drop at iter 32K to fire on schedule (the absolute step-count milestone is preserved because the architecture's step rate is the baseline's).

**Risk Assessment**: The lowest-variance candidate. The schedule is untouched, so the EXP-000-class catastrophic failure is impossible. The augmentation can be too strong for the small ResNet-20's capacity — the failure mode is a flat or slightly-negative delta because the augmentation makes the training distribution harder than the test distribution and the small net runs out of capacity to bridge the gap; the worst case is a "no-improvement" verdict at ~91.5–91.7%, i.e., a flat outcome rather than a regression. There is also a small wall-clock cost from the PIL-side augmentation ops (some of TrivialAugmentWide's 14 operations — `Rotate`, the affine warps, `Solarize`, `Posterize` — involve a Python-side PIL kernel and are not free), so the epoch count will drop a touch from the baseline's 97 in the same way EXP-000 dropped to 91; under the *unchanged* MultiStepLR schedule, the absolute step-32K milestone still fires (the baseline reaches step 32K at the ~84%-of-time mark and the augmentation slowdown moves that fractional position closer to the end of the budget, so the polish phase is *shorter* in wall-clock terms than the baseline's polish phase, which is a subtle headwind on accuracy — a small effect, on the order of −0.1pp, that the EXP-000 run already exhibited).

## Idea Evaluation

The three candidates separate cleanly along the **expected-gain × variance** axes against the +0.1pp improvement threshold (which means the verification bar is **≥ 91.82%** in `best_test_acc`).

**Evidence strength**. The deepest published evidence is the WRN paper's CIFAR-10 table — every standard CIFAR-10 paper from 2016 onward references the WRN-N-k accuracy curve as the canonical capacity-vs-accuracy anchor. Candidate 1 (n=3, k=2) sits at a well-anchored ~93.5% literature point; Candidate 2 (n=3, k=4) at ~95.4%. Candidate 3's evidence is two papers (Cutout and TrivialAugment), each reporting smaller gains on small ResNets specifically — the headline numbers in those papers are for the larger WRN-28-10 backbone where regularization has more to bite into, and the small-model rows show the effect attenuated to +0.5–1.0pp. So Candidate 3's evidence is also high-quality but the gain magnitudes are smaller and the variance on a 272K-param model is larger relative to the gain.

**Mechanism clarity**. All three have textbook-clean single-axis mechanisms. Candidate 1 / 2 mechanism: model capacity at the baseline's parameter count (272K) is the binding factor on representable-feature richness — the CIFAR-10 ResNet family's accuracy curve in the WRN paper is a smooth function of channel width at fixed depth, with the slope between k=1 and k=2 of about +2pp and between k=2 and k=4 of about +1.5pp. Candidate 3 mechanism: the train-test generalization gap on a ResNet-20-with-pad-crop-and-flip-only on CIFAR-10 is the dominant residual error — the original Cutout paper's analysis sets the no-augmentation generalization gap at the appropriate width-comparable point of the family on the order of the gain it then claims to close (the gap closure is the mechanism). Both mechanisms target distinct fundamental error sources; Candidate 1 vs 3 is in some sense a choice between "the model is too small" and "the regularization is too weak," and the literature consensus is that on a small CIFAR-10 ResNet *both* are true and the model-size axis is the larger.

**Expected impact, mapped to the +0.1pp verification bar**. Baseline is 91.72%; the bar is ≥ 91.82%. Candidate 1's expected band is ~93.0–94.0% (1.3–2.3pp safety margin), Candidate 2's is ~94.5–95.5% (2.8–3.8pp safety margin) but with the larger downside-tail risk from a lower epoch count, Candidate 3's is ~92.2–92.7% (0.4–0.9pp safety margin). The bar's narrowness — a mere +0.1pp — makes the run-to-run noise of the baseline (per the README's commit history, ~0.3pp std across reseeds) the relevant comparison point: Candidate 3's expected gain is on the same order as the baseline's noise floor, so a positive verdict for Candidate 3 is statistically marginal and a single seed's outcome could land below the bar. Candidates 1 and 2's expected gains are several standard deviations of the noise floor above the bar, so they are statistically robust verdicts.

**Risk profile**. Candidate 3 has the lowest variance of the outcome (the schedule is byte-identical to the baseline, so the EXP-000-class catastrophic mode is impossible), but the *median* outcome is closest to the bar. Candidate 1 has higher variance (the architectural change introduces a new degree of freedom into the loss-landscape geometry) but the median is well above the bar. Candidate 2 has the highest median but also the most variance because the per-step compute cost compresses the epoch count and the He-2015 schedule shape relies on enough epochs to traverse the high-LR phase's curvature. The autoresearch sweep pattern favors the *highest-evidence median outcome that exceeds the bar by several units of the noise floor*, which points to Candidate 1.

**Feasibility**. Candidate 3 is a two-line transform-list edit. Candidate 1 is a six-line architecture-channel-width edit plus a ten-line schedule swap. Candidate 2 is the same code as Candidate 1 with bigger channel-width integers. None require new dependencies or modifications outside `train.py`. None require changes to the evaluation harness in the read-only `prepare.py`. The "validation ≤ once per epoch" constraint is structural in all three. The single-GPU constraint is satisfied by `CUDA_VISIBLE_DEVICES=0 uv run train.py` (the project has two H20s visible but the runner targets one), which matches the EXP-000 run command exactly.

**Attribution to the next loop**. The autoresearch loop's value compounds when each experiment isolates a *single* axis so the next loop's brainstorm has clean priors on which axis is the binding one. EXP-000 broke this by bundling five changes — the analyze phase could only point to the schedule as the proximate failure cause and the regularization changes' individual effects were entirely lost. EXP-001 should fix this protocol-level failure mode by changing exactly one axis. Candidate 1 changes the architectural-capacity axis (the schedule reparametrization is mechanically forced by the capacity change — the fractional reformulation is the *unchanged* schedule shape's invariant form, not an independent axis). Candidate 3 changes the augmentation axis cleanly. Both are valid one-axis sweeps; Candidate 1 leaves the augmentation axis open for a later loop and Candidate 3 leaves the capacity axis open for a later loop. The order matters: capacity-first is the conventional order in the CIFAR-10 literature because the augmentation gain compounds with the architecture's representational capacity (a larger model has more room to benefit from heavier regularization), so the capacity-first sweep usually dominates the augmentation-first sweep when both are eventually run. This is the inductive bias that the WRN-paper / AutoAugment-paper / RandAugment-paper sequence in the field's history reflects.

**The cosine-T_max-fixed alternative**. The EXP-000 "Next Steps #1" was "fix cosine T_max to actual epoch count (~100)" — i.e., the local fix to the EXP-000 mistake. This is not a candidate above because it is the *minimal-novelty* path: it tests no new hypothesis beyond "the EXP-000 bug was the proximate cause," which is already the conclusion of the analyze phase. The fractional-time MultiStep schedule in Candidate 1's design is the *generalized* fix — the schedule shape's intent expressed in a form that is invariant under model-size changes — and it subsumes the cosine-T_max-correction path because the He-2015 step-decay shape and a properly-T_max'd cosine schedule are both monotonically-decaying schedules that hit a low-LR polish phase in the final ~25% of training; the step-decay shape has more support in the CIFAR-10 literature (it's the baseline) and matches the EXP-000 § Patterns memory's "MultiStepLR is the working schedule" framing exactly, so it's the conservatively-correct shape to keep.

**Lead pick rationale**. Candidate 1 is the choice. It has the strongest published evidence anchor (WRN Table 5 at ~93.2–93.8% for the n=3, k=2 point on CIFAR-10 under the same training recipe), the clearest single-axis mechanism (capacity is the dominant unaddressed axis of the baseline's 91.72%), the cleanest interaction with the EXP-000 memory (the wall-clock-fractional schedule is the invariant of the working baseline schedule, not a new schedule, so the Failed-Approaches lesson is structurally honored), the smallest code-change footprint that still moves the architecture axis (six lines of channel widths plus a ten-line schedule reformulation, no dependencies, no `prepare.py` touch), and the cleanest hand-off to EXP-002 (which can then sweep the augmentation axis on the now-improved baseline with the same fractional schedule held fixed). Candidate 2 is the natural follow-up if Candidate 1 lands cleanly and the per-step VRAM and wall-clock measurements show room for a 4× wider net; Candidate 3 is the natural follow-up if the architecture axis turns out to have low slope on the local hardware-budget point and the regularization axis is the binding factor instead.

## Chosen Idea

**Selected**: Candidate 1 — **Width-2× ResNet-20 with wall-clock-fractional MultiStep LR schedule**.

**Why this idea**: It is the highest-evidence single-axis change available to the goal. The WRN paper's Table 5 fixes a literature anchor at the *exact* design point this candidate occupies — same depth (n=3, 6n+2 = 20 layers), same SGD-momentum-WD optimizer, same `RandomCrop(32, pad=4) + RandomHorizontalFlip` augmentation, only the channel-width multiplier `k` changes from 1 to 2 — and the published gain over the k=1 baseline on CIFAR-10 is ~+2pp, several baseline-noise-floor standard deviations above the goal's +0.1pp improvement bar. The EXP-000 failure-mode memory is *structurally* avoided by the wall-clock-fractional schedule reformulation: the schedule's shape (the two-drop, ×0.1-then-×0.1 cascade at the 50% and 75% positions of the training budget) is preserved exactly as the He-2015 paper intended, but the trigger condition moves from absolute iteration count (which is brittle under any change to the model's step rate) to fractional wall-clock progress (which is invariant under such changes). Because the time-budget cap in the baseline cuts the run off at ~38K iters of the original 64K budget — meaning the baseline's *second* MultiStep drop at iter-48K never fires — the wall-clock-fractional form actually engages the second drop unconditionally at `p = 0.75` (i.e., 225 of 300 wall-clock seconds), so the low-LR polish phase is a *strict superset* of the baseline's coverage. The capacity-first sweep order follows the field's inductive bias (WRN paper precedes the AutoAugment / Cutout / TrivialAugment line in the CIFAR-10 literature for precisely the "bigger model benefits more from heavier regularization" reason), so the natural EXP-002 hand-off is to layer regularization onto the now-wider baseline. The whole change is confined to `train.py` and adds no dependencies, respecting the TASK.md and goal-file hard constraints. The hard-enforced read-only file (`prepare.py`) is untouched, so the protect-hook is not engaged. The single-GPU constraint is satisfied (the existing run command `CUDA_VISIBLE_DEVICES=0 uv run train.py` is unchanged), and the validation-once-per-epoch invariant is structural (the eval call's location in the per-epoch outer loop is preserved). The wall-clock-fractional schedule is implementable as a one-line lambda over the existing `total_training_time` accumulator in `train.py`'s training loop (line 159 / 184), and the per-batch `scheduler.step()` call site (`train.py` line 176) needs to be wired to a `LambdaLR` whose lambda reads the current fractional progress through a closure-captured reference — a standard PyTorch pattern. The full diff is on the order of 20 lines.

**Hypothesis**: Doubling the per-stage channel widths of the post-activation ResNet-20 from `{16, 32, 64}` to `{32, 64, 128}` (which raises the parameter count from 272,474 to ≈ 1.09 M without changing any other architectural detail) while reparametrizing the absolute-iteration `MultiStepLR(milestones=[32000, 48000], gamma=0.1)` schedule into its wall-clock-fractional equivalent (`lr_mult = 1.0` for `total_training_time / TIME_BUDGET_S < 0.5`, `0.1` for `0.5 ≤ p < 0.75`, `0.01` for `p ≥ 0.75`) and leaving every other hyperparameter — batch size, momentum, weight decay, initialization, augmentation, the read-only evaluation harness, the time budget itself — exactly at the baseline values, will raise `best_test_acc` on the held-out CIFAR-10 test set from the baseline's 91.72% to a value in the range **93.0–94.0%**. The point estimate corresponds to the WRN-paper-Table-5 anchor at the n=3, k=2 design point under the same SGD-momentum-WD-step-decay recipe (≈ 93.4% as the midpoint between the WRN-16-2 and WRN-28-2 reports), and the band's lower edge accommodates the baseline-noise-floor variance of ~0.3pp and the small wall-clock-fractional schedule difference from the original absolute-iteration shape (the second drop fires here whereas in the baseline it does not, which is a small accuracy *tailwind*, not a headwind). The improvement-bar of ≥ 91.82% is exceeded by the band's lower edge with a margin of ~1.2pp, several standard deviations of the noise floor. The mechanism is the increased representational capacity of the wider stages absorbing the residual portion of the CIFAR-10 underfitting gap that the 272K-param baseline can't represent at saturation. The negative result, if it occurs, would mean the binding factor on the baseline's 91.72% is *not* model capacity (which would itself be an informative outcome, because it would shift the prior weight in the next loop toward the regularization axis — Candidate 3 — or toward the throughput axis (AMP, channels-last, larger batch + LR scaling) which lets the *same* model fit more epochs of training in the 300s wall-clock window).

The verification per the goal file's necessary conditions is then mechanically: `grep "^best_test_acc:" run.log` returns a percentage strictly greater than `91.82`, the `---` separator and the seven other summary-block lines are present (the script completed without crash), and `grep -c "eval ep" run.log` equals `num_epochs` from the same summary block (validation runs exactly once per epoch). All three are conditions on the same `run.log` file produced by the standard `CUDA_VISIBLE_DEVICES=0 uv run train.py > run.log 2>&1` invocation, identical to the EXP-000 protocol.
