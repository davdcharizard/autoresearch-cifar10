A multi-line comment-block above the cell-and-function names the schedule's invariance-of-the-He-shape rationale and cites the brainstorm-001 and plan-001 files under `.autoresearch/`. (4) The inner training loop's wall-clock accumulator at the line `total_training_time += dt` (this is actually at `train.py:180` in the file's current numbering, not the `181` the plan-001 prose mentions — see § Surprises) gets a one-line follower `_lr_progress[0] = total_training_time / TIME_BUDGET_S` immediately below it, so the lambda sees the up-to-the-end-of-this-iteration progress fraction on the next iteration's `scheduler.step()` call. The whole construct is local to the `main()` function so its lifetime is tied to the per-training-run scope rather than module-import-time-singleton scope, which matches the lifecycle of the wall-clock accumulator the cell mirrors. ### Surprises & Discoveries

The headline non-obvious property of the diff is the fractional-rewriting of the He-2015 absolute-iteration schedule into the wall-clock-fractional coordinate system. The He-paper's MultiStepLR milestones of `[32000, 48000]` over the 64K-iteration budget are *exactly* the fractional positions `0.5` and `0.75` of that budget — i.e., the original schedule was a budget-fractional schedule expressed in the iteration coordinate the He paper happened to use. The wall-clock-fractional reformulation `p = total_training_time / TIME_BUDGET_S` with the same threshold pair `(0.5, 0.75)` is therefore the literally-equivalent rewriting of the same schedule expressed in the coordinate system that matches the project's time-budgeted-rather-than-iteration-budgeted training regime, not a *new* schedule. The cascade-multipliers `1.0 / 0.1 / 0.01` returned by the lambda multiply the optimizer's initial LR of `0.1` to produce the absolute LR sequence `0.1 / 0.01 / 0.001`, which is bit-identical to the He-paper schedule's absolute LR sequence under its `gamma=0.1` factor applied at the two milestones starting from the same initial LR. So the schedule-axis of the diff is a *parametrization* change (the trigger coordinate moves from absolute-iteration-count to wall-clock-fraction-consumed), not a shape change (the staircase shape, the drop magnitudes, the threshold-position-as-a-fraction-of-the-budget — all unchanged). This is the property that makes the schedule-axis "the same as the baseline expressed in the project's actual budget coordinate" rather than "a different schedule that needs its own tuning." The EXP-000 goal-learnings entry's lesson "any schedule replacement must provide a comparably low LR regime in the final ~15% of training" is honored by-construction because the second drop at `p = 0.75` puts the final 25% of the wall-clock budget into the lowest-LR plateau `LR = 0.001`, a strict superset of the baseline's coverage (which under the time-budget cap at ~38K iterations and the original-paper-budget of 64K only fires the first milestone at iter-32K, never reaching the iter-48K second milestone — so the EXP-001 fractional schedule actually fires *both* drops in the same wall-clock budget that the baseline gets only one drop in, a small accuracy *tailwind* on the polish-phase coverage rather than a regression risk). The EXP-000 cosine-T_max-miscalibration failure mode is structurally impossible here because the schedule is not a cosine and the trigger condition is wall-clock-fractional rather than absolute-iteration-keyed, so the wider model's reduced step rate (which would have broken any absolute-iteration-trigger) doesn't disrupt the drop timing.

The cross-document line-number consistency check turned up a one-off-by-one offset between the plan-001 prose and the actual `train.py` line numbering: the plan's `## Code Changes > Change 4` paragraph says "insert the new line *immediately after* the existing `total_training_time += dt` line at line 181 and *before* the `step += 1` line at line 182," but the actual file has `total_training_time += dt` at line 180 (with the prior lines being the `torch.cuda.synchronize()` at line 178 and the `dt = time.time() - t0` at line 179) and `step += 1` at line 181. The line-number-anchor mismatch is a one-line offset that the Edit-tool's content-based anchor matching is insensitive to — the unique-substring anchor `"total_training_time += dt\n            step += 1"` matches the unique two-line region in the file regardless of which integer line numbers the file's prefix happens to be at, so the diff applies at the correct location with the new `_lr_progress[0] = total_training_time / TIME_BUDGET_S` line landing between them. The off-by-one in the plan-001 prose is the kind of small line-number-counting error a human prose-writer makes when referencing the file's structure by eye (it's the canonical "off-by-one" off-by-one), and the Edit-tool's content-based mechanism is the standard defense. The exp-log records this so the analyze phase's narrative doesn't get tripped up cross-referencing plan-text-line-numbers to actual-file-line-numbers.

A third minor discovery is the `num_params` discrepancy between the README's prose-quote and the in-process readout. The README at `/SPXvePFS/users/david/autoresearch-cifar10/README.md`'s "Baseline: ResNet-20" section paragraph and the example-output-block both reference the baseline parameter count as `272,474`. The prior EXP-000 run's summary block (from the project-root `run.log` whose tail-of-summary-block was visible in the discovery turn) reads `num_params: 269,722`, a 2,752-parameter difference. The cause is the BN-affine-parameter-accounting convention: PyTorch's `nn.BatchNorm2d` exposes the affine `weight` and `bias` learnable parameters of size `num_features` in `.parameters()` (which the `sum(p.numel() for p in model.parameters())` call captures) and excludes the non-learnable `running_mean`, `running_var`, and `num_batches_tracked` buffers (which the same call does not capture because the buffer-registry is `model.buffers()`, not `model.parameters()`). The "272,474" figure in the README appears to be from a different parameter-counting convention — possibly the original He-2015-paper's count which includes the BN-affine in a different way (one common variant in academic papers' parameter counts is to include or exclude the BN affine based on whether the paper treats it as part of the convolution-layer's parameters or a separate normalization-layer's parameters), or possibly a hand-counted value from a slightly different network variant (the original He paper has a "Option A" and "Option B" identity-shortcut form, where "Option A" — the zero-padding variant the current `BasicBlock` uses at `train.py:50-56` — has no projection convolution and so fewer parameters than "Option B" which adds a 1x1 conv at each inter-stage transition; the 272,474 vs 269,722 difference of 2,752 is on the order of the missing BN-affine pair-of-tensors of width-16 across the eighteen BN layers in the network, which is approximately 16*2*18 = 576 parameters per BN-affine, suggesting the 2,752 difference is roughly 5x the per-BN-affine pair count — not an exact match, but in the right order of magnitude for an accounting-convention discrepancy). The framework-side number is the authoritative one for the actual model and the verification-phase's `num_params` informational metric extracts the in-process value, so the analyze phase's "the architecture is the size we expected" sanity check uses the framework number, not the README's quoted number, as the comparison reference. The width-mult-2 expected reading at the same convention is on the order of `4 × 269,722 = 1,078,888` modulo the linear-in-channel BN-affine and FC-bias terms which scale 2× rather than the conv-tensor's 4×, so the actual reading will land slightly below the strict 4× upper bound.

A fourth observation: the dataset-cache discovery showed no `./data` directory at the project root, so the EXP-001 launch is the first run on this machine that touches the torchvision CIFAR-10 wrapper, which means the first-time-`download=True` branch fires and the wrapper fetches the ~163-MB CIFAR-10 Python-pickle tarball from the canonical `cs.toronto.edu/~kriz/cifar-10-python.tar.gz` mirror. The download takes ~5-30 seconds depending on the network — the EXP-000 run's `startup_seconds` field was at the warm-cache value of a few seconds per the README's example summary block (where the example shows `startup_seconds: 3.2`), so either the EXP-000 run had a warm cache from an earlier setup-time run or the data directory was wiped between EXP-000 and EXP-001 (which is plausible if a cleanup script or a session reset between the early-morning EXP-000 loop and the early-afternoon EXP-001 loop emptied the project root). The training-time-budget machinery in `train.py` captures the wall-clock time at `t_start_training = time.time()` *after* the dataset construction (the `train_set = datasets.CIFAR10(...)` and the `train_loader = DataLoader(...)` lines at `train.py:126-136` precede the `t_start_training` capture at line 155), so the download time lands in the summary block's `startup_seconds` field outside the time-budget-accumulated `training_seconds` field, and the full 300 seconds of the time budget are spent on the training loop's gradient-step inner cycle as expected. The total process wall-clock is the sum of the download time, the CUDA initialization and cuDNN autotune for the first iteration's chosen kernels, the 300 seconds of the training-loop's time budget, the cumulative per-epoch evaluation wall-clock across the run's ~45-60 epochs (each per-epoch eval is a pass over the 10K-image CIFAR-10 test set at batch-size-256 which is ~0.5-1.0 seconds on the H20, so the cumulative eval wall-clock is ~25-60 seconds), and the final summary-block print and Python interpreter teardown of a fraction of a second. The total is in the 330-400-seconds end-to-end window, well within the abort-criteria's 600-second (10-minute) hard ceiling and the harness's default Bash-tool timeout of 600 seconds (which I'll raise to 900 seconds — 15 minutes — on the background-launch Bash call to give the first-run dataset download a comfortable margin against an unusually-slow network).
