# Plan EXP-001: Width-2× ResNet-20 with Wall-Clock-Fractional MultiStep LR
- **Created**: 2026-05-27
- **Goal**: goals/maximize-cifar10-test-accuracy.md
- **Brainstorm**: brainstorm/brainstorm-001.md

The hypothesis under test (verbatim from `brainstorm-001.md § Chosen Idea`): doubling the channel widths of the post-activation ResNet-20 from `{16, 32, 64}` to `{32, 64, 128}` while reparametrizing the `MultiStepLR([32000, 48000], gamma=0.1)` schedule into its wall-clock-fractional equivalent (drops at the time-budget fractions `p = 0.5` and `p = 0.75`, matching the original He-2015 32K-of-64K and 48K-of-64K iteration fractions) and leaving every other hyperparameter at its baseline value will raise `best_test_acc` from the baseline 91.72% to a value in the 93.0–94.0% band — comfortably above the goal-file's `91.82%` improvement bar with a margin of ~1.2pp, several standard deviations of the run-to-run baseline noise floor of ~0.3pp. The mechanism is the increase in representational capacity from the 4× parameter multiplier (the per-conv `c_in × c_out × k²` term grows as the channel-pair product), and the fractional schedule preserves the working baseline schedule's intent (the He-2015 fractional-position drops at 50% and 75% of the budget) under the wider model's reduced step rate. The literature anchor is the Zagoruyko-Komodakis 2016 Wide Residual Networks paper's Table 5, which places the n=3, k=2 design point on the CIFAR-10 accuracy curve between the published WRN-16-2 result (5.66% test error = 94.34% accuracy on CIFAR-10 in the unaugmented setting, the closest depth-shallower point) and the WRN-28-2 result (5.20% test error = 94.80% accuracy, the closest depth-deeper point); the interpolated anchor for n=3, k=2 is approximately 94.5% under the original WRN paper's 200-epoch SGD-momentum-WD-cosine recipe, and the discount for the 300s wall-clock-budget regime (which only fits ~45-60 epochs at width-mult-2 instead of the WRN paper's 200 epochs) lands the realistic target at the 93.0-94.0% band, which is what the hypothesis claims.

The carry-over memory entries from the prior EXP-000 loop are honored by-construction: the goal-learnings' Failed Approaches row about `CosineAnnealingLR(T_max=200)` over only 91 actual epochs is sidestepped because the schedule here is a wall-clock-fractional step decay, not a cosine, and its drop triggers are anchored on the *fraction* of the 300s training-time budget consumed rather than on any absolute iteration count, so the wider model's lower step rate (which would have broken any absolute-iteration-based schedule) is automatically absorbed. The goal-learnings' Patterns row about the baseline's first MultiStepLR drop at iter 32000 (which falls at the ~84%-of-wall-clock mark in the baseline because the baseline's `MAX_STEPS = 64000` cap is non-binding and the 300s time budget cuts the loop at ~37800 iterations) is honored because the wall-clock-fractional schedule fires its first 10×-LR drop at exactly `p = 0.5` (i.e., the 150-second mark of the 300-second wall-clock budget, which is *earlier* than the baseline's effective ~84% position so the low-LR regime gets *more* wall-clock-time coverage in EXP-001 than it does in the baseline — a side-channel tailwind on accuracy), and the second drop at `p = 0.75` (the 225-second mark, the final quarter of the budget) gives a polish-of-polish phase that the baseline's MultiStepLR-with-time-budget-cap never actually reaches because its second milestone at iter 48000 would arrive at the ~127%-of-wall-clock mark, well past the 300-second budget. Thus the EXP-001 schedule shape is a strict superset of the baseline's low-LR coverage and matches the original He-2015 schedule's intent exactly when expressed in budget-fractional terms.

**Change 2 — multiply the six channel-width literals in `ResNet.__init__` by `WIDTH_MULT`.**

The `ResNet` class's `__init__` at `train.py:60-69` contains six integer literals that encode the per-stage channel widths and the FC-input feature-dimension of the canonical CIFAR-variant ResNet (the He et al. 2015 Table 6 row labeled "stage `conv1`: 16, `conv2_x`: 16, `conv3_x`: 32, `conv4_x`: 64"). Each literal is wrapped in a `WIDTH_MULT` multiplication, expressed as `16 * WIDTH_MULT` / `32 * WIDTH_MULT` / `64 * WIDTH_MULT` so the diff visibly preserves the baseline-paper-canonical channel ratios while making the multiplier explicit. The six edit sites are:

- Line 63: `self.conv1 = nn.Conv2d(3, 16, 3, stride=1, padding=1, bias=False)` → `self.conv1 = nn.Conv2d(3, 16 * WIDTH_MULT, 3, stride=1, padding=1, bias=False)`. The stem 3×3 convolution maps the 3-channel RGB input to the first stage's input feature map; its output-channel count is the first stage's channel width.
- Line 64: `self.bn1 = nn.BatchNorm2d(16)` → `self.bn1 = nn.BatchNorm2d(16 * WIDTH_MULT)`. The stem batch-norm's `num_features` must match the stem-conv's output-channel count.
- Line 65: `self.layer1 = self._make_layer(16, 16, num_blocks, stride=1)` → `self.layer1 = self._make_layer(16 * WIDTH_MULT, 16 * WIDTH_MULT, num_blocks, stride=1)`. The first stage's input and output channel counts match (stride 1, no spatial downsample, no channel-dimension change inside the stage by the standard He-2015 design).
- Line 66: `self.layer2 = self._make_layer(16, 32, num_blocks, stride=2)` → `self.layer2 = self._make_layer(16 * WIDTH_MULT, 32 * WIDTH_MULT, num_blocks, stride=2)`. The second stage downsamples spatially (32×32 → 16×16) and doubles the channel count at the first block of the stage; the input-channel count is the first stage's output-channel count and the output-channel count is the second stage's width. The `_make_layer` helper at `train.py:78-85` handles the per-block channel-pair logic correctly because the input-channel argument is the first BasicBlock's input-channel count and the helper's stride-list `[stride] + [1] * (num_blocks - 1)` ensures only the first block in each stage carries the downsample/projection.
- Line 67: `self.layer3 = self._make_layer(32, 64, num_blocks, stride=2)` → `self.layer3 = self._make_layer(32 * WIDTH_MULT, 64 * WIDTH_MULT, num_blocks, stride=2)`. The third stage downsamples again (16×16 → 8×8) and doubles the channel count from the second stage's output to the third stage's width. The 8×8 feature-map spatial size is what the `F.adaptive_avg_pool2d(out, 1)` call on line 92 averages over to produce the per-image feature vector of length equal to the third stage's channel count.
- Line 68: `self.fc = nn.Linear(64, num_classes)` → `self.fc = nn.Linear(64 * WIDTH_MULT, num_classes)`. The final fully-connected classification head's input-feature dimension equals the third stage's output-channel count, which is the dimension of the global-average-pooled feature vector at line 92's output. The output dimension is the unchanged `num_classes = 10`.

The `BasicBlock` class definition at `train.py:33-58` does not change — its `__init__` takes the in-channel and out-channel counts as arguments (`def __init__(self, in_channels, out_channels, stride=1)`) and the `_make_layer` helper at `train.py:78-85` is the one feeding the per-block-pair channel-count arguments, so the channel-doubling is mediated entirely by the six `__init__` edits above. The identity-shortcut's channel-pad logic at `BasicBlock.forward`'s lines 50-56 — which zero-pads the residual's extra-channel-dimension when the channel count grows at the inter-stage transition — works correctly for any channel pair because the pad amount is computed at construction time as `out_channels - in_channels` (line 47, `self.pad_channels = out_channels - in_channels if self.need_pad else 0`) and the pad is applied symmetrically in the channel dimension at forward time (line 55, `F.pad(shortcut, (0, 0, 0, 0, 0, self.pad_channels))` with the 6-tuple-of-pad-counts arg targeting the channel dimension as the third-from-last). The 2× channel widening preserves the same channel-pair ratios at each stage boundary (16→32 becomes 32→64, 32→64 becomes 64→128), so the pad-counts double in absolute terms but the relative-channel-growth-factor is unchanged at 2× per stage transition — the identity-shortcut's zero-padding semantics are unaltered.

The Kaiming-normal initializer at `train.py:71-76` (the `_weights_init` static method, applied to every `nn.Conv2d` and `nn.Linear` module via the `self.apply(self._weights_init)` call on line 69) is variance-preserving across channel counts by design (it scales the weight variance as `2 / fan_in` per He et al. 2015 — the same "Delving Deep into Rectifiers" paper the docstring on line 72 cites), so the wider model's initialization is statistically equivalent to the baseline's. No initializer change is needed.

The parameter count grows from the baseline's measured 269,722 (from the EXP-000 run.log's `num_params: 269,722` line, the actually-measured value as opposed to the README's 272,474 quote which differs by a small BN-parameter-accounting margin) to a width-mult-2 value on the order of 4× the baseline because the dominant terms are the per-conv parameter counts which scale as the channel-pair product `c_in × c_out × k²`. The exact predicted value can be derived from the breakdown: the stem conv is 3 input channels × 16 × WIDTH_MULT output channels × 3 × 3 kernel = 9 × 3 × 16 × 2 = 864 params at width-mult-2 vs 432 at width-mult-1 (a 2× growth because only the output dimension widens — the input is the 3-channel RGB image); each stage's blocks have two 3×3 convs whose parameter count scales as `(c × k)^2 × num_pixels` modulo the BN-affine `2c` term per conv, so the per-stage parameter count grows as the channel-width squared (4× for the inner-stage block convs); the stage-transition blocks have a `c_in × c_out × k²` first conv where the channel pair both double, giving an `4×` growth, and the second conv is `c_out × c_out × k²` which is also `4×`; the FC head's `64 × WIDTH_MULT × num_classes = 128 × 10 = 1280` params at width-mult-2 vs 640 at width-mult-1 (a 2× growth because the output dimension `num_classes` is unchanged). The sum-of-parts net growth is dominated by the per-stage block convs and approaches 4× as the channel-width-squared term dominates the linear-in-channel BN-affine and FC-output terms. The exact predicted parameter count for width-mult-2 is therefore on the order of 1.08 M, which the verification's informational-metric grep on the summary block's `num_params:` line will read out and confirm.

**Change 3 — replace the absolute-iteration MultiStepLR with a wall-clock-fractional LambdaLR.**

The existing scheduler construction at `train.py:145-147` is:

```python
scheduler = optim.lr_scheduler.MultiStepLR(
    optimizer, milestones=[32000, 48000], gamma=0.1
)
```

The replacement defines the wall-clock-fractional decay function and instantiates a `LambdaLR` over it. The mutable state cell that the lambda reads is initialized just above the scheduler construction at the same local-scope level as the optimizer, so its lifetime is per-`main()`-call and not module-global. The exact replacement spans the same three source-lines that the original `MultiStepLR` does, plus the new mutable-cell line and the new decay-function definition, for a net diff of about ten added lines and the three original lines deleted. The patch is:

```python
# (immediately after the existing `optimizer = optim.SGD(...)` line at train.py:142-144
#  and before the existing scheduler construction line that this change replaces)

_lr_progress = [0.0]  # mutable cell read by the LambdaLR closure below; updated each
                      # inner-loop iteration with total_training_time / TIME_BUDGET_S so
                      # the schedule's drop triggers are anchored on wall-clock fraction
                      # of the time-budget rather than on absolute iteration count. The
                      # one-element-list pattern is the standard Python idiom for a
                      # closure variable mutated by the enclosing function's body.

def _wall_clock_fractional_step_decay(_step_idx):
    """LR multiplier as a piecewise-constant function of wall-clock-fraction-consumed.

    Returns the He-2015 step-decay cascade applied at the original paper's fractional
    milestones — first drop at iter 32_000 / 64_000 = 0.5 of the iteration budget, second
    drop at iter 48_000 / 64_000 = 0.75 of the iteration budget — translated from the
    iteration-coordinate that the paper used into the wall-clock-time-fraction coordinate
    that the project's TIME_BUDGET_S regime defines. The PyTorch LambdaLR API passes the
    current scheduler step index as the argument; it is ignored here because the schedule
    is wall-clock-keyed, not step-count-keyed. The progress fraction comes from the closure
    over _lr_progress, which the training loop updates each iteration."""
    p = _lr_progress[0]
    if p < 0.5:
        return 1.0
    elif p < 0.75:
        return 0.1
    else:
        return 0.01

scheduler = optim.lr_scheduler.LambdaLR(optimizer, lr_lambda=_wall_clock_fractional_step_decay)
```

The `LambdaLR` API contract from the PyTorch documentation (the `torch.optim.lr_scheduler.LambdaLR` page) is that `lr_lambda` is a callable from the integer step-index to a multiplier on the optimizer's initial per-param-group LR. The initial LR is the value passed to `optim.SGD(..., lr=LR, ...)` at the optimizer construction time, which is the constant `LR = 0.1` from the hyperparameters block. So the cascade of returned multipliers — `1.0`, then `0.1`, then `0.01` at the two threshold-crossings — produces the absolute LR sequence `0.1 → 0.01 → 0.001`, which is the exact He-2015 two-step `gamma=0.1` cascade starting from the same initial LR of 0.1. The fractional schedule is therefore *bit-equivalent* to the baseline `MultiStepLR(milestones=[32000, 48000], gamma=0.1)` shape in the *original-paper-budget* regime (64K-iteration budget) and *strictly-supersets* the baseline's actual time-budget-truncated coverage of the polish phase (the baseline at 300s wall-clock fits ~38K iterations and so the iter-48K second drop never fires — meaning the baseline gets the first drop at the ~84%-of-wall-clock mark and never reaches the third LR plateau of 0.001, whereas EXP-001's fractional form fires the first drop at the 50%-of-wall-clock mark and the second drop at the 75%-of-wall-clock mark, giving the third LR plateau the entire final 25% of the wall-clock budget to operate). The "the EXP-000 schedule reformulation kept the high-LR regime for the whole run" failure mode is structurally precluded because the fractional schedule's mid-budget transition is at the literally-50%-mark of the wall-clock window, which is the He-paper's fractional position. The EXP-000 cosine-T_max-mismatch failure is precluded because the schedule is not a cosine and its trigger condition is wall-clock-fraction, not step-index — there's no analog of `T_max` to mis-set.

The per-batch `scheduler.step()` call at `train.py:176` stays exactly where it is and the inner-loop ordering of `optimizer.zero_grad()` → forward pass → loss → backward → `optimizer.step()` → `scheduler.step()` is preserved. The standard PyTorch convention — "the LR scheduler is stepped after the optimizer to advance the per-step schedule index" — is unchanged. The `_lr_progress` cell is read by the lambda which the scheduler-step machinery dispatches to.

**Change 4 — drive the `_lr_progress` cell from the training-loop's existing wall-clock accumulator.**

The training loop at `train.py:162-203` accumulates per-batch wall-clock time in the variable `total_training_time` (initialized at `train.py:157` to `0.0` and incremented at `train.py:181` by `total_training_time += dt` where `dt = time.time() - t0` from line 180). Insert the line `_lr_progress[0] = total_training_time / TIME_BUDGET_S` *immediately after* the existing `total_training_time += dt` line at line 181 and *before* the `step += 1` line at line 182. The insertion is one new line and is the only modification to the inner training loop. It ties the fractional-progress reader-cell to the wall-clock-time-budget-fraction-consumed quantity in the canonical place where that quantity is already being computed each iteration.

The semantic is: at the end of iteration *i*'s forward-backward-step block, the cumulative training-time has been bumped by `dt_i` for that iteration's wall-clock-cost, and the fractional-progress reader cell is updated to reflect that cumulative value as a fraction of the total budget. The very next iteration *i+1*'s `scheduler.step()` reads the freshly-updated reader cell via the `LambdaLR`'s `lr_lambda` dispatch and computes the corresponding multiplier from the cascade. The lambda is evaluated at the moment of `scheduler.step()` (which is once per `scheduler.step()` call, the PyTorch contract), so the LR for iteration *i+1*'s optimizer step is the LR computed from iteration *i*'s end-of-iteration progress fraction — a one-iteration phase shift that is negligible at the 0.5/0.75 thresholds when each iteration is ~10ms and the 50%-mark falls at the 150-second mark.

**Change 5 (purely cosmetic, optional) — extend the model-info print line at `train.py:140` to surface the width-multiplier.**

The existing line at `train.py:140` prints `print(f"ResNet-{6 * NUM_BLOCKS + 2} | params: {num_params:,}")`. A small string-format extension makes the experimental knob's value visible in `run.log`'s startup section: `print(f"ResNet-{6 * NUM_BLOCKS + 2} × WIDTH_MULT={WIDTH_MULT} | params: {num_params:,}")`. The change is purely informational — the analyze phase's report can read both fields from the same `grep "ResNet-" run.log` line — and is the only place in the diff that touches a print-format string. If the change feels like scope creep relative to the "minimal-axis" principle, it can be omitted; the `num_params:` summary-block line at the end of the run already lets the analyze phase verify the architecture's parameter count against the expected ~1.08 M width-mult-2 anchor. The plan lists this change as Optional and the execute phase can include or skip it without affecting the experimental outcome.

**What is NOT changed**: the SGD optimizer's parameters at `train.py:142-144` (`lr=LR=0.1`, `momentum=MOMENTUM=0.9`, `weight_decay=WEIGHT_DECAY=1e-4`, no `nesterov=True` — the brainstorm-001's Candidate-1 spec keeps the optimizer pristine for a later single-axis sweep), the train-side augmentation pipeline at `train.py:117-124` (`transforms.RandomCrop(32, padding=4)`, `transforms.RandomHorizontalFlip()`, `transforms.ToTensor()`, `transforms.Normalize(mean=(0.4914, 0.4822, 0.4465), std=(1, 1, 1))` in that order — these match the He-2015 paper's CIFAR augmentation and the project's existing channel-mean / unit-std normalization convention that the README's "Channel-wise mean instead of per-pixel mean" footnote documents, and the Eval-side test transform in `prepare.py:13-20` uses the same normalization so the train/test feature distributions stay aligned), the cross-entropy loss at `train.py:173` (`F.cross_entropy(outputs, targets)` without the `label_smoothing` kwarg that EXP-000 added — kept off so the regularization axis is clean for a future loop), the dataloader's `batch_size=BATCH_SIZE=128, shuffle=True, num_workers=NUM_WORKERS=8, pin_memory=True, drop_last=True` settings at `train.py:129-136`, the `torch.manual_seed(42)` and `torch.cuda.manual_seed(42)` calls at `train.py:108-109` (the seed is left at the baseline's 42 so the run-to-run noise is at the baseline's noise floor), the `gc.collect()` call after the first epoch at line 215 (a memory-housekeeping kludge from the original code that helps under tight VRAM and is harmless under the H20's 98 GB envelope), the `torch.cuda.synchronize()` at line 178 (which ensures the per-batch `dt = time.time() - t0` measurement at line 179 captures the GPU's actual finish time, not the CUDA-queue-submit-time — this is the canonical "measure GPU time correctly" pattern and is essential for the wall-clock accumulator's accuracy), and the `MAX_STEPS = 64000` constant at line 24 (which is the original He-2015 64K-iteration budget cap; the wider model's reduced step rate makes this cap non-binding because the time-budget at `TIME_BUDGET_S = 300` cuts the run well before 64K steps are reached — the cap is dead code under the wall-clock-fractional regime and a small follow-up loop could remove it, but the minimal-diff principle of EXP-001 leaves it in place so the diff surface is strictly the four-line widening + the ten-line schedule replacement, no incidental cleanup).

## Configuration Changes

The configuration deltas, in the goal-file's reading order:

- **Channel-width multiplier**: implicit `1` (baseline-He-2015 channels `{16, 32, 64}`) → `WIDTH_MULT = 2` constant added to the hyperparameters block, applied to all six channel-width sites in `ResNet.__init__`. Rationale: the WRN paper's Table 5 places the n=3 (20-layer-depth) k=2 (channel-width-doubled) point on the CIFAR-10 accuracy curve at the ~94.5% literature anchor under the same SGD-momentum-WD-cosine-200-epoch recipe family; the brainstorm-001's mechanism argument is that the baseline's 269,722-parameter network is in the "underfitting on capacity" regime where the train-test gap is small but the train-set loss floor is above the achievable floor of a slightly-wider network, and the canonical fix is to widen the per-stage channel counts while keeping the depth, the block structure, the optimizer, the augmentation, and the loss exactly at the baseline values so the only varying axis is capacity. The factor of 2 (rather than the WRN paper's k=4 or k=8 settings) is the conservative widening that still moves the metric while keeping the per-step compute cost in the regime where the time-budget allows enough epochs for the schedule's two-step decay to traverse all three LR plateaus.

- **LR schedule trigger coordinate**: absolute iteration index (`milestones=[32000, 48000]` of the implicit `MAX_STEPS=64000` budget under the original He-paper protocol, which the baseline inherits but which is dead-code-truncated by the 300-second wall-clock budget at the ~38K-iteration mark in the baseline-run regime) → wall-clock-time fraction (`total_training_time / TIME_BUDGET_S`, with the same two thresholds `0.5` and `0.75` matching the original He-paper's `32000/64000` and `48000/64000` fractional positions). Rationale: the goal-learnings' Failed Approaches entry from EXP-000 ("CosineAnnealingLR with `T_max=200` over only 91 actual epochs decayed LR only to 0.058 — never reaching the low-LR regime") and the Patterns High-Importance entry ("the baseline's first MultiStepLR drop at iter 32K is critical for convergence — any schedule replacement must provide a comparably low LR regime in the final ~15% of training") together imply that the schedule shape must be the He-2015 two-step cascade and the trigger condition must be anchored on the *fraction* of the wall-clock budget consumed rather than on any absolute iteration count, because any model-architecture change shifts the step rate and breaks an absolute-iteration trigger. The fractional reformulation is the canonical "express the schedule in the coordinate of the actual budget" translation and is invariant under any change to the step-rate, so the wider model's reduced step count doesn't disrupt the polish-phase engagement that the Pattern memory flagged as critical.

- **LR schedule shape and gamma**: `MultiStepLR.gamma = 0.1` applied twice (once at each of the two milestones) starting from `LR = 0.1` → `LambdaLR` returning the cascade `1.0 / 0.1 / 0.01` at the two thresholds, which multiplies the constant initial `LR = 0.1` from the optimizer's construction to produce the absolute LR sequence `0.1 → 0.01 → 0.001`. This is *bit-identical* to the baseline's intended schedule shape — the original He-2015 schedule under the 64K-iteration budget is exactly `LR = 0.1` for iterations `[0, 32000)`, `LR = 0.01` for iterations `[32000, 48000)`, and `LR = 0.001` for iterations `[48000, 64000)`, which the wall-clock-fractional form reproduces as `LR = 0.1` for the first half of the 300s budget, `LR = 0.01` for the third quarter (150-225s), and `LR = 0.001` for the final quarter (225-300s). The baseline's effective coverage is the first segment for almost all of its 300s and the start of the second segment for the last ~16% of its 300s (the iter-32K milestone falls at the ~84%-of-wall-clock mark when the step rate yields ~38K iters in 300s); the EXP-001 fractional schedule's coverage is the first half in the first half of the budget, the second segment in the third quarter, and the third segment in the final quarter — a strict superset that adds the iter-48K-equivalent third-segment coverage that the baseline never reaches. This extra coverage at the lowest LR plateau is a small accuracy tailwind, not a regression risk.

- **Initial LR, momentum, weight decay**: unchanged (`LR = 0.1`, `momentum = 0.9`, `weight_decay = 1e-4`). The WRN paper uses `weight_decay = 5e-4` rather than the original ResNet paper's `1e-4`, which is a follow-up axis worth a separate loop (the wider model can absorb slightly more L2-shrinkage without underfitting) but the minimal-axis principle of EXP-001 keeps the optimizer settings at the baseline values so the attribution of any accuracy delta is clean against the capacity-and-its-induced-schedule-reparametrization axis. The Nesterov flag stays at its default `False` (a follow-up axis).

- **Batch size**: unchanged (`BATCH_SIZE = 128`). The H20's compute headroom would support a much larger batch (the baseline's 330 MB peak VRAM is 0.3% of the card's 98 GB), and a `bs=512` with linear LR scaling to `LR=0.4` is the standard "large-batch CIFAR-10" recipe that exploits the modern hardware envelope, but that is the throughput axis and is a follow-up. Keeping `bs=128` matches the baseline's batch statistics so the BatchNorm running-statistics' contribution to the test-time normalization is at the baseline's level — a small but real effect that would confound an isolated-capacity comparison if the batch size also changed.

- **Augmentation**: unchanged. The train-side `transforms.Compose([RandomCrop(32, padding=4), RandomHorizontalFlip(), ToTensor(), Normalize(mean=(0.4914, 0.4822, 0.4465), std=(1, 1, 1))])` matches the original He-2015 CIFAR augmentation exactly and matches the eval-side normalization in `prepare.py:13-20` exactly. The brainstorm-001's Candidate-3 ("TrivialAugmentWide + RandomErasing on top of the baseline") is the natural EXP-002 follow-up because the wider model has more capacity to absorb stronger augmentation — the WRN-paper-style "wider model + AutoAugment" recipe is exactly that compounding effect — and isolating the capacity axis from the regularization axis in EXP-001 gives the EXP-002 brainstorm a clean attribution baseline.

## Abort Criteria

The conditions under which the execute phase should kill the running training and record the experiment as a failure without proceeding to the verification protocol. These cover the in-flight failure modes that the time-budget machinery and the harness's bash-tool timeout don't already handle, and they are designed to be observable from the partial state of `run.log` while the process is still alive.

- **No output in `run.log` for 2 minutes after the launch timestamp.** The dataset-cache check, the CUDA initialization, and the first epoch's first 50-step print should all happen within the first 60 seconds of wall-clock from the launch. A `run.log` that is empty or has only the initial `Device: cuda` line after 2 minutes indicates a hang in the data-pipeline or CUDA-init phase (the canonical failure modes are a stuck `torchvision.datasets.CIFAR10(..., download=True, ...)` over a non-network or a stuck CUDA-context-creation on a GPU that another process is holding). Resolution: `pkill -f "uv run train.py"` or send SIGINT to the process group, then inspect the last-few-lines of the partial log via `tail -n 20 run.log` for a clue. If the cause is the GPU being held by another process, the milestone-2 check that `nvidia-smi -i 0` shows the GPU idle before launch is the prevention.

- **Python traceback in `run.log`.** A `Traceback (most recent call last):` line followed by stack frames in the log indicates an unhandled exception; the process exits with non-zero status on its own and the log is fully flushed before exit (because the standard Python interpreter flushes stderr on exit-via-unhandled-exception and the shell's `2>&1` merges stderr into the redirect-target). Resolution: read the traceback's bottom-most frame to identify the failing line in `train.py`, decide whether the bug is in the EXP-001 diff (in which case fix the diff, re-commit, re-run) or in the environment (a stale CUDA cache, a torchvision version mismatch — both unlikely given the EXP-000 run successfully exercised the same Python environment 75 minutes earlier today per the file timestamps on the `.autoresearch/` artifacts). The harness's bash-tool exit-code reporting will surface the non-zero exit immediately.

- **Total wall-clock exceeds 600 seconds (10 minutes) without the summary block appearing.** The summary block's `---` separator at the start is the canonical end-of-run signal. A wall-clock past 10 minutes without the separator means either the time-budget machinery has somehow failed (the outer-loop's exit-condition `total_training_time < TIME_BUDGET_S` would have to be stuck at a `total_training_time` value below 300, which would require the `total_training_time += dt` accumulation at line 181 to have stopped firing — the only realistic mechanism is a deadlock in the inner-loop's `torch.cuda.synchronize()` call at line 178 caused by an underlying CUDA error that the synchronize wasn't able to surface as an exception, a rare driver-level failure) or the process is hanging on the per-epoch eval (the `evaluator.evaluate(...)` call at line 205 is outside the time-budget accumulator and runs to its natural completion, but on a stuck CUDA context the call would never return and the outer-loop's next iteration's `time.time() - t0` measurement would not happen, freezing the wall-clock accumulator). Resolution: hard-kill the process group, write an entry to `.autoresearch/infra-errors.md` under the appropriate severity tier (Critical if the failure repeats on a clean re-launch, Important otherwise — the prior EXP-000 run completed cleanly so a single-run hang is in the Important-flake category, not the Critical-blocker category), and treat the experiment as a `crash` verdict. The harness's foreground-bash-tool default 10-minute timeout will fire this kill automatically; the abort criterion is the documented contract for what to do when it fires.

- **Loss values printed in `run.log` go to `nan` or `inf`.** The training-loss EMA accumulator at `train.py:185-188` computes a bias-corrected exponential moving average of the per-iteration loss values, and the EMA's debiased value `debiased = smooth_train_loss / (1 - ema_beta**step)` is printed in the per-50-step progress line at line 197 as the `loss:` field. A `nan` or `inf` reading there indicates numerical divergence in the training — the standard causes are an unsuitably-high initial LR, a missing gradient-clip on an unstable architecture, or a labeled-data corruption that produces a one-hot target outside the `[0, num_classes)` range. None of these are expected for the EXP-001 diff: the LR of 0.1 is the He-2015 paper's LR for the canonical schedule and is known-stable on the small CIFAR-10 dataset with batch size 128 (the original ResNet paper's CIFAR experiments demonstrated stability at this LR), the architecture is a standard pre-activation-style ResNet block with BN-normalized layers and Kaiming-initialized weights (the variance-preserving init), the dataset is the canonical torchvision CIFAR-10 wrapper which validates the labels at load-time, and the wider-channel variant changes none of the numerical-stability properties of the baseline. So a `nan` / `inf` observation would be a strong signal of an unexpected interaction, most likely an environment-side issue (CUDA driver corruption, a hardware ECC error on the H20's memory) rather than the algorithm. Resolution: kill the process, examine the partial log for the iteration at which the `nan` first appeared and the preceding loss trajectory (a sudden jump from a finite value to `inf` indicates a divergent gradient step; a gradual climb of the loss to large finite values followed by `nan` indicates a positive-feedback loop in the gradient norm), and either re-run from the same git commit (idempotency check) or investigate the hardware via `nvidia-smi --query-gpu=ecc.errors.uncorrected.aggregate.total,ecc.errors.corrected.aggregate.total --format=csv,noheader`.

- **The per-50-step LR readout in the log does not transition at the expected wall-clock-fractional positions.** This is the EXP-001-specific consistency check that the goal-learnings' Failed Approaches memory motivates: the wall-clock-fractional schedule's two LR drops should fire at the 150-second and 225-second wall-clock marks (the `p = 0.5` and `p = 0.75` thresholds against the `TIME_BUDGET_S = 300` budget), and the per-50-step log line at `train.py:197` prints the current LR field as `lr: {lr:.4f}`. The expected log trace is the sequence `0.1000 ... 0.1000 0.0100 ... 0.0100 0.0010 ... 0.0010` with the transitions occurring at the 50%-of-time-budget and 75%-of-time-budget points, which the log's `dt:` field per print and the implicit elapsed-wall-clock between prints allow to be reconstructed approximately (the log's `pct_done:` field at line 197 is the literal `100 * total_training_time / TIME_BUDGET_S` value and is the closest in-log reading of the fractional progress; an LR transition at the print line where `pct_done` first exceeds 50.0% and the LR-field-printout flips from `0.1000` to `0.0100`, and a second transition at the `pct_done = 75.0%` point flipping from `0.0100` to `0.0010`, is the expected pattern). If the log's LR readout does not show this two-step staircase aligned to the `pct_done` field's transitions, the wall-clock-fractional schedule is misconfigured — most likely the `_lr_progress[0]` cell isn't being updated by the inner-loop line, or the lambda is reading from the wrong scope, or the `LambdaLR` initialization isn't seeing the lambda. This is a *post-hoc* abort criterion that the verification phase checks, not a mid-run kill criterion, because the schedule's misbehavior is silent at runtime (the LR is still a finite value, just the wrong one, so the training loop completes its 300-second wall-clock with a no-improvement or sub-baseline best_test_acc that looks like the EXP-000-style schedule-mismatch failure mode). The verification phase's defense against this mode is the `grep "lr: 0.0100" run.log | head -1` and `grep "lr: 0.0010" run.log | head -1` commands to confirm the two LR plateaus appear in the log, and the corresponding `awk` extraction of the `pct_done` field on the same print lines to confirm the plateaus' onset fractional-position is in the right neighborhood. The full schedule-correctness check is a script-level argument the analyze phase makes in the experiment-report's `## Results` section; the abort-criterion bullet here is the heads-up that the check exists and is the EXP-001-specific protocol addition motivated by the EXP-000 lesson.

## Verification Protocol

### Verification Procedure

The three necessary conditions from `goals/maximize-cifar10-test-accuracy.md § Verification > ### Necessary Conditions` are evaluated in the goal-file's listed order. The baseline value `91.72` is the current value of the `baseline:` field in the experiment-index TSV's header line (`.autoresearch/experiment-indices/maximize-cifar10-test-accuracy.tsv`'s first line, queryable via the canonical helper `bash "/SPXvePFS/users/david/Deoxys/plugins/autoresearch/skills/shared/scripts/exp-index.sh" baseline <tsv-path>` which prints `baseline=91.72` among other fields, the standard machine-readable key-value form the autoresearch suite uses for cross-script baseline lookup). The improvement threshold is therefore `91.72 + 0.1 = 91.82`, the value the `best_test_acc` reading from the new run's `run.log` must strictly exceed. Each condition's command, the field it extracts, the pass/fail semantics, and the upstream-timeout-or-error handling are spelled out below.

The order is short-circuit: on the first FAIL, the remaining conditions are recorded as "skipped" in the experiment-log's `## Verification Results § Conditions Checked` section (matching the EXP-000 log's format at lines 64-72 of `.autoresearch/logs/exp-log-000.md`, which has the literal entries "Condition 1: FAIL — ...", "Condition 2: skipped — aborted after prior failure", "Condition 3: skipped — aborted after prior failure"). The verdict-classification table is the analyze phase's responsibility, but the conventional mapping is: all-three-pass → `improvement`, condition-1-fail-with-summary-block-present → `no-improvement`, condition-2-fail → `crash`, condition-3-fail → `invalid` (the eval-count-vs-epoch-count check is the structural-hard-constraint check from the goal-file's third necessary condition, and a violation is a protocol-level failure not a metric-level failure, so the verdict is "invalid" rather than "no-improvement").

The verification harness is purely the standard Unix shell utilities — `grep` (with the `-c` count flag and the `^anchor:` line-start anchor in the extended-regex `\|`-alternation form), `head -n 1` (to take the first match in case of any spurious extra matches in the log's body, since the field-name labels are unique to the once-per-run summary block and shouldn't appear elsewhere, but the defensive `head -1` is the canonical idiom), `awk '{print $2}'` (to extract the whitespace-separated second field from the matched line, which is the printf format's value-after-the-colon-and-spaces), `tr -d '%'` (to strip the trailing `%` from the percentage-formatted accuracy values), and the shell's `[ ... -gt ... ]` arithmetic-comparison form combined with the `bc -l` arbitrary-precision-arithmetic tool for the floating-point comparison (`bash` itself only does integer arithmetic with `(( ... ))`, and the `best_test_acc` value is a two-decimal-digit float like `93.47`, so the comparison `[ "$(echo "$best_acc > $threshold" | bc -l)" = "1" ]` is the standard idiom; the EXP-000 plan's verification section uses the equivalent extraction pattern). All commands are runnable from the project root `/SPXvePFS/users/david/autoresearch-cifar10` via a `cd` to that directory at the start of the verification block, or by passing the absolute path of `run.log` to the grep commands.

**Condition 1 — `best_test_acc` strictly exceeds the baseline by at least 0.1 percentage points.**

The summary-block line in `run.log` matching the regex `^best_test_acc: +([0-9]+\.[0-9]+)%$` (the printf format from `train.py:228` is `"best_test_acc:    {best_acc:.2f}%\n"` with four spaces of padding between the colon and the value, and a trailing percent sign) is the source of the metric value. The extraction is:

```bash
LOG="/SPXvePFS/users/david/autoresearch-cifar10/run.log"
INDEX="/SPXvePFS/users/david/autoresearch-cifar10/.autoresearch/experiment-indices/maximize-cifar10-test-accuracy.tsv"

BEST_ACC=$(grep "^best_test_acc:" "$LOG" | head -n 1 | awk '{print $2}' | tr -d '%')
BASELINE=$(bash "/SPXvePFS/users/david/Deoxys/plugins/autoresearch/skills/shared/scripts/exp-index.sh" baseline "$INDEX" | grep "^baseline=" | cut -d= -f2)
THRESHOLD=$(echo "$BASELINE + 0.1" | bc -l)
PASSED=$(echo "$BEST_ACC > $THRESHOLD" | bc -l)
```

The two checks the procedure performs are: (a) `$BEST_ACC` is non-empty (a missing summary-block line yields an empty `grep` output, which the downstream `awk | tr` chain propagates as an empty string and the `bc -l` comparison fails in a defined way returning a non-`1` value); and (b) the `bc -l`-computed boolean `$PASSED` is the literal string `1` (the standard `bc` truth-value convention is `1` for true, `0` for false, and `bc -l` handles the floating-point comparison via the IEEE-754-conformant `>` operator). On `$PASSED == 1`, condition 1 PASSES and the procedure advances to condition 2. On `$BEST_ACC` empty or `$PASSED == 0`, condition 1 FAILS and the verdict is recorded as `crash` (in the first sub-case — the run did not produce a summary-block) or `no-improvement` (in the second sub-case — the run produced a summary block and the metric is at-or-below the threshold). The analyze phase's verdict-classification logic disambiguates the two FAIL sub-cases by checking condition 2 unconditionally; the protocol order in the goal file lists condition 1 first, so the analyze phase's report shows condition 2 as "checked even though condition 1 failed, to disambiguate crash-vs-regression" in the no-improvement case and as "FAIL" alongside condition 1 in the crash case. The standard semantics here matches the EXP-000 analyze phase's treatment of its `best_test_acc: 88.79%` reading, which was a non-empty extraction from a fully-completed summary block, so the verdict was `no-improvement` rather than `crash`.

The upper-bound timeout on the condition-1 check is the shell-tool's default few-second timeout on the four commands (`grep`, `head`, `awk`, `tr`, `bc`, `cut`) and the `exp-index.sh` script's read-of-the-TSV-header, all of which complete in milliseconds against the small file sizes involved (the TSV is single-digit-of-lines, the `run.log` is hundreds of lines), so the upper bound is the harness's bash-tool default timeout of 10 minutes which is the same hard ceiling as the experiment-process timeout. No condition-1-specific timeout is needed.

**Condition 2 — the script completes without crashing and prints the full summary block.**

The summary block at the end of `run.log` consists of the literal `---` separator line from `train.py:227` and the ten metric-name-labeled lines at lines 228-237: `best_test_acc:`, `final_test_acc:`, `final_test_loss:`, `training_seconds:`, `total_seconds:`, `startup_seconds:`, `peak_vram_mb:`, `num_epochs:`, `num_steps:`, `num_params:`. The completeness check counts the occurrences of each anchor and asserts the expected counts (the separator should appear exactly once, the ten field-names should each appear at least once — the once-per-run convention is enforced by the single-pass-through-main()-at-end-of-training semantics of the training script).

```bash
SEP_COUNT=$(grep -c "^---$" "$LOG")
FIELD_COUNT=$(grep -E -c "^(best_test_acc|final_test_acc|final_test_loss|training_seconds|total_seconds|startup_seconds|peak_vram_mb|num_epochs|num_steps|num_params):" "$LOG")
```

The pass condition is `SEP_COUNT == 1 && FIELD_COUNT >= 10` — the inequality on the field count allows for the possibility that one of the metric labels also appears in a per-epoch debug-print line elsewhere in the log, which it doesn't in the current `train.py` (the per-epoch eval line at line 211 is the only other line referencing the test metrics and it uses the prefix `test_loss: / test_acc:` rather than `final_test_loss: / final_test_acc:`, so no overlap), but the inequality is the defensive form. The FAIL outcomes are: a missing `---` separator (`SEP_COUNT == 0`, the process did not reach the end-of-`main()` print sequence — either it crashed mid-run with a traceback that's now in the log instead, or it was killed by the abort-criteria timeout) and a partial field set (`FIELD_COUNT < 10`, indicating that the summary block started printing but didn't finish — this would happen only in a SIGKILL-during-print race that interrupts the Python stdout buffer flush, an extremely unlikely outcome).

On condition 2 PASS the procedure advances to condition 3. On condition 2 FAIL the verdict is `crash` and the experiment-log's `Errors & Dead Ends` section records the partial-log-content at the kill point.

**Condition 3 — validation runs at most once per epoch.**

The per-epoch eval print line at `train.py:210-212` is the once-per-epoch newline-terminated line of the form `  eval ep {epoch:3d} | test_loss: {f:.4f} | test_acc: {f:.2f}% | best: {f:.2f}%` (a two-space-indent prefix, the literal `eval ep ` token, a 3-digit-right-aligned epoch number, and three colon-separated metric fields). Counting the occurrences of this line in the log and comparing the count against the summary-block's `num_epochs:` field gives the per-epoch eval-count check.

```bash
EVAL_LINES=$(grep -c "^  eval ep " "$LOG")  # the leading two-space-indent makes the match specific to the eval-line format
NUM_EPOCHS=$(grep "^num_epochs:" "$LOG" | head -n 1 | awk '{print $2}')
```

The pass condition is `EVAL_LINES == NUM_EPOCHS` — exactly one eval line per epoch. The goal-file's wording "validation must not run *more than* once per epoch" is asymmetric and would technically permit `EVAL_LINES < NUM_EPOCHS` (some epochs evaluated zero times), but the structural property of the training script's once-per-epoch eval call site at line 205 — which is inside the outer `while` loop and fires at the end of each completed inner-batch-loop pass — guarantees an exact-one-per-epoch firing in the normal path (the only way to get `< 1` per epoch is for an epoch to exit the inner loop via the `if total_training_time >= TIME_BUDGET_S or step >= MAX_STEPS: break` clause at line 202-203, in which case the inner loop's `break` falls through to the outer loop's natural-iteration-end which then runs the eval call once at line 205 — so even the time-budget-truncated final epoch gets one eval line, matching the increment of the `epoch` counter at the top of each outer-loop iteration). The EXP-000 log's structure confirms this — the EXP-000 summary block's `num_epochs: 91` matches the count of "eval ep" lines in the EXP-000 log (the exact match is what the EXP-000 plan-000's verification protocol's condition-3 check was verifying). So in practice the equality `EVAL_LINES == NUM_EPOCHS` is the structural invariant; the inequality `EVAL_LINES > NUM_EPOCHS` would indicate a bug-introduction (an extra eval call elsewhere in the training loop), and the EXP-001 diff does not add any new `evaluator.evaluate(...)` call site, so this check is a defense-in-depth audit, not an expected discriminator. The strict-equality form is the safe interpretation of "exactly one eval per epoch" and matches the EXP-000 plan-000 § Verification Protocol § Condition 3's phrasing "This count should equal the `num_epochs` value in the summary. If it exceeds `num_epochs`, the condition is violated." The "exceeds" wording in the goal-file and the prior plan matches the asymmetric "no more than" hard-constraint reading. The plan-001's check uses the equality form because the structural property guarantees the lower-bound side and the asymmetric upper-bound is the actual hard constraint.

The verification commands' upstream-failure handling: if the `run.log` file does not exist at all (the redirect-target was never created because the process didn't launch), all three commands' `grep` invocations return empty stdout and exit code 1, and the downstream `awk | tr | bc` chains propagate the empty values to the comparisons which all fail; the analyze phase's interpretation of "all three conditions FAIL with empty extraction" is a `crash` verdict at the launch-failure tier, which goes into the `infra-errors.md` log at the Critical severity tier per the standard autoresearch convention. The standard mitigation is to confirm the launch command produced the file by checking `ls -la /SPXvePFS/users/david/autoresearch-cifar10/run.log` after the harness's bash-tool's exit-code-reporting indicates the launch returned, and to retry the launch if the file is missing (a one-shot transient failure goes in the Warning tier of `infra-errors.md`; a repeating failure goes Critical).

The per-step LR-schedule trace check from the abort-criteria last bullet (the wall-clock-fractional schedule's two LR drops at the 50% and 75% wall-clock-fraction marks) is a *post-hoc consistency check* in the verification protocol's narrative-section rather than a pass/fail condition in the necessary-conditions list. The check is: grep the log for the literal LR-readout substrings `lr: 0.1000`, `lr: 0.0100`, and `lr: 0.0010` (the f4-precision format from `train.py:197`'s `lr: {lr:.4f}` field), confirm that all three substrings appear in the log, and confirm that the order in which they first appear is the expected `0.1000 → 0.0100 → 0.0010` cascade with the transitions occurring near the lines where the `pct_done:` field (also on line 197, the literal `pct_done = 100 * total_training_time / TIME_BUDGET_S` value from `train.py:191`) crosses 50.0 and 75.0 respectively. This consistency check is recorded in the experiment-log's `## Run Log § Run 1 § Observations` section so the analyze phase can confirm the schedule's correctness independent of the metric outcome — a `no-improvement` verdict combined with a confirmed-correct LR-schedule transition trace is "the capacity hypothesis is wrong" (the next-loop direction is to explore the regularization or throughput axis), whereas a `no-improvement` verdict combined with a *missing* LR transition is "the schedule has a bug" (the next-loop direction is to fix the bug). Surfacing this distinction in the report is the EXP-001-specific protocol-correctness contribution that the goal-learnings memory's "the schedule must engage the polish phase in the final ~15% of wall-clock" pattern entry effectively requests.

The full process wall-clock from process-start to summary-block-print, and the pre-training-loop initialization time, respectively. The relation `total_seconds = startup_seconds + training_seconds + sum-of-per-epoch-eval-times + summary-block-print-time` holds approximately (the per-epoch eval time is the unaccounted-for difference between `total_seconds - startup_seconds - training_seconds`, on the order of the per-epoch eval cost times `num_epochs`). For the analyze phase's narrative, the reading confirms the experiment's total wall-clock fits inside the 10-minute hard-kill ceiling and gives a per-epoch-eval-time estimate from the residual arithmetic — useful for the throughput discussion but not part of any pass/fail check.
