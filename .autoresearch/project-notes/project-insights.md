### High Importance

- **Compute envelope is huge: H20 98 GB, baseline/EXP-001 use ~0.3–1.6 GB — memory & throughput are free levers** (EXP-001)
  Evidence: goals/maximize-cifar10-test-accuracy/experiments/001/04-analysis.md § Results — peak_vram_mb 1592 at batch 512, 6.5M-param net.
  Implication: any future goal can widen/deepen models, grow batch, and use bf16+channels_last freely; VRAM is not a binding constraint on this dataset/GPU.

### High Importance

- **At a fixed WALL-TIME training budget, ANY per-step-cost increase (more PARAMS or more COMPUTE) trades directly against the optimizer-update count, and on a net whose accuracy concentrates in the low-LR anneal tail that update loss usually dominates the technique's benefit — so literature gains quoted at MATCHED epochs do not transfer** (EXP-005, EXP-007 capacity; EXP-013 SAM compute)
  Evidence: three datapoints, same mechanism. EXP-005 (deepen 4×4) cut 142→131 ep and lost; EXP-007 (widen layer2 256→384) cut 150→94 ep, best==final still-climbing, −0.15pp; EXP-013 (tail-only SAM, 2× fwd-bwd in the low-LR tail) cut 150→124/132 ep and lost (96.29/96.18 vs same-session 96.47) — the lighter SAM cell fully ANNEALED and still lost, isolating epoch-cost from under-anneal. SAM's published +0.3–1.0pp CIFAR gains are all at matched epochs / heavier backbones (experiments/013/04-analysis.md).
  Implication: on any fixed-time training goal, score a candidate technique by its expected accuracy gain MINUS the accuracy lost to the epochs its per-step cost removes — not by its matched-epoch literature number. Read `num_epochs` as the first-class diagnostic on EVERY cost-increasing change (capacity, SAM, multi-pass losses, heavier aug-on-GPU). Prefer throughput-FREE levers; if spending steps, keep the loss small (mild capacity, partial-schedule application) and pre-register num_epochs. A technique only earns a re-test in a near-free variant (e.g. ESAM sparse perturbation) — and even then EXP-013 showed zero positive SAM signal where it ran.

- **When a net saturates well before a fixed TIME budget (large epoch surplus), throughput-FREE regularization converts the surplus into accuracy WITHOUT the under-anneal risk that sinks capacity adds — BUT the SAME-class lever has diminishing returns: the FIRST strong regularizer wins big, a SECOND ties; and the WHOLE input-augmentation lane saturates across its 3 mechanisms** (EXP-008 win; EXP-011/EXP-015 diminishing; contrast EXP-005/007)
  Evidence: goals/maximize-cifar10-test-accuracy/experiments/008/04-analysis.md § Results — the net fit ~142–150 epochs for ~96% vs airbench96's 37 (≈4× surplus); stronger CPU-worker augmentation (cutout 8→12 + light RandomErasing) gave +0.38pp (96.00→96.38), num_epochs unchanged. BUT EXP-011 (experiments/011/04-analysis.md): a SECOND, mechanistically-distinct input-space aug (CutMix, MIXING) ADDED on top only matched baseline (96.40 vs 96.38, +0.02pp), throughput-free and fully annealed. EXP-015 (experiments/015/04-analysis.md): the THIRD aug mechanism — transform/policy RandAugment(1,6) — tied as BOTH a replacement (96.34) and an addition (96.36 = same-session c0 96.36) at a matched 149ep, with ep25 (92.04/92.19 vs 92.27) and full anneal ruling out under-fit. EXP-012 extended saturation to the regularization-ALLOCATION axis (wd-shaping tied 96.29 vs 96.32; clean LS 0.1 DEGRADED −0.23pp).
  Implication: on any fixed-wall-time training goal, first ask whether the metric is epoch-bound or saturated; if saturated, prefer ZERO-throughput-cost levers over capacity that costs steps — but throughput-free is NECESSARY not SUFFICIENT: once a net is regularization-bound near its ceiling, the throughput-free regularization sub-levers saturate one after another within the ~0.1pp noise floor. The input-augmentation LANE specifically is now exhausted across ALL THREE mechanisms here (occlusion EXP-008 / mixing EXP-011 / transform EXP-015) — after one class wins, treat ALL further input-aug AND same-axis regularization tweaks (2nd/3rd aug, wd reallocation, LS retune) as likely-redundant; diversify to a genuinely different MECHANISM (architecture/representation, schedule shape) and verify against a SAME-SESSION baseline (stored number too weak at the noise floor). Verify "throughput-free" empirically (budget excludes DataLoader-wait; a worker bottleneck inflates WALL time, not epoch count — confirmed EXP-015).

- **A time-budgeted metric can be GENERALIZATION-CEILING-bound rather than epoch/throughput-bound — directly test by BUYING epochs (torch.compile) before assuming "more compute → more accuracy"** (EXP-014, settling EXP-005/007/013; ceiling reinforced by EXP-016/017 BN-noise + EXP-018 anti-aliasing nulls)
  Evidence: experiments/014/04-analysis.md § Results — torch.compile (off-budget warmup) bought a clean, math-equivalent +12% throughput (154→173 epochs) on the whitened ResNet-9, yet the extra 19 anneal epochs added only +0.03pp, and using the same surplus to fund a +1.03M capacity widen (layer2 256→320, annealed at a HEALTHY 143 ep) LOST −0.08pp. Two arms, one verdict: neither more optimizer-update epochs nor more annealed capacity moves the metric → a genuine generalization ceiling at ~96.3–96.5, NOT an epoch/throughput limit. This DISCONFIRMS the prior framing (above bullet + the per-step-cost bullet) that treated the epoch budget as THE binding constraint: those experiments under-annealed, but EXP-014 gave them the epochs and they STILL didn't pay off.
  Implication: when a fixed-time net has plateaued across many no-improvements, do NOT keep spending loops on throughput/epoch levers (compile, faster kernels, sync-reduction, mild capacity) hoping accuracy follows — first run the cheap discriminating test: BUY epochs (compile is ~free and math-equivalent) and a matched +epochs control; if accuracy is flat, the limiter is the architecture's generalization ceiling, and the high-EV move is a DIFFERENT base architecture, not another within-architecture lever. Bank compile as free headroom to fund that new architecture's per-step cost. (9 straight no-improvements EXP-006→015 now make the ceiling diagnosis strong; EXP-015 added a 3rd, mechanistically-distinct augmentation class — transform/policy RandAugment — which also tied, with ep25/anneal ruling out under-fit, further closing the input-aug lane.) UPDATE (EXP-016→018, now 13 straight nulls): even axes OUTSIDE regularization tied — BN/activation-statistic noise (GhostBN/NoisyBN, EXP-016/017) and the FIRST architectural-INDUCTIVE-BIAS lever, anti-aliased downsampling (BlurPool, EXP-018, throughput-free, lost −0.08/−0.15pp monotonically with blur strength). Strong RandomCrop+flip aug appears to already supply the translation invariance BlurPool would add. The ceiling is robust to within-DavidNet structural tweaks; remaining untried: channel attention (SE), readout pooling, or a wholesale different backbone. UPDATE (EXP-019, now 14 straight nulls): the FIRST channel-attention lever — Squeeze-Excitation (identity-init, throughput-neutral, layer2+3 r=16) — also TIED: a session-1 +0.28pp same-session lead did NOT replicate (+0.02pp on a confirmation pair) and never cleared 96.48; the session-1 c0 (96.11) was a confirmed low host draw. Adding a genuinely-new functional form (content-adaptive per-channel recalibration) is redundant on this saturated net. The ceiling now resists capacity, optimizer, regularization, BN-noise, downsampling AND channel-attention — every within-DavidNet lever. The remaining high-EV move is a wholesale DIFFERENT backbone (throughput-free within-architecture probes like schedule-shape/readout are most-likely-to-tie); banked torch.compile +12% funds the new backbone's per-step cost. UPDATE (EXP-020, now 15 straight nulls): the predicted "most-likely-to-tie" throughput-free probe — LR-schedule SHAPE (cosine one-cycle vs linear triangular) — DID tie exactly as forecast, despite cosine carrying both an internal flag (EXP-012) and external evidence (MosaicML ~0.5% cosine>cyclic on CIFAR). Same low-control-draw artifact AGAIN: session-1 cos +0.32pp (c0 drew 96.04) collapsed to +0.04pp on a normal-draw confirmation (c0b 96.35), never clearing 96.48. The schedule-shape axis is now closed alongside every other within-DavidNet lever. The within-architecture search space is effectively exhausted; the ONLY remaining high-EV direction is a structurally different backbone. This CLOSES the EXP-014 ceiling-diagnosis loophole: EXP-014 showed more epochs/WIDTH don't help; EXP-021 shows more DEPTH at the proven stage, properly annealed, also doesn't → the net is at a genuine generalization ceiling across BOTH capacity dimensions, not anneal-limited. ### Medium Importance

- **Time-budgeted training has a ~0.1pp metric noise floor: a host-throughput-dependent step count fits in the fixed budget, so the trained model varies run-to-run even with the seed fixed** (EXP-006, EXP-004, EXP-005)
  Evidence: byte-identical training code fit 142/131/150 epochs across runs as the shared host (GPU 0 busy) varied; EXP-006's eval-only improvement still landed −0.07pp below its own baseline. goals/maximize-cifar10-test-accuracy/experiments/006/04-analysis.md § Results.
  Implication: on any fixed-wall-time goal on this harness, a single-run improvement bar near ~0.1pp sits at the noise floor — prefer interventions with clearly larger expected effect, and be skeptical of sub-noise single-run wins. Seed re-rolling is disallowed, so the noise can't be averaged out cheaply.
- **The train.py/prepare.py harness budgets TRAINING-loop COMPUTE time only (eval + startup + DataLoader-wait excluded); the 10-min wall cap is separate** (EXP-001, EXP-015)
  Evidence: goals/maximize-cifar10-test-accuracy/experiments/001/04-analysis.md § Execution — training_seconds=300.0 vs total 447.4s. EXP-015 (experiments/015/04-analysis.md): a CPU-bound RandAugment loader (20.6k img/s < 26k GPU rate) inflated WALL (450→514s) but left num_epochs at 149 — the `for ... in train_loader` wait precedes the per-step `t0`, so loader cost is OFF the compute budget.
  Implication: for any time-budgeted goal on this harness, optimize throughput to fit more training, key schedules to elapsed training time, and watch eval cadence against the wall cap. COROLLARY (EXP-015): a CPU-side data-pipeline change (augmentation policies, heavier transforms) is "epoch-free" but "wall-bounded" — it does NOT under-anneal like a GPU per-step cost; pre-smoke loader img/s vs the GPU compute rate, and gate only on `total_seconds` < the wall cap. Workers (NUM_WORKERS=8) are frozen in prepare.py.
- **Identity-init a residual branch via ReZero (learnable α=0), NOT by zeroing the final BN γ, when the block ends in ReLU** (EXP-004)
  Evidence: goals/maximize-cifar10-test-accuracy/experiments/004/04-analysis.md § Approach — cross-model plan review caught that conv_bn's post-BN ReLU makes ReLU'(0)=0, so a zeroed final-BN-γ block gets no gradient and stays dead/identity forever; ReZero (`x+α·branch`) kept α.grad=0.0179≠0.
  Implication: any future goal that adds residual/identity-init blocks to a Conv-BN-ReLU net must use a gate with a live gradient path (ReZero/LayerScale) and verify trainability with a one-step backward smoke before the official run; gradual gate ramp also removes the need for an LR retune.

- **FLOP-equal ≠ wall-clock-equal: a small-spatial/large-channel conv (4×4/512) runs ~10% SLOWER than a large-spatial/small-channel one (8×8/256) on this GPU** (EXP-005)
  Evidence: goals/maximize-cifar10-test-accuracy/experiments/005/04-analysis.md § Results — adding a 4×4/512 ReZero block dropped throughput to 23.4k img/s (vs EXP-004's 8×8/256 block at 26.1k), costing 11 epochs under the fixed time budget.
  Implication: on any time-budgeted goal, do NOT assume equal-FLOP architecture changes are equal-cost — cuDNN kernel efficiency varies sharply with the spatial/channel shape; measure throughput empirically and prefer adding capacity at larger-spatial stages where kernels run at full rate.

- **A peak LR proven on a SHORT schedule does not transfer to a LONG schedule — the high-LR plateau duration, not the peak value, sets stability** (EXP-009)
  Evidence: goals/maximize-cifar10-test-accuracy/experiments/009/04-analysis.md § Results — Muon at airbench's peak LR 0.24 (proven over 8 epochs, no warmup) diverged to ~random under our 150-epoch triangular one-cycle (ep25-100 ~10-20%), recovering only as LR→0; the optimizer/implementation were correct (clean late recovery to 94.11).
  Implication: when porting an optimizer/LR from a reference recipe, match the SCHEDULE SHAPE (warmup + plateau length), not just the peak number. A scale-fixed optimizer (Muon: NS normalizes update magnitude, so per-step rotation ≈ lr·‖update‖/‖p‖ is constant at peak) is especially sensitive — a peak safe for a few epochs is too hot when held for many. Budget a LR-retune loop after any optimizer swap; read the early-epoch trajectory (divergence vs slow-convergence) to set the retune direction.

- **A record-setting reference optimizer (Muon), once correctly LR-tuned, only MATCHED well-tuned SGD-Nesterov here — an optimizer swap is not a free win; diagnose whether the metric is optimizer-bound before spending on one** (EXP-009, EXP-010)
  Evidence: goals/maximize-cifar10-test-accuracy/experiments/010/04-analysis.md § Results — tuned Muon (lr 0.03) reached 96.33 vs SGD 96.38 (tie within the ~0.1pp noise floor) on the whitened ResNet-9 at the 300s budget; the monotonic LR ranking and full anneal rule out under-tuning/under-anneal.
  Implication: on any goal, treat an optimizer swap as a fix for a SPECIFIC pathology (instability, poor conditioning), not as a generic accuracy lever — when well-tuned SGD already trains stably to convergence, a fancier optimizer tends to land at the same minimum. Confirm the limiter is the optimizer (e.g. train loss not minimized, gradients ill-conditioned) before investing; here the limiter was regularization, so the productive levers were augmentation/eval-side, not the optimizer.
