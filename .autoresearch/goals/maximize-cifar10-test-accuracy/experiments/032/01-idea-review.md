# Idea Review EXP-032

## Prioritized Feedback

1. **Reflection padding is the strongest candidate, but its causal evidence is presently only mechanistic.** The current `RandomCrop(32, padding=4)` in `train.py` does inject zero-valued borders before normalization, while `02-system-understanding.md` identifies test-boundary quality rather than compute or I/O as the limiter. That makes the treatment relevant and distinct from the closed residual-masking family, but neither the cited RandAugment note nor a prior local result establishes that reflection improves CIFAR-10 here. Frame it as a clean exploratory input-geometry test, not a known correction. Precommit the exact one-line `padding_mode="reflect"` diff and close reflection geometry after one valid miss; do not follow with symmetric/replicate padding or a new padding width.

2. **The reflection verifier must prove decision-stream preservation without requiring pixel equality.** `RandomCrop` pads before drawing crop coordinates, and padding itself should consume no RNG, so crop offsets, flip decisions, sampler order, and private RandAugment operation draws can remain aligned even though candidate pixels intentionally differ. Test that directly with an instrumented/tagged crop or separately replayed crop parameters and worker-private RNG states. Require construction and post-iterator CPU RNG equality, identical sampler indices and transition epoch/lag, no RandAugment marker after the exhausted-epoch cutoff, and exact accepted/candidate equality for crops whose sampled window never touches padding. Do not require active-image equality when a crop includes padding, and do not claim the late phase is pixel-identical to accepted: reflection remains active during the hard-label tail.

3. **Reflection's main failure mode is not compute but distorted edge content.** Mirroring can duplicate edge texture or object fragments and remove the strong translation/occlusion regularization supplied by zeros. Report how often the fixed crop stream touches padded pixels and show the intended difference is confined to those pixels before normalization. Since the production transform is worker-side and GPU work is unchanged, a heavyweight step-throughput gate is unnecessary; a short real-loader stability check is sufficient to catch PIL/worker errors and confirm the 65% exhausted-iterator cutoff. Preserve the accepted 130-pass exposure floor and one-score rule.

4. **Batch 512 is an indivisible optimizer/normalization operating point, not the optimization-equivalent inverse of EXP-029.** `experiments/031/proposals/idea-01.md` correctly concedes that doubled LR and roughly halved updates change Nesterov's example-domain horizon, BatchNorm update count/statistics, batch-shared mixup refresh, dropped samples, and worker assignment. Even at its 1.10 image-rate gate it projects only about 14.3k updates versus 25,978 accepted. Remove or qualify “fully scaled”/“counterpart” language: first-order drift and coupled decay are only approximate, and no local result supports the resulting dynamics.

5. **The batch-512 upside is real but attacks compute rather than the demonstrated accuracy limiter.** Ample H20 memory and 98% forward/backward share justify measuring it, yet EXP-009, EXP-016, and EXP-028 show that 159-172 passes can still regress when optimization or representation changes. Retain the strict complete-body `>=1.10x` image-rate and `>=146.308096` projected-pass gates, balanced mixup/hard timing, real-loader/wall checks, final-accuracy corroboration, and no adjacent batch/LR/momentum/warmup rescue. A passing gate earns one exploratory score; it does not establish equivalence or make exposure evidence for accuracy.

6. **Alpha 0.1 has excellent attribution but the weakest empirical prior.** `experiments/031/proposals/idea-03.md` gives an exact one-line treatment and correctly handles alpha-dependent Beta rejection and downstream CUDA permutation divergence. However, alpha 0.4, 50%/75% duration, and per-example coefficients all regressed, while EXP-027 shows no direct over-regularization symptom. The deeper-plus-RandAugment interaction makes alpha 0.1 novel enough for one closure run, but not stronger than the new crop geometry. Preserve batch sharing, 65% cutoff, global seed 42, normal-exposure gate, and the prohibition on adjacent alpha/seed/cutoff repairs.

7. **Keep the goal verdict and one-run closure uniform.** For every scored candidate, success is `best_test_acc >=94.42%`; pre-register `final_test_acc >=94.32%` and `final_test_loss <=0.2523` as stability/boundary corroboration rather than substitutes for the primary metric. A valid normal-exposure miss closes exactly the selected family as stated. Semantic or feasibility failure closes only the exact implementation tested and must not trigger a same-run rescue.

## Scored Verdict

| Candidate | Evidence / reasoning | Potential impact |
|---|---|---|
| Reflection-Padded Random Crops | **3/5** - It directly changes artificial input-boundary statistics at effectively zero counted GPU cost, preserves the accepted optimizer/model, and is distinct from failed feature masks; support is mechanistic rather than local empirical evidence. | **3/5** - A better crop prior could move enough boundary decisions to clear the 0.10-point margin without sacrificing 133-pass exposure, though duplicated edge content could just as plausibly hurt. |
| Batch 512 With a Fully Scaled LR Curve | **2/5** - H20 headroom and convolution dominance justify the systems hypothesis, but the doubled-LR, halved-decision learner has no local positive evidence and is not optimization-equivalent. | **3.5/5** - If it clears the 1.10x gate and larger-batch dynamics remain useful, 146+ passes offer the largest ceiling; that upside is inseparable from coarser SGD/BN/mixup decisions. |
| Weaker Alpha-0.1 Batch-Shared Mixup | **2/5** - The one-line intervention and distribution reasoning are unusually clean, but every neighboring mixup perturbation is negative and accepted EXP027 shows no excess-regularization signal. | **2/5** - Endpoint-heavy batches might improve a handful of boundary decisions for free, but the likely effect is small and under-regularization can erase the accepted gain. |

## Pick

**Reflection-Padded Random Crops.** It wins because it is the only candidate that targets a concrete generalization/boundary mechanism while preserving the accepted model, full-gradient update count, LR trajectory, batch statistics, mixup decisions, and measured exposure regime. Batch 512 has a higher ceiling but changes too many optimization dynamics for its hardware evidence to predict accuracy, while alpha 0.1 is a closure experiment in a uniformly negative local neighborhood. Advance reflection only with decision-stream/cutoff verification, a real-loader stability check, the 130-pass floor, and a precommitted one-run closure of reflection padding geometry.
