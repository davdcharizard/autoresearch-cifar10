# Plan Review: EXP-046

1. **High - Milestone 4 / Verification step 12 conflates exposure support with run validity.** The plan says a valid completion must have at least 127 realized passes and that "Completion/resource passes require >=127." The chosen proposal explicitly says a structurally valid low-exposure completion still consumes the sole score and may not be rerun; it merely fails the protected-exposure success hypothesis. As written, an executor could label a complete `<127`-pass score invalid and create an apparent basis for another run. Separate exit/resource/integrity validity from the two-part success rule (`best_test_acc >=94.58%` and `passes >=127`) and require any valid low-exposure result to be recorded once as non-success.

2. **High - Verification step 10 does not define an unbiased H20 timing comparison or the exposure formula.** "One live arm at a time" permits all accepted windows to precede all candidate windows, so clock/thermal drift can decide a narrow gate. "Median weighted projection" also leaves open whether early/hard times are arithmetically averaged or converted to fixed-time step rates; those calculations are not equivalent. Predeclare a locally counterbalanced accepted/candidate order, state restoration for every retained window, raw-window emission, and the exact fixed-time retention equation before execution. Otherwise the executor has discretion to choose a favorable ordering or aggregation after seeing timings.

3. **High - Verification steps 8-9 time a heavily instrumented loader rather than the production loader.** Returning index, worker id, crop/flip decisions, active state, RandAugment decision/private-state hashes, and the tensor changes worker computation, serialization, queue payload, and main-process consumption. With a fixed 11 ms consumer delay, this extra work can either create stalls absent in production or mask a small fill-specific difference under the delay, making the projected wall delta unsupported. Decision/RNG tracing needs an independently qualified non-consuming semantic run; the wall gate must use production-equivalent payload and transform execution, or explicitly measure and bound instrumentation distortion.

4. **Medium - Verification steps 6 and 8-9 never exercise the real active-to-inactive transition on one persistent worker pool.** Step 6 tests isolated calls, while each timing occurrence creates a fresh loader already assigned to one phase. Production keeps the same workers and their accumulated crop/flip and private RandAugment states, then flips the shared flag only after an exhausted iterator, with prefetched work being the central temporal hazard. Fresh inactive pools cannot prove the claimed transition semantics, absence of leaked active samples, or tail delivery behavior. Add a same-pool exhausted-active-epoch to inactive-epoch semantic replay for accepted and candidate; phase-isolated pools may remain timing fixtures but cannot substitute for that control.

5. **Medium - Verification step 4's pixel oracle shares the implementation primitive under test.** The plan calls `ImageOps.expand`/crop/flip an independent oracle even though the candidate's claimed semantics specifically depend on torchvision reaching PIL `ImageOps.expand`. A fill coercion, channel-order, or mask error in that primitive can agree with itself. Construct the expected padded RGB array, crop, flip, and padding-derived mask independently of PIL/torchvision, force all 81 offsets and both flip states through the installed production transform operations, and compare those outputs to the independent bytes.

6. **Low - Execution Environment deletes `run.log` "after the loop" before analysis ownership is defined.** The score log is the primary evidence for the summary, transitions, cadence, timing, and error audit. Removing it immediately after execution can make the later analysis unverifiable; retain it through report/verification completion and remove it only before the next experiment, as required by the goal.
