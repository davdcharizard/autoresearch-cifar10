# Adversarial Review - EXP-028 Candidate Ideas

## Prioritized Feedback

1. **The late-freeze accuracy premise is unverified, and the cited paper does not establish it.** `knowledge/papers/time-matters-regularization.md` supports removing regularization after an early critical period; it does not show that early convolution gradients become dispensable. Here the cutoff also removes mixup and RandAugment, so the clean-input distribution is exactly where frozen low-level filters may need to adapt. Keep this as an explicitly high-risk temporal-allocation test, and preregister final accuracy/loss as mechanism corroboration rather than claiming the literature predicts success.
2. **Freeze speed estimates cannot be inferred from forward-stage shares.** `02-system-understanding.md` measures stage 1 at 39.6% of forward and total backward at about 74%, but does not attribute backward by stage. The proposal's balanced hard-tail timing gate correctly resolves this; the plan must treat the analytic 1.44x estimate as non-evidence and fail closed at the fixed 1.20x / 145-pass gates.
3. **More legal evaluations can inflate the freeze candidate's best-only metric.** At least 145 passes create more every-fifth-epoch looks than EXP-027's 133.01 passes. This is allowed by `01-definition.md`, but a marginal best spike would be weak evidence of better boundary quality. Preserve the formal best-accuracy rule while requiring final accuracy at least the 94.32 baseline as preregistered corroboration and reporting the best-final gap.
4. **The freeze intervention preserves representation but not accepted tail optimization.** Frozen prefix parameters stop momentum and weight-decay updates, while their BN statistics continue moving; `experiments/007/04-analysis.md` shows broad late decay removal harmed generalization, and `experiments/016/04-analysis.md` shows high-resolution representation quality is essential. Neither result makes the idea a retry, but both are concrete negative priors. The semantic checks must prove frozen values and momentum buffers remain bitwise fixed while all stage-1 BN buffers stay live, exactly as proposed.
5. **Batch 128 is an indivisible optimizer-regime experiment, not a clean granularity test.** Halving batch and the entire LR curve also halves the momentum horizon in examples, doubles BN-stat and batch-shared mixup refreshes, and weakens the terminal floor that `experiments/008/04-analysis.md` identifies as useful. The proposal acknowledges these confounds; do not interpret either result as batch size alone, and do not rescue a miss with the accepted 0.002 floor.
6. **Batch 128 has lineage but little direct local evidence on the accepted model.** `experiments/001/04-analysis.md` introduced batch 256/LR 0.2 inside a large architecture-and-schedule bundle, so it is not evidence that reversing those two constants improves the `(2,2,3)` plus RandAugment model. Its value is that `02-system-understanding.md` names this operating point as genuinely untested. Retain the strict image-exposure and update-count gates so a throughput collapse cannot masquerade as an optimizer result.
7. **The drop-path mechanism is mostly post hoc and faces direct contrary evidence.** `experiments/011/04-analysis.md` reports high loss for the extra block, but does not identify a brittle shortcut; `experiments/027/04-analysis.md` already shows RandAugment repairs much of that loss. Adding a third early regularizer repeats the failure direction in `experiments/006/04-analysis.md`, which lost 0.55 points at normal exposure. Targeting one block, halving probability, and using per-example branch masks make it materially distinct, not well supported.
8. **Drop-path may be too weak to clear the fixed margin even if harmless.** Masking one of seven branches on 5% of early examples has a small effective dose, while its private seed adds a new stochastic trajectory and its full branch computation buys no exposure. Keep the fixed seed and no-reroll rule, but classify a normal-exposure miss as closure of this targeted masking family rather than tuning nearby probabilities.

| Candidate | Evidence / reasoning | Potential impact |

## Pick

**Late Stem and Stage-1 Freeze.** It wins because it is the only candidate that converts the measured dominant cost into a preregistered, temporally isolated change while preserving the entire accepted first 65% trajectory and full inference representation. Batch 128 is a reasonable runner-up, but it perturbs the optimizer, BN, mixup, and LR floor for the whole run and has no direct local signal. Advance the freeze only with the exact semantic oracle, fixed 1.20x tail-speed and 145-pass gates, and final-accuracy corroboration; otherwise fail it before scoring.
