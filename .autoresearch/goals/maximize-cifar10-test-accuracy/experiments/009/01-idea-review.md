# EXP-009 Blind Idea Review

## Prioritized Feedback

1. **BF16 is the strongest candidate, but the proposal over-attributes a possible gain to exposure.** `proposals/idea-01.md` has unusually strong local feasibility evidence: repeated H20 measurements put BF16 batch 256 at 8.70-8.71 ms versus 9.98-9.99 ms for FP32, and its implementation preserves FP32 parameters, gradients, optimizer state, mixup interpolation, and evaluation. However, the history does not establish that 14.7% more updates will improve accuracy. EXP-008 shows that retaining a nonzero late LR matters; it does not show that increasing the number of updates at every wall-time LR is beneficial. Autocast also changes convolution numerics from the first update, and faster execution maps each batch to an earlier, generally higher LR. Refine the hypothesis and later attribution to the combined deployed treatment: BF16 numerical training plus denser updates on the time schedule. Record realized passes, final loss, and final accuracy, but do not claim a gain proves exposure alone was limiting.

2. **The BF16 preflight does not exactly measure the production timed region.** In `train.py:211-249`, the counted timer starts before nonblocking host-to-device copies and ends after CUDA synchronization. The proposal-development benchmark and proposed preflight use CUDA-resident synthetic inputs, so both omit the copy cost and any copy/compute interaction. The ratio is still useful and the 1.147x observation has margin over the 1.10 gate, but the gate can overpredict full-loop exposure. Improve the fresh preflight by timing pinned-host-to-device copies inside both matched paths, or explicitly downgrade the gate to kernel feasibility and require the scored run's realized exposure for the mechanism verdict. Preserve balanced ordering, report all window times/CV, and keep the aggregate benchmark independent of evaluator data. This is not fatal because the scored run remains valid under the fixed budget.

3. **Faster BF16 creates more legal `best_test_acc` observation opportunities.** Evaluation is every five epochs in `train.py:273-282`; the projected 162.8 passes would complete more epochs and therefore several more test evaluations than EXP-002. This follows the frozen cadence and does not violate the once-per-epoch constraint, so it is not a disqualifying metric hack. It can nevertheless make a marginal best-only gain partly checkpoint-selection luck. Preregister that 94.17% is still the formal decision rule, while reporting the best/final gap and treating a one-checkpoint spike with worse final accuracy/loss as weak mechanistic evidence. Do not change cadence inside EXP-009, because that would add another treatment.

4. **Selective final-stage widening is compute-aware but has no direct evidence that the final stage is the capacity bottleneck.** `proposals/idea-02.md` correctly distinguishes `[32,64,160]` from the rejected uniform width-3 model and gives auditable parameter/MAC counts. The WRN evidence supports width in general, not this asymmetric allocation; the unchanged 64-channel stage can bottleneck information before the abrupt 64-to-160 expansion. Its affine timing estimate is also only a rough interpolation across very different kernel shapes, though the matched preflight appropriately makes it non-authoritative. Keep the strict 85%/120-pass gate and unchanged optimizer if this is tried later, but interpret a negative result as rejection of this allocation only. The candidate has a higher architectural ceiling than BF16 but weaker evidence and pays about 10-15% exposure even if its gate passes.

5. **`N=1, M=5` RandAugment is not uniformly “mild,” and it stacks with a repeatedly fragile regularization recipe.** `proposals/idea-03.md` acknowledges magnitude-independent operations but understates their consequence: lowering `M` does not weaken operations such as identity-independent policy choices, and `N=1` applies one sampled operation to every image. The policy also remains active during the 35% hard-label tail, whereas EXP-003/005/006 already show that target, spatial, and feature regularization can regress at normal exposure. The transform is materially different from those failures and is constraint-compliant, but the exact policy has weak local support. Before advancing it, verify the installed torchvision operation space and empirical operation frequencies without test data; either accept the standard policy as a one-shot exploratory bet or define a genuinely bounded transform set in a new proposal. Do not call the current treatment uniformly low strength or retry stronger settings after a negative result.

6. **The RandAugment wall-time model is operationally sensible but not a precise projection.** The loader yields before `t0` in `train.py`, so worker stalls are excluded from `training_seconds` but included in the 600-second external wall limit. Prefetch makes per-yield latency nonlinear: added transform time is mostly hidden until workers fall behind the GPU consumer. The proposed real-loader, consumer-paced benchmark and conservative 500-second gate are suitable feasibility controls, but record complete-cycle wall throughput rather than relying only on `ra_ms_per_batch - base_ms_per_batch`. This prevents a misleading near-zero per-yield result from a prefilled queue.

7. **No candidate has a fatal scope or hard-constraint violation.** All three retain seed 42, use one local H20 and existing dependencies, leave `prepare.py`/`Eval` untouched, keep at most one evaluation per epoch, and preregister the required 94.17% threshold with no reroll. The proposed preflights inspect no evaluator metric and gate only operational feasibility, so they do not select on test accuracy. Confirm the CIFAR files already exist locally because `download=True` remains in the accepted code and this session forbids network use.

| Isolated BF16 autocast, batch 256 | **8.5/10** - repeated local H20 timing, semantic dtype checks, a narrow code change, and clean rejection of batch-512 confounds provide strong support; the missing production-copy timing and unproven accuracy-from-exposure step prevent a higher score. | **7.5/10** - roughly 15% more counted updates gives credible room to clear +0.10, but the baseline is mature and BF16 rounding or excess update density can erase the gain. |

## Pick

**Advance isolated BF16 autocast at batch 256.** It wins because it is the only finalist aimed directly at the diagnosed counted-compute/exposure limiter with measured local H20 upside, while preserving the accepted architecture, batch size, optimizer, regularization, seed, and FP32 evaluator. Selective widening has a somewhat larger theoretical ceiling but weaker bottleneck evidence and necessarily sacrifices exposure; RandAugment has the weakest fit to six consecutive negative regularization/schedule results. Harden the BF16 preflight around the actual timed copy-plus-step path, and phrase the experiment as a joint numerical/throughput treatment whose formal success still requires `best_test_acc >= 94.17%` in the single fixed-seed run.
