# EXP-015 Adversarial Idea Review

## Prioritized Feedback

1. **Per-Example Mixup Strengths has the cleanest local premise, but its key causal assumption remains unverified.** EXP-002 establishes alpha-0.2 mixup through 65% as useful, and EXP-003/005/006 show that changing its semantics or adding regularization is harmful (`04-results.tsv`). This candidate uniquely preserves each example's `Beta(0.2, 0.2)` marginal and the hard-label tail while changing only within-batch coefficient correlation. The hidden assumption is that batch-shared coefficient noise is harmful rather than useful SGD noise; the proposal already names this failure mode, and no evaluator-free preflight can resolve it. Keep the treatment standalone as specified, report realized exposure, and interpret a miss as rejection of decorrelation rather than evidence for stronger mixup. The predicted 94.15-94.30 range is more precise than the evidence supports and should be treated as directional, not calibrated.

2. **Mixup-Window Gradient Centralization projects away directions without establishing that they are nuisance directions.** The proposal itself concedes that the all-ones coefficient direction is not a symmetry. This is especially serious for `fc.weight`: the classifier consumes ReLU-activated, globally pooled features in `train.py`, so each row's mean direction can encode useful evidence and has no downstream BatchNorm invariance. The same concern applies to the unnormalized stem and projection shortcuts. The claimed link from common-mode filter gradients to the diagnosed top-1 generalization boundary is therefore weaker than the exact implementation makes it sound. A better-founded future variant would derive the eligible layer set from an actual invariance or measure projected-gradient fractions first; as written, the mandated all-17-tensor projection must be judged as one arbitrary, overconstraining treatment and must not be repaired after seeing its result.

3. **Ten-Percent Early-Window SAM has the largest conceptual ceiling but an under-supported operating point and a fixed-budget confound.** It directly targets solution sharpness, which is genuinely distinct from the failed capacity, EMA, initialization, and exposure changes. However, neither the repository knowledge nor prior experiments validate SAM for this model, `rho=0.05`, or a 10% window. More importantly, the proposal deliberately spends about 5% of total data exposure and removes early high-LR optimizer updates; EXP-009 shows that exposure is not sufficient, but it does not show those early updates are expendable. The strict throughput and state-restoration gates are appropriate. If pursued later, first use a training-only perturbation diagnostic to show that `rho=0.05` produces a finite, nondegenerate loss increase; do not tune it against evaluator accuracy. As EXP-015's first choice, this is too weakly calibrated relative to its confounds.

4. **The SAM BatchNorm protocol is careful but still tests a compound training rule.** Restoring post-first-forward running buffers after the perturbed pass preserves one persistent update, yet the optimizer gradient still comes from batch statistics recomputed at perturbed weights. That is legitimate SAM behavior, not an implementation error, but it means the result jointly tests sharpness-aware gradients, altered early update density, and this BN interaction. The analysis must not attribute a failure solely to sharpness or generalization. The proposed exception-safe restoration and exact one-step checks are necessary hard gates because the accepted WRN is BatchNorm-heavy.

5. **All three candidates obey the stated scope, but only the per-example candidate has a high-quality single-variable attribution.** Each keeps changes in `train.py`, fixed seed 42, one H20, the 300-second counted budget, and at-most-once-per-epoch evaluation. Gradient centralization changes 17 gradient subspaces and their momentum history; SAM changes objective geometry and update/exposure density. Per-example mixup changes one explicit correlation structure with negligible expected compute, making either a positive or negative result more reusable.

## Scored Verdict

- **Per-Example Mixup Strengths**
  - **Strength of evidence and reasoning: 8.5/10.** It builds directly on EXP-002 and explicitly distinguishes itself from the normal-exposure regressions in EXP-003 through EXP-006 while preserving the accepted marginal coefficient law, alpha, timing, and tail.
  - **Potential impact: 6.5/10.** The effect is likely modest because the expected per-example objective is unchanged, but decorrelating 256 coefficients on every mixed update could plausibly move the narrow 0.10-point boundary without sacrificing exposure.

- **Ten-Percent Early-Window SAM**
  - **Strength of evidence and reasoning: 6.0/10.** The sharpness mechanism and state semantics are well reasoned, but the exact radius/window lack local support and the proposal necessarily trades away early updates under the time-based LR schedule.
  - **Potential impact: 8.0/10.** If the operating point is suitable, SAM attacks a qualitatively new solution-geometry mechanism with more ceiling than the incremental candidates, though the 10% window may be too brief to realize it.

- **Mixup-Window Gradient Centralization**
  - **Strength of evidence and reasoning: 5.0/10.** It is operationally precise and orthogonal to prior code changes, but there is no local evidence that per-filter mean gradients are harmful, and centralizing the classifier, stem, and shortcuts is not justified by the normalization argument.
  - **Potential impact: 6.0/10.** A cheap optimizer-geometry change could clear the threshold, but projecting all 17 tensors may discard useful representation and classifier directions while mixup is already regularizing training.

## Strongest Idea

**Per-Example Mixup Strengths** is the single strongest idea. It wins because it has the best combined evidentiary basis and attribution: it refines the one regularizer already proven beneficial, preserves its marginal strength and validated temporal schedule, avoids the failed additive-regularization pattern, and costs essentially no exposure. SAM has a higher upside but too many uncalibrated fixed-budget interactions for the first test; gradient centralization's exact projection is not mechanistically justified for several mandated tensors.
