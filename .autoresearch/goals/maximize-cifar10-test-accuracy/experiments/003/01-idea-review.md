# Adversarial Idea Review — EXP-003

**Baseline:** 94.07% (EXP-002). **Bar:** ≥94.17% (+0.10 pp), single fixed-seed run, 300 s counted budget. **Diagnosis (from `01-brainstorm.md:22-24`):** the model is *generalization-limited* — hard-label train loss falls near zero, final == best, so more low-LR fitting won't move the metric. Under a 300 s budget, **image exposure and optimizer updates are the scarce resource** (EXP-002 got 141.9 passes / 27,735 steps). I judge each idea by mechanism-to-limiter match, exposure cost, and attribution cleanliness.

## Prioritized feedback (most important first)

**1. [WRN-16-3 — the exposure/update tax fights the stated limiter] The scarce resource is spent to buy the resource the diagnosis says isn't binding.**
The proposal's own gate floor is ~80 passes vs EXP-002's 141.9 (`idea-03.md:81-88`), i.e. ~10,417 steps at batch 384 vs 27,735 — roughly **62% fewer optimizer updates on a 2.24× larger model** (1.55M vs 0.69M params, `idea-03.md:36`). A wider net needs *more* optimization to realize its capacity and gets far *less*. And the limiter is generalization, which exposure helps — so you are trading away exposure (helps the limiter) to buy capacity (the diagnosis says is not the primary limiter). The stated 94.2–94.6 range looks optimistic under that throttle.
*Path to improve:* raise the pass floor well above 80 (e.g. ≥110), or take a smaller width step, so the wider net actually gets enough updates to pay for its capacity.

**2. [WRN-16-3 — four confounded knobs] Width, batch 384, LR 0.3, and floor 0.003 change at once (`idea-03.md:35-39`), so a null is uninterpretable — and two of them oppose the goal.** Batch 384 reduces SGD gradient noise, itself a generalization regularizer, potentially cancelling width's benefit; the proposal concedes this (`idea-03.md:110`). *Path:* isolate width at batch 256 first (accept lower H20 utilization) so a positive result is attributable to capacity, not to the batch/LR pairing. *Credit:* this version materially answers the prior fatal objection to WRN-16-3 by **retaining mixup as the regularizer** (`idea-03.md:49-52`) — it is not a forbidden unchanged retry.

**3. [CutMix — it discards the only proven intervention] Replacing mixup (the validated +0.69, `002/04-analysis.md:22`) with a mechanism no local source supports (`idea-02.md:31-33,120-125`) means a null isn't just "no gain" — it's a regression path if CutMix ≤ mixup on 32×32.** The proposal's own estimate is +0.10–0.35 (`idea-02.md:118`), so the **low end sits exactly on the threshold**. *Path to improve:* since CutMix cost is trivial and throughput is preserved, de-risk by keeping mixup live and choosing mixup-vs-CutMix per batch via Bernoulli(0.5) during the early phase — a proven regularizer stays active while the new one is tested (costs some attribution, which the proposal correctly values — call the tradeoff explicitly). At minimum, pre-register that a 94.07–94.16 result routes cleanly back to mixup.

**4. [CutMix — area-as-label-mass is weak on 32×32] A large `Beta(1,1)` patch can cover most of a small object, so pasted-area is a poor proxy for semantic label mass (`idea-02.md:122-125`), injecting label noise.** *Path:* the mandatory area-correction (`idea-02.md:55-64`) plus the unchanged hard-label tail are the right mitigations; also log mean pasted-area fraction (already specified) to detect over-large patches. The in-place-aliasing and zero-area guards (`idea-02.md:52-54,70`) are correctly handled — sound.

**5. [EMA — final-only evaluation forfeits the live eval that produced the baseline's best] This is a genuine regression path, not just "small upside."** In EXP-002 final == best == 94.07 (`002/04-analysis.md:22`), so the *final* live eval was the peak. The proposal replaces exactly that eval with EMA (`idea-01.md:113-121`), making `best_acc = max(regular live evals [≤94.07], one EMA endpoint)`. To clear 94.17 the EMA endpoint must beat the live final by ≥0.10 — but see #6. If it doesn't, and the last regular eval was <94.07, the run lands *below* baseline. *Path:* not cleanly fixable under one-eval-per-epoch; a BN-recalibrated EMA or a raised LR floor (to create genuine iterate variance) would be needed — both out of scope here.

**6. [EMA — the headroom argument is self-defeating, and shortening the horizon doesn't add headroom] The cosine schedule already anneals to 0.002 (`train.py:24`), so late iterates barely move; a low terminal LR is itself implicit averaging.** EXP-001's final-vs-best gap was 0.04 pp — the very variance EMA smooths — so ≥0.10 pp from averaging is implausible. The new 0.99/10-step design (`idea-01.md:56`) reduces *lag* but does nothing to increase the *variance available to average*. *Credit:* it is a real refinement of the rejected EXP-002 EMA (shorter horizon, live non-final evals), not an unchanged retry — but the core headroom problem is untouched.

**7. [All three — thin margins over a noisy bar] No idea estimates seed/step jitter, yet all treat 94.17% as a clean line.** CutMix and WRN-16-3 both have expected low-ends at or near the threshold; EMA sits below it. Only WRN-16-3 has a genuinely high ceiling above the bar, but it's the one most throttled by the budget. Log realized steps/passes so any near-flat result is attributable to mechanism vs throughput.

**Pick: Early-Only CutMix With an Area-Corrected Hard-Label Tail (idea-02).**

It is the only candidate that attacks the diagnosed limiter (generalization) with a mechanism in the *same validated family* as EXP-002 — softened, spatially-composed early regularization followed by a clean hard-label tail — while changing exactly one variable, so a result is cleanly attributable and a null yields a pre-registered next step. It preserves EXP-002's image exposure and single-forward-pass cost, and its ceiling (CutMix's well-established edge over mixup on CIFAR-scale WRNs) comfortably clears the +0.10 bar with margin, unlike EMA. Its one real weakness — no CutMix entry in the local corpus and 32×32 label noise — is bounded by the unchanged recovery tail and mandatory area-correction, and is smaller than idea-03's triple-variable confound plus exposure sacrifice or idea-01's structurally-capped upside. WRN-16-3 is the runner-up and a legitimate higher-structural-ceiling bet now that it's regularized — if chosen instead, strip it to a single width change and scale mixup strength. EMA is the weakest as a *primary* shot (its safety net cannot manufacture a pass) but is the cheapest follow-on to graft onto whichever mechanism wins.

**Refinements to fold into the winner before running:** (1) log mean pasted-area fraction to confirm the regularizer isn't collapsing; (2) keep the `65%→50%` and "milder/shorter CutMix" fallbacks pre-registered so a weak-at-switch curve is interpretable; (3) verify the shared-rectangle-per-batch choice isn't under-regularizing via the throughput/smoke gate, with per-sample rectangles as the cheap escalation.
