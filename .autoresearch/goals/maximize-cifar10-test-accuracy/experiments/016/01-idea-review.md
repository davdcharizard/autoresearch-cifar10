# EXP-016 Adversarial Idea Review

## Prioritized Feedback

1. **Stage-depth redistribution `[1,2,3]` has the strongest local premise, but it assumes the useful part of EXP-011 survives removal of an early block.** EXP-010 and EXP-011 show repeated positive movement from dense stage-3 capacity, while EXP-012 shows that a compressed late path does not reproduce it (`experiments/010/04-analysis.md`, `experiments/011/04-analysis.md`, `experiments/012/04-analysis.md`). That evidence supports the added dense late block, not the claim that the second 32x32 block is expendable. The likely failure is underdeveloped local features before the first downsample, compounded by a 40% parameter increase and the confidence degradation already seen in EXP-011. This is not fatal because the proposal tests a genuinely new allocation at matched MACs. Strengthen interpretation by treating accuracy below baseline with normal throughput as evidence that early refinement is essential, and avoid attributing any gain solely to recovered exposure because block placement and parameter count both change.

2. **Always-on mild RandAugment conflicts with the best local temporal-generalization evidence.** The accepted EXP-002 result gains from removing mixup for a 35% hard-label tail, and the local Time Matters note says early regularization can persist after removal (`experiments/002/04-analysis.md`; `knowledge/papers/time-matters-regularization.md`). Yet this proposal keeps RandAugment active throughout that tail for implementation convenience. Its most likely failure is not CPU cost but interference with clean-label margin refinement, especially after stronger mixup, CutMix, and dropout all worsened accuracy (`03-experiment-learnings.md`). The RandAugment note provides CIFAR-level support but no evidence for this exact `num_ops=1, magnitude=5`, crop/flip-plus-mixup policy (`knowledge/papers/randaugment.md`). The candidate remains valid, but the causal claim should be narrowed to always-on mild augmentation; a future worker-safe early-only design would be materially better aligned with the diagnosis.

3. **Neutral stage-3 SE makes a larger mechanistic leap than its evidence admits.** EXP-010/011 establish a weak positive signal for more dense late transformation, not for channel selection, and the cited WRN note contains no attention evidence (`knowledge/papers/wide-residual-networks.md`). EXP-012 also warns that a cheap added mechanism at stage 3 can regress even with good exposure. Exact identity initialization is excellent experimental control, but it does not show that the ratio-16 controller will learn useful gates within 300 seconds; zeroing the second projection delays learning of the first projection, and mixed examples supply ambiguous pooled semantics during 65% of training. Improve the proposal by recording gate displacement and saturation using training-only diagnostics after the run, so a failure can distinguish an inert controller from harmful learned amplification. This is not a hard-constraint violation, but its claimed 94.22% center is weakly supported.

4. **The redistribution proposal's equal-MAC framing must not be mistaken for equal optimization or statistical complexity.** The proposal itself calculates 968,538 parameters versus 691,674 accepted, and H20 kernel shapes plus larger optimizer tensors can change realized exposure. Its 97% measured-throughput gate appropriately addresses operational cost, but no preflight can address the higher-capacity overconfidence observed in EXP-011's 0.2782 test loss. Preserve the strict one-run decision rule and report final loss alongside top-1; do not rescue a near miss with `[2,1,3]` or a local-seed change.

5. **None of the three candidates violates scope, evaluator, seed, dependency, or wall-time constraints as specified.** All keep changes in `train.py`, use a single fixed seed and one scored run, preserve the frozen evaluator, and include fail-closed semantic and timing gates. The extensive preflights should remain strictly training-only: they must not become parameter selection based on evaluator output.

## Scored Verdict

- **Stage-depth redistribution `[1,2,3]`**
  - **Strength of evidence and reasoning: 8.5/10.** Two independent dense stage-3 treatments moved top-1 positively and the failed rank-64 substitute sharpens the full-width hypothesis; the unsupported part is sacrificing stage-1 depth.
  - **Potential impact: 8.0/10.** It could retain EXP-011's +0.08 signal while recovering roughly nine passes, enough to clear 94.17%, though the coupled early-block removal and 40% parameter growth cap confidence.

- **One-operation mild RandAugment**
  - **Strength of evidence and reasoning: 6.5/10.** RandAugment has direct CIFAR precedent and targets missing invariances, but the exact weak policy is not supported locally and always-on use runs against both the clean-tail success and repeated additive-regularization failures.
  - **Potential impact: 8.0/10.** Data augmentation can move generalization by several tenths with almost no counted GPU cost, but this stacked full-run policy has a substantial downside and its benefit is less likely than the architecture candidate's locally repeated signal.

- **Neutral stage-3 squeeze-and-excitation**
  - **Strength of evidence and reasoning: 5.5/10.** Identity initialization and placement are carefully controlled, but dense late-capacity results do not establish channel-attention demand and no attention-specific source or local diagnostic supports ratio 16 on both blocks.
  - **Potential impact: 7.0/10.** Input-conditioned channel selection could cross the small margin at low arithmetic cost, but an inert controller or confidence-amplifying gates are at least as plausible within the fixed budget.

## Strongest Idea

**Pick: Stage-depth redistribution `[1,2,3]`.** It wins because it is the only finalist that directly converts the goal's strongest repeated local observation, dense 8x8 capacity improving top-1 in EXP-010 and EXP-011, into a new fixed-compute hypothesis. Its main uncertainty is explicit and falsifiable: whether one early block can be traded for one dense late block. RandAugment has a higher broad-literature ceiling but is mismatched to the locally successful regularization-removal tail, while SE is operationally elegant but rests on an unsupported inference from capacity to attention. Proceed only with the proposal's exact common-state/RNG checks and 97% throughput gate, and interpret the result as a verdict on the whole `[1,2,3]` allocation rather than on late depth alone.
