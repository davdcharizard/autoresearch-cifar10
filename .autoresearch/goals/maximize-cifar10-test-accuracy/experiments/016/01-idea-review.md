**Prioritized Feedback**

1. The real bar is `>=96.48`, not “beats 96.38.” The goal requires baseline +0.1pp, and the history says that is at the noise floor: [01-definition.md](/SPXvePFS/users/david/autoresearch-benchmarks/autoresearch-cifar10/runs/v3.0.0-opus-4-8/.autoresearch/goals/maximize-cifar10-test-accuracy/01-definition.md:46), [04-results.tsv](/SPXvePFS/users/david/autoresearch-benchmarks/autoresearch-cifar10/runs/v3.0.0-opus-4-8/.autoresearch/goals/maximize-cifar10-test-accuracy/04-results.tsv:1), [03-experiment-learnings.md](/SPXvePFS/users/david/autoresearch-benchmarks/autoresearch-cifar10/runs/v3.0.0-opus-4-8/.autoresearch/goals/maximize-cifar10-test-accuracy/03-experiment-learnings.md:31). Any candidate needs a same-session control and a result clearly above 96.48.

2. BlurPool and SE both pay the fixed-budget compute tax. Prior results say per-step GPU cost reliably trades away low-LR tail epochs, and matched-epoch literature gains do not transfer cleanly: [project-insights.md](/SPXvePFS/users/david/autoresearch-benchmarks/autoresearch-cifar10/runs/v3.0.0-opus-4-8/.autoresearch/project-notes/project-insights.md:45). `train.py` times forward/backward/step inside the 300s budget: [train.py](/SPXvePFS/users/david/autoresearch-benchmarks/autoresearch-cifar10/runs/v3.0.0-opus-4-8/train.py:280). Mitigation: pre-smoke `num_epochs`; reject anything materially below the ~142-155 healthy range unless the mechanism is very strong.

3. GhostBN is the best-aligned candidate, but its implementation risk is real. The current model has full-batch BN at batch 512: [train.py](/SPXvePFS/users/david/autoresearch-benchmarks/autoresearch-cifar10/runs/v3.0.0-opus-4-8/train.py:20), [train.py](/SPXvePFS/users/david/autoresearch-benchmarks/autoresearch-cifar10/runs/v3.0.0-opus-4-8/train.py:101). With `AveragedModel(use_buffers=True)`, bad BN running stats get EMA-averaged into eval: [train.py](/SPXvePFS/users/david/autoresearch-benchmarks/autoresearch-cifar10/runs/v3.0.0-opus-4-8/train.py:255). Mitigation: normalize train activations per ghost, but update one eval buffer using correct full-batch or exactly aggregated moments; include a `ghost_size=512` equivalence smoke.

4. GhostBN’s scientific weak point is redundancy. The net already has LS 0.2, Cutout12, RandomErasing, EMA, and several regularization lanes have tied or lost. The candidate correctly names BN-stat noise as a different mechanism: [01-brainstorm.md](/SPXvePFS/users/david/autoresearch-benchmarks/autoresearch-cifar10/runs/v3.0.0-opus-4-8/.autoresearch/goals/maximize-cifar10-test-accuracy/experiments/016/01-brainstorm.md:48), but the likely failure mode is just another within-noise regularizer. Mitigation: test `ghost_size=128` and maybe `64`; avoid `32` first unless early accuracy is healthy.

5. BlurPool has the cleanest “different inductive bias” story, but weak local evidence. The model really does have aggressive pooling sites: [train.py](/SPXvePFS/users/david/autoresearch-benchmarks/autoresearch-cifar10/runs/v3.0.0-opus-4-8/train.py:149). But on 32x32 CIFAR, low-pass filtering can erase useful detail, especially if applied before the final `MaxPool2d(4)`: [train.py](/SPXvePFS/users/david/autoresearch-benchmarks/autoresearch-cifar10/runs/v3.0.0-opus-4-8/train.py:152). Mitigation: do not touch the final pool first; test only stride-2 downsamplers with the smallest fixed binomial depthwise blur.

6. SE is the weakest of the three. It is mostly a capacity/type-of-capacity bet, while EXP-014 says extra epochs and compile-funded width did not move the ceiling: [03-experiment-learnings.md](/SPXvePFS/users/david/autoresearch-benchmarks/autoresearch-cifar10/runs/v3.0.0-opus-4-8/.autoresearch/goals/maximize-cifar10-test-accuracy/03-experiment-learnings.md:66). Also, “identity init” is underspecified: plain sigmoid SE starts at 0.5, not identity. Mitigation: use `gate = 2 * sigmoid(...)` with zero-init final FC, or another exact-identity gate, and verify output equivalence plus live gradients before running.

**Scored Verdict**

| Idea | Evidence and Reasoning | Potential Impact |
|---|---:|---:|
| GhostBatchNorm | 7.5/10 - Closest evidence to this architecture and batch regime; genuinely untried BN-noise mechanism; near-free, but regularization redundancy and BN-buffer correctness are serious risks. | 6.5/10 - Plausible +0.1 to +0.3pp if large-batch BN noise is missing; unlikely to be a large jump on this already regularized plateau. |
| BlurPool | 5.5/10 - Mechanistically coherent and targets real pooling sites, but evidence is not local to clean CIFAR-32 at 96.4%, and it pays GPU cost. | 7/10 - If aliasing is a true limiter, it could clear the bar cleanly; downside is equally plausible from detail loss. |
| Squeeze-Excitation | 4.5/10 - General literature support, but local history argues against capacity additions, and the plan needs stricter identity-init discipline. | 5.5/10 - Could add useful channel recalibration, but expected upside over this saturated ResNet-9 is modest. |

**Pick: GhostBatchNorm.** It is the most likely to clear `>=96.48` because it is the only candidate with close DavidNet/large-batch rationale and near-zero epoch cost. BlurPool has a higher architectural novelty ceiling but weaker local evidence and a compute/detail-loss penalty. SE is too close to the already-failed capacity lane unless implemented as an exact-identity, very cheap probe.
