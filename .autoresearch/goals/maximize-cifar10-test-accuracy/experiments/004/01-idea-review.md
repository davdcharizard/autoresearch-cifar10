# Adversarial Idea Review — EXP-004

Read: 004 brainstorm, goal definition, learnings, results.tsv, EXP-002/003 analyses, EXP-003 idea-01 (EMA proposal), mixup/time-matters/weight-averaging notes, and train.py (confirmed: single per-batch scalar `mix`, time-based cosine to `MIN_LR=0.002`, `MIXUP_END_FRACTION=0.65`, seed 42, single run).

## Prioritized feedback (most important first)

**1. [Cross-cutting, near-fatal for the weakest ideas] The +0.1 gate sits on top of single-run, fixed-seed noise — expected gains below ~0.15 are not credible.** All three ideas are single seed-42 runs (no rerolling allowed, `01-definition.md:17`) and the brainstorm itself calls the gains "close to single-run noise" (`01-brainstorm.md:70`). EXP-002 cleared this comfortably (+0.69). An idea whose *expected* gain is ~0.1–0.3 is a coin flip against the 94.17% threshold. **Fix:** prefer the idea with the largest *directional* upside anchored in local data, and treat any winner's sub-0.15 result as inconclusive rather than an improvement.

**2. [EMA — mechanism/limiter mismatch, the decisive flaw] EMA denoises endpoint iterate variance, but the diagnosed limiter is the generalization gap, and the endpoint is already shown to have almost no variance to remove.** EXP-003 measured a 0.02-point best/final gap = "stable lower endpoint" (`003/04-analysis.md:23`); EXP-002 final==best; cosine ends at LR 0.002. The proposal itself concedes "expected gain is only about 0.10–0.30 points" and "modest-headroom" (`003/proposals/idea-01.md:41-44`), and the brainstorm estimates "upside may be below 0.10 points" (`01-brainstorm.md:57`). So the single most complex change (FP32 shadows, BN-buffer exclusion, param swap/restore, live-vs-EMA eval bookkeeping, EMA/BN mismatch risk — `idea-01.md:98-137`) buys the smallest, most likely sub-threshold gain. **Fix:** if pursued at all, only after a cheaper regularization/allocation lever is exhausted; it does not attack the stated bottleneck.

**3. [Alpha-0.1 — the supporting mechanism is backwards, and the cited evidence doesn't transfer] Lowering alpha makes Beta(a,a) *more* U-shaped, not "gentler."** Var rises from 0.179 (α=0.2) to 0.208 (α=0.1): mass concentrates *harder* at 0/1, so most batches are nearly unmixed → **weaker** regularization, pushing back toward the 93.38% non-mixup baseline (the idea's own risk note admits this, `01-brainstorm.md:44`). Worse, with a single per-batch scalar (train.py:121-124), a λ≈0.05 batch yields an almost-original image paired with a 95%-flipped target — that injects label noise, not cleaner linearity. And the justification borrows EXP-003 ("stronger spatial mixing hurt", `01-brainstorm.md:38`) — but EXP-003's regression was attributed to *area-weighted spatial labels being a worse mechanism*, not to strength (`003/04-analysis.md:24`). That evidence does not imply α=0.2 is too strong. **Fix:** if strength is worth probing, test α=0.3–0.4 (toward the mixup paper's CIFAR range) where "stronger" is a coherent hypothesis, rather than a direction with no local support and a likely-regressive mechanism.

**4. [50% cutoff — strongest local anchor, but its evidence is partly confounded] The "more hard-label tail helps" argument leans on EXP-002's tail improvement, which is confounded with LR annealing.** EXP-002 improving through its tail and ending at best (`002/04-analysis.md:24`) is the most direct local support any EXP-004 idea has — and this is exactly the "narrow next question" the diagnosis names (`01-brainstorm.md:21`). But the tail gain is at least partly cosine LR→0.002, not tail *length*; moving the cutoff to 50% starts hard labels at a *higher* mid-cosine LR, so it's not a clean "more of the good thing." There's also no local evidence the mixup critical period ends by 50% (time-matters note gives no cutoff, `time-matters-regularization.md`), so this could simply shorten the regularizer and overfit. **Fix:** frame as a genuine two-sided probe (65% is validated; 50% may under-regularize), and consider that a *later* cutoff (75%) is equally motivated by "critical period + more mixup" — 50% is only one arm of the untuned range EXP-002 flagged (`002/04-analysis.md:36`).

**5. [Sound aspects worth crediting] All three respect hard constraints** (train.py-only, ~142-pass exposure preserved, ≤1 eval/epoch, no seed hacking), and none retries a failed approach unchanged — EXP-003's CutMix failure is correctly *not* repeated. The EMA proposal is unusually rigorous on attribution and downside controls even though its ceiling is low.

| **Gentler Alpha-0.1** | **3/10** — direction is mechanistically backwards (more bimodal = weaker), cited EXP-003 evidence doesn't transfer. | **4/10** — plausible small gain, but real downside toward 93.38 baseline. |

## Pick: **Earlier 50% Mixup Cutoff**

It wins on merit, not caution. It is the only idea whose core justification comes from this goal's *strongest and most direct* datapoint — EXP-002's continued hard-label-tail improvement with final==best — and it directly probes the exact balance the diagnosis flags as the open question, as a clean low-effort single-variable change with unambiguous attribution. EMA loses because its mechanism doesn't match the diagnosed limiter and its own expected gain is below the gate; alpha-0.1 loses because its "gentler" premise is mechanistically inverted and its supporting evidence is a mismatched transfer from EXP-003. My one caution for the winner: treat it as two-sided (50% may under-regularize) and consider sweeping the cutoff (50% *and* 75%) rather than betting on a single untested arm — and require a result comfortably above +0.15 before calling it real, given single-run noise.
