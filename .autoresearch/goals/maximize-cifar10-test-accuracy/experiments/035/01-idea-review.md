# Idea Review EXP-035

## Prioritized Feedback

1. **Alpha 0.1 is the stronger candidate, but only as a low-confidence closure experiment.** EXP002 established early batch-shared alpha-0.2 mixup as a +0.69-point treatment, and EXP027 retained it inside the current +0.25-point depth/RandAugment composition. The alpha-0.1 proposal changes one concentration while preserving the proven input/target interpolation, batch coherence, 65% cutoff, and hard tail. However, EXP004/005/015/020 all regressed under neighboring mixup changes, and EXP027 shows no direct excess-regularization symptom. Keep the claim strictly falsifiable: this tests whether the new composition needs weaker interpolation, not whether lower alpha follows from the alpha-0.4 miss.

2. **Replacing mixup with label smoothing discards the strongest local causal evidence without identifying a failure it corrects.** `knowledge/papers/label-smoothing.md` supports mild smoothing for overconfident classifiers, but neither `02-system-understanding.md` nor any cited report measures confidence or calibration; near-zero training loss alone does not establish that uniform target smoothing is the missing mechanism. More importantly, label smoothing removes both interpolated inputs and example-pair targets that produced EXP002's gain. This is not a hard-constraint violation, but it is a major mechanism replacement whose generic literature prior is weaker than the project-specific evidence being surrendered. It should not advance unless future evidence specifically implicates mixed-image bias or calibration.

3. **Epsilon 0.05 is essentially ungrounded and a one-point miss would be hard to interpret.** For ten classes, PyTorch-style smoothing yields target mass 0.955 on the labeled class and 0.005 on each other class, a much milder and qualitatively different objective than alpha-0.2 mixup. The cited note warns that smoothing requires calibration but supplies no reason for 0.05, and there is no local epsilon bracket. A miss could mean label smoothing is inferior, epsilon is wrong, or removal of input interpolation is harmful. Choosing another epsilon afterward would become an adjacent rescue rather than a conclusion supported by this experiment.

4. **The label-smoothing rationale overstates preservation of the accepted regularization structure.** It preserves the 65%/35% timing, but not the early treatment: uniform class mass does not encode the paired image content or enforce linear behavior between examples. `knowledge/papers/time-matters-regularization.md` supports early removal schedules in general, not interchangeability of regularizers. If this candidate is ever revisited, its hypothesis must explicitly separate target-entropy benefit from lost vicinal/input regularization, and the exact manual or built-in loss convention must be preregistered and verified.

5. **Alpha 0.1 has a real under-regularization risk that the proposal already quantifies.** Its endpoint mass rises from 67.34% to 81.28%, while EXP004 showed that reducing the duration of useful interpolation lost 0.16 points and worsened loss. Duration and concentration are not equivalent, so this is contrary rather than disqualifying evidence. Preserve the proposal's >=130-pass gate, sole seed-42 score, final-accuracy/loss corroboration, and full one-run family closure; do not follow a valid miss with alpha 0.05, 0.15, cutoff changes, or RNG realignment.

6. **Changing alpha is clean at source level but not bitwise trajectory isolation.** EXP005 established that concentration-dependent Beta rejection changes later CUDA coefficient and permutation draws. The existing proposal handles this correctly with independent distribution checks and describes the result as the alpha-defined fixed-seed process. The brainstorm should retain that limitation prominently: even a ten-example win advances the fixed protocol but does not estimate a seed-averaged effect or identify a continuous optimum.

7. **Keep the same primary and interpretive thresholds for either candidate.** Success is `best_test_acc >=94.42%`; `final_test_acc >=94.32%` and `final_test_loss <=0.2523` are corroboration only. A lower-loss label-smoothed run cannot override a primary miss, especially because EXP022 and EXP033 already showed that better or smoother loss behavior need not improve top-1.

## Scored Verdict

| Candidate | Evidence / reasoning | Potential impact |
|---|---|---|
| Weaker Alpha-0.1 Batch-Shared Mixup | **2.5/5** - It preserves the strongest proven regularizer and cleanly closes the only unmeasured one-constant strength bracket on the new composition, but every nearby mixup perturbation is negative and no result shows alpha 0.2 is too strong. | **2/5** - A weaker interpolation bias could move the required handful of boundaries at effectively zero cost, but 81.28% endpoint-heavy batches create substantial under-regularization risk and the likely effect is small. |
| Early Epsilon-0.05 Label Smoothing Instead of Mixup | **1.5/5** - Generic literature supports smoothing, but there is no local confidence diagnosis, no evidence for epsilon 0.05, and the treatment abandons EXP002's +0.69-point input/target interpolation mechanism. | **2.5/5** - A qualitatively different target prior could outperform mixup if mixed images are now harmful, but that upside is speculative and inseparable from removing the project's best demonstrated regularizer. |

## Pick

**Weaker Alpha-0.1 Batch-Shared Mixup.** It wins because it makes the smallest interpretable change to the strongest locally validated generalization mechanism and already has a complete fail-closed semantic, throughput, scoring, and family-closure contract in `experiments/033/proposals/idea-01.md`. The negative neighborhood makes expected improvement low, but the result will still resolve a defined open bracket. Epsilon-0.05 label smoothing has a somewhat broader exploratory ceiling, yet replacing proven mixup without measured overconfidence, a justified epsilon, or local evidence leaves a miss fundamentally ambiguous. Advance only the exact one-line alpha change, preserve batch sharing and the strict 65% cutoff, and close immediate mixup-strength tuning after any valid normal-exposure miss.
