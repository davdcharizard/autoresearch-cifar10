# Adversarial Review — EXP-033

## Prioritized feedback

**1. Idea 1 (Conv fan-out): mechanism does not attack the diagnosed limiter — this is the "improves something, but not the bound" gap.** The system understanding (`02-system-understanding.md` §Current Bottleneck) is explicit: the accuracy bottleneck is "generalization under a short strong-view phase," and a representation candidate "must preserve EXP-010's healthy 89.73% switch fit." Idea 1 changes the initial scale of exactly 3 tensors (stem + two widening convs), and the proposal *itself* proves (idea-01.md §"What BatchNorm makes invariant") that BN following all three approximately cancels the forward function. The entire residual thesis is transient optimizer geometry (10.67x/2x relative first updates). But running-stat adaptation and weight-norm re-equilibration erase that within a handful of steps, and there is *no* evidence — local or in torchvision's ImageNet lineage — that fan_out vs fan_in changes final accuracy in a BN network. Precedent is implementation convention, not accuracy evidence (the proposal concedes this). **Path to improve:** none that rescues the ceiling — this is a low-upside change, likely landing in the 94.1–94.2 null band that EXP-011/012/017/023/026 already populate, below the +0.10 gate.

**2. Idea 1 sits squarely in the failed "global-LR optimizer-path / init-geometry" family (EXP-012/014/015/020/022/024/025/028/031/032).** The one thing it has going for it is safety (BN cancellation makes class-collapse unlikely, unlike EXP-014/020/024). But "safe because it does almost nothing" is the core problem, not a virtue, when the objective is to clear a gate. The mandatory exact-corpus gates are appropriate and well-specified, but they only prove *no harm* — they cannot manufacture the missing upside.

**3. Idea 3 (Random Erasing): the strong-phase-underfit risk is the real and repeated failure mode of this recipe — but the differentiation argument is genuinely valid.** Local history is a graveyard for *added strong-phase regularization*: EXP-006 (16x16 Cutout replacement, −0.67), EXP-011 (CutMix p→0.75, −0.15), EXP-026 (Mixup split, deeper switch underfit), EXP-027 (−0.46). The diagnosis warns the width-2 model is at the underfit boundary. This is the dominant strike against idea 3. **However**, those failures were *replacements* or *intensifications* of existing mechanisms; idea-03.md's 1.5%-average, probabilistic, *compositional* mean-fill erasing (kept N1/M7+CutMix intact until 80%) is a distinct, orthogonal invariance (absence-tolerance), not a stronger dose of regional mixing. That is materially different — it is not a disqualifying retry of EXP-006. **Required gate:** the switch-fit checkpoint vs 89.73% (and the 87.08% underfit marker) is the decisive attribution instrument and must be honored as a diagnosis, exactly as the proposal states.

**4. Idea 3's confounds are well-identified but must not be waived:** (a) **Exposure** — per-image `fork_rng` + two provenance scalars are real host work; learnings (EXP-003, EXP-029) show "nominally cheap" helpers can cost 2–7% of steps. The paired full-step timing gate demanding ≥26,629 steps (99%) and worker-throughput headroom is mandatory, not optional — a step loss would confound "regularization hurt" with "less training." (b) **CutMix-over-erasing** — erasing *before* CutMix means CutMix can overwrite erased regions, so effective erased area < nominal; the provenance mask must be propagated through CutMix (proposal does this). (c) **RNG neutrality** — `fork_rng` must not shift downstream crop/flip/RandAugment/CutMix streams, or the whole comparison is confounded. All three are correctly gated in idea-03.md §4–6.

**5. Idea 2 (small FC init): weakest evidence, and the collapse precedent is directly on-point.** The proposal admits "weak direct transfer evidence, not a paper-backed expected win," and there is no local or literature support for `std=0.01` specifically in this recipe. EXP-014 (zero-init max classifier → 4.10x classifier gradient → collapse to 10.00%) is the exact geometry family this touches; BN does *not* protect the unnormalized head (proposal concedes this). The 12.5x scale cut is more aggressive than idea 1's changes. The "implicit warmup" mechanism is plausible but symmetric — it could equally undertrain the backbone or be overwritten in a few steps (proposal admits both). Medium collapse risk with no evidence of upside. **Path to improve:** if pursued, a less aggressive scale (e.g., match fan-in more closely) and an explicit first-update FC-norm-growth gate — but that becomes a different, still-unsupported hypothesis.

**6. Cross-cutting caveat for all three: single-seed, +0.10 = 10 test images.** Every proposal honestly flags this. A bare pass on any of them is protocol-valid but weak causal evidence versus CUDA/trajectory noise. This does not disqualify any candidate (the protocol forbids reroll), but it means the winner should show a *coherent* trajectory (switch fit + first-weak + final NLL all consistent), not just a lucky peak.

No candidate has a hard-constraint violation, reward-hack, or unchanged-retry disqualifier. All are scope-legal (train.py-only, zero/near-zero VRAM, RNG-preserving).

## Scored verdict

| Idea | Evidence & reasoning | Impact ceiling |
|---|---|---|
| **Random Erasing (idea-03)** | **6/10** — strong, well-established CIFAR literature + honest, rigorous mechanism; but genuine local counter-evidence that this tight strong phase rejects added regularization. | **7/10** — the *only* candidate attacking the diagnosed generalization limiter; high upper tail (94.3–94.4 plausible) if width-2 has absence-invariance headroom. |
| **Conv fan-out (idea-01)** | **7/10** — meticulous, self-honest, direct torchvision precedent and exact scale accounting; but the reasoning undercuts itself (BN cancels the forward; precedent ≠ accuracy). | **3/10** — 3 tensors, BN-neutralized, off-limiter; most likely a sub-gate null. |
| **Small FC init (idea-02)** | **4/10** — self-admitted weak transfer evidence, no support for std=0.01 here, EXP-014 collapse precedent on-point. | **4/10** — symmetric/uncertain effect, medium collapse risk, no evidenced upside. |

**Strongest idea: Conservative Random Erasing (idea-03).**

It is the only candidate whose mechanism targets the actual stated bottleneck — generalization under the short strong phase — rather than the init/optimizer-geometry family that has failed ~10 times in this goal's history. Its literature backing (Random Erasing, a reliably positive CIFAR augmentation reported complementary to crop/flip) is real accuracy evidence, unlike idea 1's implementation-convention precedent, and its key risk (strong-phase underfit) is materially differentiated from the specific prior failures it superficially resembles: mild (1.5% avg), probabilistic, and *additive* to — not a replacement for — the validated N1/M7+CutMix phase. On the judge's explicit standard ("credit a high ceiling backed by sound reasoning; do not risk-discount a well-argued exploratory bet"), idea 3's fatter upper tail and on-limiter mechanism beat idea 1's rigorous-but-near-null ceiling and idea 2's unsupported, collapse-prone head change.

Idea 1 is the runner-up purely on safety and rigor, but a change the proposal itself shows BN erases is unlikely to move a +0.10 gate — its expected outcome is a no-improvement null. Idea 2 places last: weakest evidence plus the most on-point collapse precedent.

**Required gates for the pick (idea-03), in priority order:** (1) paired full-step timing → ≥26,629 steps (99% exposure) with demonstrated worker throughput headroom — exposure loss would confound the result; (2) switch-fit checkpoint vs 89.73% / 87.08% marker as the underfit-attribution instrument; (3) exact-corpus concentration/update gates + RNG-neutrality proof for `fork_rng`; (4) provenance mask propagated through CutMix to confirm effective erased area. Treat a sub-gate result at healthy switch fit as evidence the erasing effect is below protocol resolution, and a low switch as confirmation of compounded underfit — do not tune p/area to rescue within EXP-033.
