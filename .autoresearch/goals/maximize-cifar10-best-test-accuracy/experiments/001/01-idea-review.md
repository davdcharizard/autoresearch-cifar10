# Adversarial Review — EXP-001 Finalists

## Prioritized Feedback

**1. [Idea 2 — fatal-adjacent] Label smoothing does not attack the diagnosed limiter, and its accuracy evidence is weak.** The brainstorm and both other proposals agree the *measured* defect is schedule/horizon misalignment: milestones `[32000, 48000]` in `train.py:146` vs. a run that reaches only 38,525 steps, so the model gets ~0 updates below `lr=0.01`. Label smoothing is orthogonal regularization — it does nothing about underconvergence. Worse, the cited Müller et al. ("When Does Label Smoothing Help?") is primarily a *calibration/distillation* result; its top-1 accuracy gains on CIFAR are small and inconsistent, and it can hurt an underfit model. The proposal itself concedes the effect "may be below the required 0.1-point gain" (idea-02.md:105) — against a hard 0.1pp bar, that is a coin-flip on a `no-improvement` verdict. *Fix:* if kept, stack it on a fixed schedule rather than the broken baseline schedule, so it regularizes a model that actually converges — but then it's no longer an isolated first experiment.

**2. [Idea 3 — most severe executability risk] The first run is a 6-way confounded kitchen sink with high crash/invalid probability.** BF16 + channels-last + `torch.compile(reduce-overhead)` + GPU-resident vectorized crop/flip + batch 512/LR 0.4 + a new 1.08M preact architecture, all at once. Any single failure burns the loop. Two concrete, under-addressed failure mechanisms: (a) `mode="reduce-overhead"` uses CUDA graphs, which require static input tensor addresses — but the per-batch `randperm`, per-image crop offsets, and flip masks allocate fresh tensors each step, a classic trigger for capture errors or silent fallback; (b) the run is killed at 600s total, and compile warm-up + wider-model steps make the total-time envelope much harder to predict than the guarded "≤120s compile" bound suggests. *Fix:* de-bundle. The single highest-value, lowest-risk slice is **wider PreAct ResNet + elapsed-time cosine in FP32/eager** (no compile, no BF16, no vectorized aug, keep the CPU loader). That isolates the capacity bet — the actual source of the high ceiling — from the fragile systems stack, which can be layered in later ablations the proposal already plans.

**3. [Idea 3 — unacknowledged confound] Weight decay is silently changed `1e-4 → 5e-4`.** idea-03.md:71 sets `weight_decay=5e-4` alongside LR 0.4, batch 512, and the new model, but the "Confound Controls" section (idea-03.md:114) doesn't list WD among the changed variables. That's a fourth optimizer-side change riding along uncontrolled. *Fix:* either justify 5e-4 explicitly as part of the large-batch recipe or hold it at baseline 1e-4 for the first run.

**4. [Idea 1 — the main open risk] Immediate cosine decay from step 0 discards the high-LR exploration phase the baseline relies on.** The standard CIFAR ResNet recipe holds `lr=0.1` for a long plateau; cosine drops below 0.1 immediately and hits ~0.05 by 50% (idea-01.md:66-69). For ResNet-20 this can underfit relative to "hold-then-anneal." The proposal flags this and pre-registers the right follow-up (a 10-20% flat 0.1 hold then cosine). *Suggested improvement:* consider making the first run itself a short-hold + cosine, since that is the closest time-aligned analog to the recipe the baseline was tuned for and de-risks the one real failure mode — at essentially no extra cost or confound.

**5. [Ideas 1 & 2 — shared latent timeout risk they don't fix] Both keep 99 per-epoch evaluations, leaving only 4.6s under the 600s kill.** Baseline total is 595.4s with ~292s spent on 99 evals. Neither idea reduces this, so ordinary jitter (or label smoothing's tiny extra loss-reduction cost) can cross 600s → auto-failure. Ironically only idea 3 addresses it (sparse eval). *Fix:* both could cheaply buy large wall-clock margin by evaluating less often (still ≤1/epoch, and skipping some epochs is allowed) — near-free insurance against a spurious timeout failure.

**6. [Idea 3 — measurement tension] Sparse validation can *lower* recorded `best_test_acc`.** Cutting to 5 evals reduces the number of chances to catch a transient peak, and `best_test_acc` is a max over evaluations (`train.py:207`). The cosine-favors-late argument is plausible but not guaranteed; a mid-run peak under the aggressive LR-0.4 transient could be missed. *Fix:* evaluate a few extra times in the final 20% where the best is expected, keeping total evals modest but denser near the annealed tail.

**7. [Idea 1 — minor] Nesterov is a second simultaneous variable.** Low-cost and low-risk, but it muddies attribution of a schedule result. The proposal already pre-registers the cosine-only disambiguation follow-up (idea-01.md:98), which adequately contains this.

## Scored Verdict

**Idea 1 — Budget-aligned cosine SGD + Nesterov**
- Evidence/reasoning: **9/10** — grounded in the exact measured defect in `train.py` (unreached milestones, quantified step budget) plus standard SGDR support; the one real risk (early anneal) is identified with a pre-planned fix.
- Potential impact: **7/10** — reliably clears the 0.1pp bar; expected +0.3-0.7pp with a genuine terminal-refinement phase the baseline entirely lacks. Ceiling moderate.

**Idea 3 — H20 throughput dividend + wider PreAct ResNet**
- Evidence/reasoning: **6/10** — mechanistically well-argued and the capacity lever is sound, but evidence is diluted by ~6 confounded changes (incl. an unlisted WD change) and two concrete, under-mitigated failure modes (CUDA-graph capture, total-time envelope).
- Potential impact: **8/10** — highest raw ceiling (+0.6-1.5pp); added capacity is the most reliable way to move CIFAR accuracy *if the run survives*. Risk-adjusted, the first-run success probability is the concern, not the ceiling.

**Idea 2 — Label smoothing 0.1**
- Evidence/reasoning: **5/10** — clean and attributable, but does not target the diagnosed limiter and leans on a paper whose accuracy (vs. calibration) benefit is weak/inconsistent.
- Potential impact: **3/10** — low ceiling (+0.1-0.4 optimistic) with a real chance of falling under the 0.1pp threshold; dominated by idea 1 on the same low-cost, one-variable footing.

**Pick: Idea 1 — Budget-aligned cosine SGD (with Nesterov).** It wins on the strongest reasoning of the three — it repairs a *measured, quantified* defect (zero updates below `lr=0.01`) rather than a hypothesized weakness — at near-zero cost and risk, and it reliably clears the acceptance bar with a solid moderate ceiling. Idea 2 is strictly dominated: same low-risk footing, weaker mechanism, and it doesn't touch the diagnosed limiter. Idea 3 has the highest ceiling and would be the most exciting bet, but as a *first* experiment it is over-bundled and crash-prone, and its central value (added capacity) is partly independent of the diagnosis and can be captured later with far less risk. Notably, idea 1's time-aligned cosine is a *component* of idea 3, so running it first is also the correct sequencing: it validates the schedule fix cleanly, after which the wider-model capacity bet (a de-bundled idea 3, per feedback #2) becomes the natural high-ceiling follow-up.
