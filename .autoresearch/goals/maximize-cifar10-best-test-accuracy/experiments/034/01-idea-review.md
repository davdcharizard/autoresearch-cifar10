# Adversarial Review — EXP-034

## Prioritized feedback (most important first)

**1. [Stochastic Depth — fatal] The accuracy mechanism is contradicted by this goal's own history, and its stated falsifier is likely pre-tripped.** Dropping a residual branch returns the identity — this *is* an "identity-oriented residual change," the exact family that already failed: EXP-012 full preactivation lowered switch fit 2.85 pts and EXP-015 zero-gamma lowered it 3.25 pts (`03-experiment-learnings.md`, Failed Approaches / High). The model *underfits* the strong phase; adding a depth-ensemble regularizer pushes the wrong direction, alongside every other strong-phase regularization that lost (EXP-011 CutMix-p0.75, EXP-026 balanced Mixup, EXP-027 early hard N1/M7). The proposal's own gate requires switch accuracy ≥ 87.08%, but a 2.85–3.25 pt drop from 89.73% lands at ~86.5–86.9%, *below its own floor*. The p=0.03/83.3%-full-graph "conservatism" only shrinks the effect; it does not change the sign. This is close to retrying a rejected approach unchanged (EXP-020 already reviewed and rejected the p=0.05 variant). *Path:* there is no cheap fix that preserves the mechanism — the direction itself conflicts with the diagnosis.

**2. [Channels-Last — serious] The sole accuracy link (exposure) has direct counter-evidence, not just missing evidence.** The proposal admits it is a two-link bet and that link 2 is unproven, but the record is worse than "unproven": EXP-026 reached 27,268 steps and EXP-032 ran at 100.52% exposure — both ≥ accepted exposure — and both *missed* the +0.10 gate (`04-results.tsv`). `02-system-understanding.md` states plainly "additional exposure has not yet been causally tested at the accepted recipe." So even a *successful* 3% speedup → 27,730 steps (vs 26,898, a ~3% bump) is very unlikely to clear +0.10 when larger exposure gains already failed. The systems work is rigorous, but the payoff argument is the weak link. *Path:* reframe honestly as a systems-enabling result (freeing budget for a future capacity change) rather than a standalone accuracy bet — but that concedes it should not be a scored accuracy run on its own.

**3. [Channels-Last — serious] High probability the timing gate never opens on this workload, yielding no scored run.** The cited knowledge file (`pytorch-channels-last.md`) explicitly warns that channels-last gains concentrate in reduced precision and large activations, while this is fixed FP32/TF32 on 32×32 tiny kernels; Option-A slice/pad shortcuts, `.view`, and adaptive-pool are flagged fallback risks. The load-bearing ≥3% gate is plausibly unreachable, so the most likely outcome is "gate fails, no production run." That is a legitimate negative result, but it means low expected value from spending the experiment slot here. *Path:* none needed — the gate is correctly load-bearing; just recognize the low hit-probability when choosing.

**4. [Fan-Out — moderate] The forward effect is largely cancelled by BN, so the real lever is optimizer-geometry perturbation — the recurring class-collapse family.** The proposal is admirably honest that only 3 tensors change and BN approximately cancels the scale, leaving a ~10.67× larger relative stem update as the actual mechanism (`idea-02.md` §"BatchNorm invariance is approximate"). That is the same optimizer-path-perturbation profile behind the recurring failure (EXP-020/022/028, `project-insights.md`). The immutable-corpus + update-spike gates are exactly the right mitigation and are well specified. The residual concern is impact: with only 3 changed tensors and BN cancellation, the expected gain is plausibly below the +0.10 threshold. *Path:* keep the gates as written; set expectations that a sub-threshold valid result is the modal outcome.

**5. [Fan-Out — evidence gap] "torchvision uses fan_out" is precedent, not evidence for this setting.** Torchvision's convention targets deep ImageNet nets; for a 9-block BN'd CIFAR ResNet the fan_in/fan_out distinction is mostly absorbed by BN (as the proposal itself derives). Fine to proceed, but the point prediction (94.28%) is overconfident given the mechanism is a small, BN-damped reparameterization. *Path:* state the prediction as a wide band, not a point.

**6. [Stochastic Depth — compute] The 1.69% MAC prior is likely erased in practice.** EXP-029 showed a theoretically-tiny 11-line change added 1.97% full-step cost; Python per-block branching + residual scaling here can similarly consume the 1.69% forward-MAC saving, and backward (the real bottleneck) is only avoided on the 16.7% of batches that skip a single block. The ≥1% measured gate is marginal at best.

**7. [Cross-cutting — positive] Integrity design is sound across all three.** All correctly use immutable, worker-materialized corpora (respecting the EXP-019/021/026 forkserver lesson), forbid rescue/reroll, and both Channels-Last and Stochastic Depth add the MAX_EVALUATIONS=19 cap so faster epochs cannot inflate the max-over-checkpoints metric (the EXP-013 bias in `project-insights.md`). No reward-hacking detected in any candidate.

## Scored verdict

| Idea | Evidence & reasoning | Potential impact |
|---|---|---|
| **Conv2d-only fan_out** | **6.5** — clean, honest mechanistic derivation; orthogonal and untried; collapse risk well-gated. But "evidence" is torchvision precedent with weak transfer, and BN largely cancels the forward effect. | **5.0** — modest but genuine; the only candidate attacking representation without relying on the discredited exposure lever; small 3-tensor footprint caps the ceiling near the gate. |
| **End-to-end channels-last** | **6.0** — superb systems diagnosis and airtight integrity protocol, but the accuracy payoff rests entirely on exposure, which EXP-026/032 actively contradict. | **4.0** — likely fails the 3% gate on tiny FP32 (no run); even if it runs, ~3% more steps is unlikely to move +0.10 when larger-exposure runs already missed. |
| **3% batchwise stochastic depth** | **3.5** — self-defeating: its own switch-fit floor (87.08%) is threatened by the identity-residual history it cites (−2.85/−3.25); retries an EXP-020-rejected family and a losing regularization direction. | **3.0** — double weak link: ~1% speedup likely erased by Python branching (EXP-029), and the regularization side is contradicted by strong-phase underfit. |

**Pick: Conv2d-only Kaiming fan_out initialization.**

It wins on expected value, narrowly over channels-last. It is the only candidate whose success mechanism is *not* contradicted by this goal's history: it attacks representation/optimization (the class of lever — capacity, curriculum — that has actually produced this project's gains) rather than the exposure link that EXP-026/032 already showed does not clear the gate, and it does not retry a failed family the way stochastic depth does. Its downside is genuinely bounded — it cannot burn the time budget, it will produce a scored run, and its one real risk (amplified stem updates) is precisely what the immutable-corpus gate is built to veto. Channels-last is the more elegant and rigorous proposal, but its payoff is doubly gated by a mechanism with counter-evidence and a timing gate it will probably fail; stochastic depth is the weakest, pointing the strong phase in the direction that keeps losing. Fan-out's honest weakness is a possibly sub-threshold effect — but a small, safe, mechanistically-coherent bet beats an elegant bet whose payoff history has already falsified.
