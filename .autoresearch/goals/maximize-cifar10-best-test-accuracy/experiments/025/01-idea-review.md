# Adversarial Review — EXP-025 Candidate Ideas

## Prioritized Critique (most important first)

**1. [Channels-Last & Batch-96] Both attack the systems bottleneck, but the accuracy mechanism they depend on is explicitly untested.** `02-system-understanding.md` §Headroom states plainly: *"additional exposure has not yet been causally tested at the accepted recipe."* Both ideas are pure exposure plays — their entire accuracy story is *more updates → higher top-1*. Neither cites any local result showing that link holds. This is the classic mechanism-vs-limiter gap: the diagnosis says generalization under the short strong phase bounds the metric, not update count. A successful speedup could land squarely at +0.00. *Fix:* neither should be run as a bare exposure bet — they'd only be worth it paired with a lever independently shown to convert exposure into accuracy, and no such lever exists in the history.

**2. [Channels-Last] The speedup itself is doubtful, not just the downstream link.** The brainstorm concedes "tiny FP32 NCHW kernels may already be optimal." Channels-last wins are largest for tensor-core FP16/BF16 convs with large channel counts; here it's FP32/TF32 with 32/64/128 channels at 8–32px. cuDNN often lacks well-tuned NHWC FP32 kernels at these sizes and can be *slower*, with layout-conversion of every input/eval batch eating any gain (Risk Assessment admits this). Combined with the strict paired-timing gate, the most likely outcome is *invalid / no scored evidence*. *Fix:* would need a quick standalone kernel micro-benchmark before committing an experiment slot — but even a win runs into weakness #1. Low expected value.

**3. [Batch-96] Likely *reduces* image exposure while raising gradient noise at an unchanged, now-too-high LR.** Backward (75.46%) scales roughly with batch, but launch/sync overhead is fixed, so smaller batches are *less* efficient per image → fewer total images in 300s, the opposite of the intended effect. Meanwhile the idea keeps LR 0.1 (smaller batch conventionally wants lower LR) and refuses LR tuning — the brainstorm itself flags "LR 0.1 may be too high for 96," and over-noisy CutMix soft-target gradients are a real collapse risk (cf. the concentration transients that have vetoed EXP-016/020/022/024). *Fix:* none that stays in scope — dropping LR would confound the batch change; this is a poorly-posed single knob.

**4. [Batch-96] Evaluation-count reward-hack smell.** Smaller batch → more epochs → more dense-tail evals in the final 20% → more max-over-checkpoint draws. The idea acknowledges this and pre-registers equal elapsed eval count, which is the right instinct, but a benefit that appears only through extra checkpoint lotteries would violate the goal's "genuine training enhancement, not evaluation changes" clause. *Fix:* the pre-registration must be airtight and reported, or the result is uninterpretable.

**5. [ECA] Razor-thin acceptance margin vs. a ~15-parameter, 3-block intervention — the dominant impact concern.** Success is 94.25%, only ~10 CIFAR-10 test examples above 94.15%. The proposal itself flags this. Conditional recalibration of only `layer3` via 15 weights is a small-effect intervention; the true effect could easily sit inside single-run variance, and the goal forbids seed rerolls, so there's no averaging to recover statistical power. *Mitigation already in the proposal (credit it):* it corroborates any top-1 pass with trajectory signatures (89.73% switch fit, 93.16% first-weak checkpoint, 0.1934 NLL). Lean hard on those — a top-1 pass *without* the NLL/fit signature should be treated as weak evidence, not a win.

**6. [ECA] Claimed mechanism operates mostly in the CutMix regime where its descriptor is most ambiguous.** The gain must accrue during the 80% plateau (the tail is hard-label weak), and 50% of plateau batches are CutMix. Global-avg-pool produces one descriptor over two pasted regions while the target is area-weighted — the single per-channel gate can amplify whichever region dominates features, fighting target mass. ECA-Net's evidence is clean-label ImageNet; the transfer to shallow-CIFAR + regional soft targets is genuinely unestablished (`eca-net.md` §Relevance concedes this). *Fix:* the safety gate measures gate behavior separately on hard vs. soft batches — extend that to *report* whether gates behave differently under CutMix, so a null/negative result can be attributed to descriptor ambiguity rather than left ambiguous.

**7. [ECA] Channel-locality prior is arbitrary in a from-scratch net.** The length-5 Conv1d assumes semantic adjacency of channel *indices*; ECA's k=5 (gamma=2,b=1 at 128ch) is an ImageNet-derived heuristic with no reason to hold under CIFAR channel ordering, potentially imposing an unhelpful bias. This is acknowledged. It's a real ceiling-limiter but not fatal; a global mixing alternative (SE) was already shown costly in EXP-012, so ECA is a defensible choice to avoid that.

**8. [ECA] Timing feasibility is a live invalid-risk, not fatal.** EXP-012's 9-block SE cost 1.233×; this removes the MLP bottleneck and 6 of 9 sites, but 3× (pool, Conv1d, sigmoid, multiply, +backwards) sequential launches against a 3.5% allowance is tight. Outcome on failure is a clean invalid, which the gate handles — bounded, known risk.

**9. [ECA] NOT a disqualifying retry of EXP-012/015 (checked).** Those failed by *suppressing* the branch (zero-gamma) or changing ordering (full preactivation). ECA initializes to *exact unit gates* (2·sigmoid(0)=1), preserving step-zero function, depth, widths, and Option-A ratios — materially different, and the proposal argues this explicitly. Legitimate.

## Scored Verdict

| Idea | Evidence & Reasoning | Potential Impact |
|---|---|---|
| **Identity-Init Final-Stage ECA** | **8** — strong EXP-007/010 lineage, pre-reviewed with exhaustive identity/safety/timing gates, and materially differentiated from EXP-012/015/023/024 failures; CIFAR+CutMix transfer remains unproven. | **6** — targets the *diagnosed accuracy* bottleneck directly, but ~15-param/3-block effect size vs. a 0.10-pt gate caps realized upside. |
| **Channels-Last Accepted Model** | **4** — speedup plausible but unverified for FP32 small kernels (may be neutral/slower), and the exposure→accuracy link is explicitly untested. | **3** — indirect; ceiling bounded by an unproven link, most-likely outcome is invalid or +0.00. |
| **Batch-96 Accepted Recipe** | **3** — directionally weak: likely fewer images, LR mismatch at unchanged 0.1, and a diagnosis that doesn't point here. | **3** — probable exposure *loss* plus eval-count reward-hack smell; low and possibly negative. |

**Strongest idea: Identity-Initialized Final-Stage ECA.**

It is the only candidate that attacks the bottleneck the diagnosis actually names — generalization/feature allocation under the short strong phase — rather than a systems bottleneck whose accuracy payoff is explicitly flagged as untested. Its reasoning is the most rigorous of the three: it inherits the EXP-007→010 capacity thesis, sidesteps the exact static-reallocation failures of EXP-023/024 by staying conditional and preserving the accepted step-zero function/depth/widths/ratios, and arrives with a pre-reviewed safety and timing harness. The two exposure ideas share a single fatal weakness (unproven exposure→accuracy) and add their own problems (channels-last may not even speed up; batch-96 likely reduces exposure and risks noise-driven collapse at an unadjusted LR). ECA's real weaknesses — a thin statistical margin and CutMix-descriptor ambiguity — are ceiling-limiters, not disqualifiers, and are best managed by insisting the verdict rest on the NLL/switch-fit/first-weak trajectory signatures rather than a bare max-over-checkpoints top-1. Pick ECA; require the mechanistic signatures for acceptance and report gate behavior split by hard vs. CutMix batches so a null result is interpretable.
