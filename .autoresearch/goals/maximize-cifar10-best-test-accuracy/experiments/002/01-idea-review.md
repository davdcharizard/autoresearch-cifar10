# Adversarial Review — EXP-002 Finalists

## Prioritized Feedback

**1. [Idea 2 — mechanism vs. diagnosis tension, most important] Added capacity attacks a limiter the diagnosis does not claim, and trades against the one resource the run is actually short of.** EXP-001's analysis is explicit: train loss fell to `0.0215` while test stayed below baseline, so "missing terminal decay was not the only limiter... the most plausible mechanism is generalization" (`001/04-analysis.md` §Analysis). The model already fits the training set to ~zero loss — it is *not* train-capacity-limited. Bolting on ~4× parameters (269,722 → ~1.08M) without adding any regularization/augmentation (deliberately held constant) typically *widens* a generalization gap, not closes it. Worse, the budget is **fixed wall-clock, not fixed steps** — the baseline is already step-starved (never reaches its 2nd milestone at 48000; stops near 38,500). A 4× model in 300 s gets a fraction of those steps and can land *underfit and below baseline*. *Fix:* before committing the loop, run ~30 steps and measure realized img/s and projected epoch count; the binding risk here is step-collapse, not VRAM (330 MB → ~1.3 GB is a non-issue). If throughput only drops ~1.5–2× on the heavily-underutilized H20 (eager FP32, batch 128), the bet is live; if ~4×, it will underfit. This measurement is cheap and decisive and should be a gate, not an afterthought.

**2. [Idea 2 — understated change count] "Orthogonal capacity" bundles three changes, not one.** The summary frames this as pure capacity, but it swaps post-activation → **full pre-activation** (a residual/BN topology change, He 2016) *and* width 16→32 *and* the milestone schedule (idea's own text). A neutral/negative result won't distinguish arch topology from width from schedule. The idea concedes this ("Architecture and schedule still change together"). *Fix:* if the goal is the capacity lever specifically, hold post-activation and only widen (fewer confounds), or explicitly own pre-activation as an intended second lever. Credit where due: WD is correctly held at baseline `1e-4` — the exact confound the EXP-001 reviewer flagged (`001/01-idea-review.md` feedback #3) — so that lesson landed.

**3. [Ideas 1 & 3 — shared latent weakness] Both cut the high-LR plateau *below* the known-good baseline, i.e. they under-apply the fix the diagnosis prescribes.** The diagnosis is "restore high-LR exploration"; the *known-good* reference (baseline 91.67%) holds `lr=0.1` until its first drop at ~83% of realized steps (`32000/38525`). Idea 1 drops at 70%; Idea 3 begins cosine at 65%. Both sit *under* 83% — they interpolate toward the failed 15% point rather than anchoring at the value that already works. The free lunch both exploit (a terminal `1e-3`/`1e-4` phase the baseline never reaches) is real, but it is being paid for with exploration the diagnosis says is scarce. *Fix (cheap, applies to both):* anchor the plateau at ~78–82% (matching/slightly under baseline) and compress the decay tail into the remaining budget. This keeps the one component proven to reach 91.67% and *adds* the refinement phase on top, instead of betting the added tail outweighs lost exploration.

**4. [Idea 1 vs Idea 3 — attribution] Idea 3's cosine dominates Idea 1's hard 70% drop on the exploration/refinement profile.** A hard drop `0.1→0.01` at 70% abruptly ends exploration; a cosine from 65% keeps LR elevated (>0.05) through ~80% *and* delivers a graded tail with several thousand steps below `0.01`. So Idea 3 plausibly buys *more* effective exploration **and** more refinement than Idea 1 for the same budget — and its tail is already validated (EXP-001's cosine-to-`1e-4` produced a clean monotonic climb; the failure was the *start* at 15%, not the tail). Idea 1's countervailing merit — a flat, stable `1e-3` phase (10%) matching the discrete regime the baseline was tuned for — is legitimate but weaker, since EXP-001 showed the smooth tail works.

**5. [Idea 3 — minor] Two variables move vs EXP-001 (hold 15→65% *and* Nesterov removed).** Fine for the goal (only need +0.1pp), but if it succeeds you cannot separate hold-length from Nesterov-removal — and EXP-001's unexplored avenues explicitly pre-registered a *Nesterov-only* discriminator (`001/04-analysis.md`). Not disqualifying; just note the learning will be muddy. Idea 1 is cleaner here (standard momentum + baseline-style discrete schedule = closest single-lever test).

**6. [All three — non-issue, worth confirming] Timeout risk is now resolved.** EXP-001 ran 321.7 s total under the 600 s kill with persistent workers + 7 evals. All three retain those controls, so the timeout concern that dominated the EXP-001 review no longer binds — good. No action needed beyond keeping the bounded 7-eval schedule (do not revert to 99 per-epoch evals).

## Scored Verdict

**Long-Plateau Cosine, Standard Momentum (65% hold + cosine→1e-4)**
- Evidence/reasoning: **8/10** — repairs the *measured, quantified* EXP-001 limiter (early anneal at 15%) with a validated tail and standard momentum; only flaw is the 65% anchor sitting under the known-good 83%.
- Potential impact: **7/10** — reliably clears the bar via a terminal-refinement phase the baseline never reaches, while restoring most exploration; ceiling moderate.

**Baseline-Like Time-Fraction Milestones (70/90, standard momentum)**
- Evidence/reasoning: **7/10** — cleanest single-lever test, closest to the tuned discrete regime; but the 70% hard drop sacrifices more exploration than needed and the 0.001 gain may be near the 0.1pp noise floor.
- Potential impact: **6/10** — likely lands within ±0.2pp of baseline; the abrupt 70% cut caps its upside relative to the cosine.

**Wider PreAct ResNet, Eager FP32 (70/90 schedule)**
- Evidence/reasoning: **6/10** — capacity is a real CIFAR lever (WRN precedent), but the mechanism is in tension with the *generalization-gap* diagnosis (train loss already ~0.02), it bundles pre-activation + width + schedule, and the fixed-wall-clock step-collapse risk is under-mitigated.
- Potential impact: **8/10** — highest ceiling (+0.6–1.5pp) *if* the H20's spare throughput keeps step count high enough to realize the wider asymptote; genuine but conditional upside.

**Pick: Long-Plateau Cosine with Standard Momentum.** It wins on the strongest reasoning-to-risk ratio: it attacks the *exact defect EXP-001 measured* (annealing that started at 15% instead of after real exploration), reuses the one component EXP-001 already validated (a clean monotonic cosine tail to `1e-4`), removes the Nesterov confound, and is materially different from the failed run (65% vs 15% hold) — so it is not a blind retry. It dominates the milestone idea because cosine's shape delivers both more exploration (elevated LR through ~80%) and a richer refinement tail than a hard 70% drop, for the same budget. It beats the wider-ResNet bet not out of caution but because that bet's core mechanism (capacity) is misaligned with the stated limiter (generalization, train loss ~0) and rides an unmitigated fixed-wall-clock step-collapse risk that can push it *below* baseline; its high ceiling is real but conditional on a throughput measurement the idea hasn't yet made. If EXP-002 lands, the de-bundled wider model — gated on a throughput pre-check (feedback #1) — is the natural high-ceiling EXP-003. **Strongly recommended refinement for the pick:** move the cosine anchor from 65% up to ~78–80% (feedback #3) so the plateau matches the known-good baseline rather than interpolating toward the failed 15% point.
