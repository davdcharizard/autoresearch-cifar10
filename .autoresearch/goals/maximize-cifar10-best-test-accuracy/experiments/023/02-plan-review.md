## Adversarial concern list — EXP-023 plan (execution soundness)

### 1. The plan's own MAC estimate predicts a timing no-go before the hypothesis is ever tested
**Where:** Milestone 2 / Abort Criteria / VP step 5 (mean step ratio ≤1.345; brainstorm line 87, "1.456x MAC estimate sits near the allowed 1.345x mean ratio").
The 1.345 gate is not arbitrary — it is calibrated so the candidate still lands ~20,000 steps against baseline's 26,898 (26,898/1.345 ≈ 20,000). But the plan's own compute estimate is **1.456× MACs, which exceeds 1.345**. Because production charges `total_training_time` only against per-step compute (`dt` in `train.py:225–250` is measured *inside* `t0…synchronize`, excluding the dataloader wait between iterations), exposure is almost purely compute-bound — so step count scales roughly inversely with per-step compute. Unless width-3 FP32 convs at 48/96/192 are materially more efficient on H20 than their MAC ratio implies, the timing gate mathematically forces a no-go and the experiment terminates at Milestone 2 with **zero accuracy signal**. This is the dominant outcome risk; the whole bet rides on the gap between 1.456 (MACs) and 1.345 (allowed) being closed by kernel efficiency.

### 2. The safety-veto thresholds are calibrated for optimizer-path interventions, not an architecture swap
**Where:** Milestone 2 / Abort Criteria — "candidate terminal loss EMA ≤1.5× control," "normalized global gradient/update norms ≤2× control," ">95% prediction concentration."
These comparative gates come from the EXP-014/020/022 lineage (brainstorm lines 19–24), where candidate and control share the *same* parameterization and a divergent loss/gradient signature indicates cheating optimizer dynamics. Here the control is width-2 ResNet-20 and the candidate is width-3 **ResNet-14** — different depth, width, init scale, and 1.43× the parameters. Loss trajectory and gradient-norm magnitude legitimately differ across architectures over just 200 steps, so these thresholds are either vacuous or risk a **false abort that retires a sound architecture**. The plan compounds this by forbidding any "rescue" (Abort Criteria, line 56), so one spurious trip permanently kills the idea. Applying a "did the optimizer misbehave" gate to a pure capacity change is a category error; the plan gives no rationale for why 1.5×/2× are meaningful cross-architecture bounds.

### 3. The success margin is thin against two compounding headwinds
**Where:** Milestones 3–4, Hypothesis (plan line 26, `best_test_acc ≥94.25`).
Even in the best case where timing passes exactly at 1.345, the candidate gets ~20,000 steps vs baseline's 26,898 (−26% optimization) **and** loses one residual block per stage — an untested depth/receptive-field reduction the brainstorm itself flags (lines 81, 87, 103). To clear the bar it must overcome both simultaneously while beating baseline by only +0.10pp. EXP-007's +1.25pp width gain absorbed a 29% step loss, so it's plausible — but the required margin is razor-thin and a valid-but-miss is a likely outcome. The plan's diagnostic capture (switch/first-weak/NLL/exposure, Milestone 4) is the right mitigation, but the design should acknowledge that "pass timing, miss accuracy" is the modal success-of-timing path, not an edge case.

### 4. Controllers must build the control arm from explicit args, not train.py's mutated globals
**Where:** Milestone 2 (preflight/timing "fresh accepted and candidate arms"), VP steps 4–5.
By the time the controllers run, `train.py` has already been edited to `NUM_BLOCKS=2`/`WIDTH_MULTIPLIER=3` (Milestone 1). Since the controllers `import` train.py, its module-level constants and the module-level `cutmix`/`evaluator` (`train.py:20–35`) now reflect the *candidate* config. The "accepted control arm" (width-2 ResNet-20) must therefore be instantiated by explicitly calling `ResNet(3, 10, 2)` — if the controller instead re-instantiates from module constants or a default, it will silently compare **candidate-vs-candidate**, making every safety/timing ratio ≈1.0 and rendering both gates meaningless (a false pass). The plan never states how the control arm is constructed; this pitfall should be pinned down explicitly.

### 5. The "≤19 evaluation looks" ceiling is benchmarked to baseline epoch structure, not a principled invariant
**Where:** Milestone 3 / VP step 8 ("evaluation count ≤19," "no more than the accepted 19 looks").
Eval count is structural: one eval per epoch, plus every tail epoch (`train.py:283–302`), so it is a function of how many epochs fit in 300s — which depends on architecture speed. 19 is inherited from the accepted run, not an intrinsic bound. A slower candidate will almost certainly run *fewer* looks (so no metric-inflation risk here), but that also means the ceiling is not detecting anything for this experiment, and if the candidate ever completed a 20th legitimate epoch the gate would flag a non-existent violation. Lower priority, but the gate as written conflates "more looks than baseline" with "eval hacking" when the two are unrelated for an architecture change.
