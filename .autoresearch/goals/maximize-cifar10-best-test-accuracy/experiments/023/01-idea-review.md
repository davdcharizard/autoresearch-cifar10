# Adversarial Review — EXP-023 Candidate Ideas

Frontier is EXP-010 = 94.15%; acceptance gate = **≥94.25% on a single seed**. Note up front: a 0.10-point gate is ~10 test images out of 10k, which sits inside CIFAR-10 single-seed test noise (~±0.2–0.3%). This asymmetrically penalizes small-effect ideas and favors any candidate whose *expected* effect clearly exceeds noise. Keep that lens on all three.

## Prioritized concerns

**1. GeM-3: the strongest cited source argues against the actual intervention.** `mixed-pooling.md` (line 8–10) reports that *learned/adaptive* pooling beats a "universal fixed statistic." The proposal freezes `p=3`, untuned, with no local evidence — i.e., it adopts exactly the fixed-statistic form the paper says is inferior, while claiming the paper as support. The remaining support (GeM in image *retrieval*) is cross-domain, as `idea-02.md` itself concedes. *Fix:* either make `p` a single learnable scalar (identity-init at `p=1` so avg-pooling is the exact starting function, cleanly distinct from EXP-014's unbounded raw-max branch), or drop the mixed-pooling citation and argue purely from the salience mechanism.

**2. GeM-3 vs CutMix targets is a mechanism/limiter mismatch, not just a risk.** `idea-02.md` line 26 waves at this, but the conflict is structural: GeM up-weights the dominant salient region, while CutMix targets are *area-proportional* over two classes (`cutmix.md` line 8). During the 50% CutMix strong batches, the pooled feature emphasizes the majority region while the loss expects blended evidence — pushing against the very ingredient (EXP-010) that produced the frontier. *Fix:* if kept, apply GeM only in the weak (no-CutMix) tail, or gate its exponent by CutMix-vs-hard batch — though that adds a second mechanism and muddies attribution.

**3. Width-3 ResNet-14 confounds the one lever it has evidence for (width) with an untested one (depth cut).** EXP-007's +1.25 was width *at constant depth-20*; WRN's width>depth claim is at longer schedules/different recipe (`wide-residual-networks.md` line 10, 14). This proposal moves width 2→3 **and** depth 20→14 simultaneously, so the strong local evidence does not cover the risky half of the change (removing one block per stage = lost hierarchical/receptive-field refinement, `idea-03.md` line 39). *Mitigation to state:* this is genuinely a *net fixed-time* bet, which the proposal honestly frames; but the switch-fit and first-weak diagnostics (line 49) are the only way to separate "capacity saturated" from "depth loss" post hoc — make sure those are recorded even on a pass.

**4. Width-3's timing floor is the real go/no-go and it's on a knife-edge.** 1.456× MACs (`idea-03.md` line 23) against a 75.46%-backward, conv-bound step means step time likely scales ~1.35–1.45×, right at the proposal's own `1.345` weighted-mean gate and `20,000`-update floor (line 32). A plausible outcome is a valid-but-underoptimized run at ~20k updates with fewer eval looks — a structurally *unfair* test of the architecture, not a clean falsification. *Fix:* nothing to change in the plan (the gate is correctly conservative), but weight the verdict: a near-floor exposure landing should read as "inconclusive," not "depth-width refuted."

**5. ECA adds sequential kernels to the exact dominant cost center.** Three `layer3` gates each add a global-avg-pool reduction + `Conv1d` + sigmoid on the backward-critical path, which is already 75.46% of step time. The proposal's timing gate (`≤1.02` median, `idea-01.md` line 39) is extremely tight; three added launches per block may breach it purely on launch/reduction overhead, retiring the idea before it tests accuracy. This is not fatal (no bad run results) but materially lowers the probability the idea ever reaches the scored slot. *No fix needed* — just factor into expected value.

**6. ECA's effect ceiling likely sits below the acceptance margin.** `idea-01.md` line 47 admits "an effect smaller than the ten-example acceptance margin." ECA-Net gains are sub-0.5% at ImageNet scale (`eca-net.md` line 9) and typically shrink on a saturated width-2 CIFAR model near 94%. Combined with concern #1's noise floor, the most rigorous proposal here may pass every safety/identity gate and still fail the metric gate for reasons unrelated to whether the mechanism "worked." *Fix:* none available within the constraint; this is an inherent impact cap.

**7. ECA's 2·sigmoid gate can still drift into the suppression family late.** The identity-at-init argument (line 26) only covers step 0; gates ∈ (0,2) can learn toward 0 over 300s, recreating the residual-suppression pathology of EXP-012/015 in the back half where the safety corpus (200 steps) doesn't look. *Fix:* add a late-run gate-mean log (already partially present) and treat a layer3 gate-mean collapse < ~0.6 at the weak-tail as a diagnostic red flag even on a metric pass.

**Positive notes (not weaknesses):** GeM-3 has the cleanest integrity story (parameter-free, no RNG, no module — bitwise-identical init). ECA is the most carefully defended proposal against every prior failure family and correctly scopes to layer3 to dodge CutMix-controls-early-features. Width-3 FP32 is genuinely de-risked on safety by EXP-016 (FP32 control was stable; only BF16 collapsed) and is *not* a retry of any failed family.

## Scored verdict

| Idea | Evidence & reasoning | Potential impact |
|---|---|---|
| **Width-3 ResNet-14** | **7/10** — strongest *in-goal* lever (EXP-007 +1.25) + WRN + FP32 safety precedent (EXP-016); weakened by depth-cut confound and edge-of-floor timing. | **8/10** — only candidate with a demonstrated ≥0.2 lever in this exact goal; ceiling clears the noise floor. |
| **ECA layer3 recalibration** | **7/10** — most rigorous proposal, direct ResNet attention precedent, cleanly distinct from all failure families; but ImageNet-scale evidence, no CIFAR/short-budget validation. | **5/10** — channel-recal gains typically < the 0.10 gate on a saturated 94% model; author-admitted sub-margin ceiling. |
| **GeM-3 pooling** | **4/10** — cited mixed-pooling paper argues for *learned* not fixed pooling; retrieval-domain transfer; untuned p=3; CutMix-target mismatch. | **4/10** — speculative readout tweak with a fixed spatial prior; low, uncertain upside. |

## Pick: **FP32 Width-3 ResNet-14 Depth–Width Rebalance**

It wins on the combination of highest ceiling and best in-goal evidence for its core lever. EXP-007 is the only precedent in this entire history that moved the metric by more than a point, and it moved it via *exactly* the width axis this idea pushes further; EXP-016 already retired the safety objection for the FP32 case. Its two genuine weaknesses — the depth-reduction confound (concern #3) and the near-floor exposure risk (#4) — are honestly scoped in the proposal and, crucially, are *diagnosable*: switch-fit vs. 89.73% and realized-update count let the analyze phase attribute any miss. It is also the only candidate whose intended effect size sits above single-seed test noise, which matters directly given the 0.10 gate.

ECA is the better-*engineered* proposal and the most direct attack on the stated generalization/allocation bottleneck, but its expected effect is too likely to fall inside the acceptance margin *and* the noise floor — a rigorous idea that probably can't clear the gate is worth less than a slightly riskier one that can. GeM-3 is last: its headline citation undercuts its own fixed-exponent design, and it fights the CutMix ingredient that built the frontier.

If the team wants a hedge: run Width-3 first (highest EV); hold ECA as the fallback only after fixing the timing-gate margin, and shelve GeM-3 unless reformulated with a single learnable `p` initialized at 1.
