# Adversarial Review — EXP-014 Final-Aggregation Family

## Prioritized Feedback (most important first)

**1. [Cross-cutting] The diagnosis names *generalization/strong-fit* as the limiter, but all three ideas move a *readout* lever with no evidence that average pooling is what bounds it.** `02-system-understanding.md:17` states the accuracy bottleneck is "generalization under a short strong-view phase" and that a representation candidate "must preserve EXP-010's healthy 89.73% switch fit." The final-aggregation lever is listed only as an *open question* (`02-system-understanding.md:32`), never as a diagnosed cause. Two of the three ideas (Blend, GeM) emphasize *peak* responses, which is a form of strong-phase sharpening — the exact axis where CutMix-0.75 (EXP-011) and preactivation (EXP-012) already failed by crossing the 87.08 underfit marker. *Fix:* every finalist must predeclare the 80% switch-checkpoint it expects and treat any value below 87.08 as a fail, and should argue *why* it won't suppress strong fit — not just that it might help readout.

**2. [Blend — the idea-05 proposal — mechanistically weakest] Hard max is anti-aligned with the one piece of local evidence cited.** EXP-010's 0.60-point win came from CutMix with *area-proportional* labels (`03-experiment-learnings.md:133`, idea-05 §Local CutMix Mechanism). `adaptive_max_pool2d` is area-*insensitive*: a tiny donor patch producing one strong cell gets full weight despite a small soft-target. The proposal admits this (idea-05:32, 121) but keeps a 50% max weight anyway. Worse, the gradient math (idea-05:62-69) concentrates 65× the signal on one of 64 spatial cells — reducing pressure for spatial coverage, which is the wrong direction for a *generalization* bottleneck. *Fix:* if this family is pursued, prefer a statistic whose weight still scales with patch extent (GeM, or a softmax-weighted pool with moderate temperature) over hard max.

**3. [Blend & GeM] Uncontrolled feature-scale shift is a hidden confound that breaks the "clean readout test" claim.** Post-ReLU max ≫ mean, and GeM's `(mean(x³))^(1/3)` ≥ mean, so both raise classifier-input magnitude while keeping identical Kaiming init (idea-05:124 lists this as a risk). A gain or loss could then come from a de-facto logit/effective-LR change, not from the spatial statistic — undermining the whole "narrow attribution" pitch (idea-05:20-24). *Fix:* re-normalize the pooled feature to unit-ish scale (e.g., divide by a fixed constant, or LayerNorm-free rescale) so the test isolates the *statistic* from the *magnitude*, OR explicitly own the scale change as part of the method and drop the "clean attribution" framing.

**4. [Concat] Capacity + RNG-trajectory confound, and the proposal's own strongest objection.** Building `Linear(256,10)` changes fan-in init, consumes different RNG draws, and diverges every downstream loader-shuffle/worker seed (idea-05:20-24 argues exactly this against concat). A marginal pass then cannot be causally separated from capacity or a lucky stochastic trajectory — uncomfortable under the goal's explicit anti-seed-selection stance (`01-definition.md:18`). *Fix — high value:* zero-initialize the max-half of the classifier weights so the model *starts* output-identical to EXP-010's average-pool readout and only departs as it *learns* to use max features. This simultaneously (a) neutralizes the scale-shift confound, (b) gives a clean performance floor at the current frontier, and (c) makes any gain attributable to *learned* use of the max statistic. The loader-seed divergence remains and should be acknowledged, not hidden.

**5. [GeM] Evidence is indirect and p=3 is an unjustified operating point; it also carries the most kernel/numeric risk.** GeM's support is *image retrieval* (`01-brainstorm.md:8`), a different task/loss; no CIFAR-classification or ResNet-20 endpoint evidence. Fixed p=3 is asserted as "a common moderate operating point" with no local calibration, and p can't be tuned post-hoc (`01-brainstorm.md:56`). The clamp→pow→mean→pow chain is the most sequential-kernel-heavy of the three at the endpoint — the SE precedent (1.233× launch-bound, idea-05:58) is the cautionary analog. *Fix:* verify FP32 numerics for the epsilon clamp under CutMix probability targets, and state that p=3 degrades gracefully toward average (p→1 = control) so downside is bounded; run the same 97%-exposure paired-timing gate.

**6. [All three] The acceptance margin is dangerously thin relative to noise, and a similar-class change already got within 0.03 and failed.** The bar is 94.25 (baseline 94.15 + 0.10, `04-results.tsv:1`); EXP-012 reached 94.22 via a representation change yet *missed* the gate by 0.03 while weakening strong fit (`03-experiment-learnings.md:84`). The required delta is ~10 CIFAR examples on a single seed (idea-05:129) — none of these ideas has an evidentiary basis to expect materially more than the ~0.05-0.15 swings seen across EXP-011/012. This is not fatal, but it means the *ceiling* argument matters far more than the *point estimate*; a 94.30 point prediction (idea-05:18) is not credible precision.

**7. [Minor, all] Timing gate is correct and mandatory — keep it.** The one-endpoint reduction is genuinely cheaper than SE's nine, and 598.7 MB → ~610 MB has ample headroom (`02-system-understanding.md:22`). The 97%-exposure floor and paired fresh-process discipline (idea-05:88-101, `03-experiment-learnings.md:50`) are the right guard; no change needed. GeM is the one most likely to trip it.

None of the three is fatally disqualified (no hard-constraint violation, no reward-hack, no unchanged retry of a failed approach). All are legitimate but thin bets.

## Scored Verdict

| Idea | Evidence & Reasoning | Potential Impact |
|---|---|---|
| **Concatenated Avg-Max Classifier** | **7/10** — learned mixed pooling is the strongest AISTATS variant, and it's the *only* idea backed by *local* evidence that added capacity beats fixed-time cost (EXP-007 width win, `03-experiment-learnings.md:129`); weakened by capacity/RNG confound. | **7/10** — highest ceiling: can represent avg-only as a special case (floor at frontier if zero-init'd), can learn class-specific peak/extent balance; near-free 1,280 weights. |
| **Fixed GeM-3 Pooling** | **6/10** — principled continuum, parameter-free (cleanest attribution, no RNG/capacity confound), extent-sensitive so best-aligned with CutMix; but retrieval→classification transfer is indirect and p=3 arbitrary. | **5/10** — moderate; a fixed wrong p just degrades toward average, limited upside, no floor guarantee. |
| **Fixed Average-Max Blend (idea-05)** | **5/10** — most rigorously written and best attribution-controlled, but the thesis is mechanistically weakest: hard max is anti-CutMix and gradient-concentrating against the generalization bottleneck; scale-shift confound. | **5/10** — moderate; single-index emphasis risks hurting strong fit as much as helping. |

**Pick: Concatenated Average-Max Classifier.**

It wins on the two things that actually decide a thin-margin metric bet: **ceiling and floor.** Unlike the fixed Blend and GeM — which impose a *universal, untunable* peak-vs-extent coefficient chosen with no local calibration — the concat readout *learns* the balance per class and can recover current average-pool behavior as a limiting case, so its downside is bounded and its upside is the largest. Critically, it is the only candidate whose reasoning rests on *local* evidence (EXP-007's width win directly shows near-free capacity beats fixed-time cost in this exact recipe), and it is the safest on the diagnosed strong-fit constraint because it can learn to ignore the max statistic rather than being forced to over-weight peaks. Its one real weakness — the capacity/RNG attribution confound the proposal raises against it — is largely dissolved by the zero-init-the-max-half fix in feedback #4, which converts it into a clean strict-superset test with a frontier floor. GeM is the runner-up on cleaner attribution and better CutMix alignment; the Blend, despite being the most polished write-up, is the weakest thesis and should not win on presentation.

**Caveat carried into planning:** all three are thin bets against a 0.10 gate that a comparable change (EXP-012) already got within 0.03 of and failed — treat a bare pass as weak causal evidence and preserve the ≥87.08 strong-checkpoint / ≥97% exposure guards regardless of which is chosen.
