## Prioritized concerns

### 1. [idea-02] **Poly-1 with ε=1.0 is not a loss-geometry change — it is provably a per-example gradient rescale that nearly doubles the effective LR at convergence. Near-fatal as specified.**

For softmax CE, `d(-log p_t)/dz = (p − y)`. For the Poly-1 term `−ε·p_t`, `dp_t/dz_j = p_t(δ_jt − p_j)`, so `d(−ε·p_t)/dz = ε·p_t·(p − y)`. The total logit gradient is therefore exactly

```
(1 + ε·p_t) · (p − y)
```

Poly-1 does **not** change the gradient direction at all — it multiplies each example's gradient by a scalar in `[1, 1+ε]`. With ε=1.0 and late-training `p_t → 1`, that is a **~2× effective learning rate**, applied precisely over the clean SAM+EMA tail that produced EXP-011's 95.49 plateau. `idea-02.md`'s "Forbidden" list explicitly rules out LR rescaling, yet the mechanism *is* an LR rescale. The framing ("decision-boundary quality through loss geometry", `01-brainstorm.md:74`) is mechanically wrong, and the preregistered falsification ladder tests the wrong hypothesis.

It also up-weights already-confident examples (the inverse of focal loss) — the opposite of what "improve the hard boundary cases" would suggest.

**Remedy** (any one, preregistered): (a) set ε from a target inflation budget, e.g. ε=0.1 giving ≤10% effective-LR growth, and log measured `mean(1+ε·p_t)` per epoch as the mechanism counter; (b) apply Poly-1 only in the CutMix prefix (`progress < CUTMIX_END`) so the validated clean-tail convergence is untouched; (c) keep ε=1.0 but divide the loss by `mean(1+ε·p_t).detach()` to isolate the reweighting from the scale change — that is the actual "geometry-only" test.

*Silver lining, and credit where due*: because `sam_perturb` normalizes by `grad_norm` (`train.py:186–196`), a roughly uniform scale factor **cancels** in the SAM perturbation. So the proposal's self-declared "may change SAM perturbation geometry unfavorably" risk (`01-brainstorm.md:82`) is largely unfounded — ‖ε_SAM‖ stays 0.05 and this is *not* an EXP-007 repeat.

### 2. [idea-02] **ε=1.0 has no source.** `papers/polyloss.md:9,17` states twice that the optimal coefficient is *task-dependent* and never reports a value. `01-brainstorm.md:76` says "preregister the paper's simple coefficient", but line 16 of the packet supplies only the *form* `CE + ε₁(1−p_t)`, not a number. Combined with the proposal's binding no-sweep commitment, the experiment locks in an unjustified constant and cannot recover. **Remedy**: derive ε from the gradient-inflation budget in #1 — that is an accuracy-blind, mechanistic rationale, exactly what the packet's Relevance line asks for.

### 3. [idea-01] **The proposed mask is not the operator that produced 3.89% → 3.08%; it is strictly more aggressive and heavily center-biased.** `idea-01.md` draws `top ~ U[0,16]` so the 16×16 square is *always fully contained*. Reference CIFAR Cutout draws the center uniformly over the image and clips. Consequences, computed from the proposal's own mask bank:
- Masking probability for corner pixel (0,0) = `1/289 ≈ 0.35%`; for center pixel (16,16) = `(16/17)² ≈ 88.6%`. A 250× spatial bias.
- Masked area is a fixed 256 px (25%) vs the clipped operator's expected `14² = 196 px` (19.1%) — **31% more occlusion**, all of it on the object-bearing center of a center-biased dataset.

So the cited effect size is being transferred from a different operator. **Remedy**: draw `top ~ U[−15, 31]` (47 values/axis) and build a 2,209-entry bank — `2209 × 1024` bool = 2.3 MB, nothing against the 1,222 MiB / 97,871 MiB headroom — then log realized mean masked fraction and confirm it lands near 19%. This costs one line and makes the RICAP citation actually apply.

### 4. [idea-03] **The SWA packet's own caveat — BatchNorm — is unaddressed, and idea-03 makes it worse by design.** `stochastic-weight-averaging.md:11` says explicitly: "verify that the averaging window is not effectively identical to the final weights **and handle BatchNorm state explicitly**"; line 16 lists "BatchNorm-statistics management for the averaged model" as a key technique. EXP-011's EMA is full-state, so BN running stats are EMA'd alongside parameters. The parent got away with this because live/EMA distance was only 1.51%; idea-03 *predicts pushing that to 1.5–5%* — i.e. deliberately widening the exact gap that makes averaged BN statistics stale relative to averaged weights. SWA's answer is a BN re-estimation pass, which costs charged forwards and is ruled out by `02-system-understanding.md:20`. **Remedy**: preregister per-checkpoint EMA-vs-live accuracy delta as the primary mechanism counter (not just parameter distance), and abort-criterion on EMA underperforming live — that at least detects the failure instead of reading it as "the idea didn't work."

### 5. [idea-03] **Variance injection into the tail mechanically inflates a max-selected metric — the closest thing here to reward hacking.** `best_test_acc` is the max over ~133 evaluations. Raising late LR raises checkpoint-to-checkpoint spread across the 16-checkpoint tail, which raises `E[max]` without raising `E[mean]`. `02-system-understanding.md:15` sets the standard the idea would violate: a credible child needs "a plausible stable effect near 0.25-0.30 points, not a micro-change that merely shifts the selected maximum." The idea's central mechanism *is* diversity — i.e. variance. Partial credit: the proposal does preregister final-16 EMA mean ≥95.65, which is the right guard. But this must be the *primary* criterion, not a secondary one, or the result is uninterpretable. **Remedy**: make the tail-mean the pass criterion and treat the formal best as reporting only.

### 6. [idea-03] **"EMA-aligned" is decorative: only ~1.8 of 4 cycles fire.** By the proposal's own arithmetic, `max()` selects the parent schedule until crossover at progress ≈0.887525, leaving ~33.74 s of a 75 s tail active. The 18.75 s period equals `EMA_HALF_LIFE_S` (`train.py:42`) only in the *nominal* window; in the active window the EMA's 18.75 s half-life covers roughly one period, so the EMA preferentially samples whichever cycle phase the run happens to end in — a throughput-dependent lottery, given the goal's own finding that identical-intent runs land at 25,560/25,644 steps (`03-experiment-learnings.md:47`). **Remedy**: set `LATE_LR_START = 0.8875` (the actual crossover) and `LATE_LR_CYCLES = 2` so every declared cycle is genuinely active and the period genuinely equals the half-life. As written the alignment claim in `01-brainstorm.md:46` is not true of the executed schedule.

### 7. [idea-03] **The family is rated low-confidence by the base node's own analysis.** `011/04-analysis.md` Next Steps rank "Test an averaging-horizon variant only with a strong mechanistic rationale" at **low confidence** and "Add a low-overhead representation or calibration mechanism" at **medium confidence**; `00-navigate.md:5` states the search intent as exactly the latter. idea-03 also rests on an unverified premise — that 1.51% live/EMA distance is *too small* — for which no evidence exists anywhere in the tree. **Remedy**: if pursued at all, first justify the target distance from `how-to-scale-your-ema.md:15` ("express EMA through an effective time constant") rather than from the raw distance number.

### 8. [idea-01] **The RICAP table has no CutMix+Cutout row — marginal value over an existing occlusion mechanism is unestablished.** `papers/ricap.md:9` reports 3.89% → 3.08% for Cutout against a plain WideResNet baseline. The parent already applies CutMix on 10,345 batches, which is itself spatial occlusion (with label mixing). The 0.81-point transfer is an upper bound, likely substantially redundant. The packet's own line 12 flags the differentiation argument as a *hypothesis*, not evidence. **Remedy**: state the honest expected effect as a fraction of 0.81 in the hypothesis, and make the complementary-gating claim testable by logging the counters the proposal already defines (10,512 Cutout / 10,345 CutMix / 0 clean early) so a null result is attributable.

### 9. [idea-01] **Zero clean early batches — the diagnosis never established that the parent is overfitting.** 20,857 − 10,345 = 10,512 means every early batch gets either CutMix or Cutout, on top of `RandomCrop(32,4)` + flip + drop-path 0.08. Under a fixed 300 s budget (~132 natural epochs), added regularization can underfit rather than generalize; nothing in `02-system-understanding.md` or `011/04-analysis.md` reports a train/test gap. Note the parent's final *train* loss 0.1552 is measured on the clean-tail phase, so it does not settle this. **Remedy**: log train-vs-test gap at a few tail epochs as a diagnostic (free — it uses existing evals), and if a hedge is wanted, gate Cutout at probability 0.5 within the non-CutMix branch (≈5,256 batches, ~20% of steps, preserving ~20% clean early supervision). The full-dose version is defensible — literature Cutout is applied to 100% of images and this is less — but it should be an *argued* choice, not an unnoticed one.

### 10. [idea-01] **Concrete API defect in the timed hot path.** `torch.randint(0, 17, positions.shape, out=positions, device=inputs.device, generator=generator)` passes both `out=` and `device=`; factory functions conflict on tensor-option merging when `out=` is supplied. This will fail or warn on the very first Cutout batch. **Remedy**: drop `device=` (the out tensor already fixes device), or use `positions.random_(0, 17, generator=generator)`. Verify in the preflight, not the timed run.

### 11. [idea-01] **The mean-fill claim is correct, and correct for a fragile reason.** `train.py:681` sets `std=(1,1,1)`, so the normalized tensor is `x − mean` and `inputs.mul_(mask)` is *exactly* dataset-mean fill in pixel space. This is a genuine strength and the proposal earned it. But nothing in the code documents the dependency — if std ever changes, Cutout silently becomes zero-fill (a different, worse operator; cf. `papers/ricap.md:9`, unstructured input dropout degrading to 4.69%). **Remedy**: one `assert std == (1, 1, 1)` next to the helper, or compute the fill value from `mean/std` explicitly.

### 12. [idea-01] **Latency gate is loose relative to the predicted cost.** The proposal's gate is weighted median ≤1.03× parent. The added work is ~4 tiny kernels on 40.7% of a 10 ms step — expected well under 0.5%. A 3% allowance silently permits losing ~770 steps, and `02-system-understanding.md:21` (EXP-005) shows step count is not the only thing that matters but is not free either. **Remedy**: tighten to ≤1.01×; if the measurement exceeds it, the kernels are misbehaving and you want to know before the run, not after.

### 13. [idea-02] **Integration defect**: `poly1_loss` returns a 4-tuple, but `train.py:888` does `train_loss_f = loss.item()`. Both call sites (`train.py:819–831`, `848–855`) need unpacking or the run dies at first logging. Minor but a guaranteed crash.

### 14. [cross-cutting] **All three shift step counts and can trip a post-summary hard raise.** `train.py:945–948` raises on `live_evals + ema_evals != epoch`, and `985–986` re-raises the final audit. The summary prints first, but the process exits non-zero — which fails the goal's "no crash" verification condition even though the metric is on screen. The `|ordinary − sam| ≤ 1` parity relies on cadence 31 being coprime to `SAM_PERIOD=2` (`train.py:172`, `375`) and survives all three ideas, but nobody should discover this at minute nine. **Remedy**: assert the audit invariants in the preflight harness, and transcribe metrics durably before any deletion (`03-experiment-learnings.md:37`).

### 15. [cross-cutting] **Every target is set against the wrong baseline number.** All three preregister ≥95.71, but the stable tail is 95.493 (`011/04-analysis.md`), so the required *stable* effect is ~+0.22, not +0.10. Only idea-01 has a cited effect size of that magnitude — and only before redundancy discounting (#8). All three should state the required stable delta explicitly so a 95.72-with-95.50-tail outcome is read as "no mechanism", per the EXP-011 learning at `03-experiment-learnings.md:40–42`.

**No hard-constraint or scope violations found in any of the three.** All modify only `train.py`, preserve `TIME_BUDGET_S`, add no dependencies, keep one eval per epoch, and do not touch seeds for selection purposes. None is an unchanged retry of a failed approach — notably, idea-01 is the *remedy the learnings file itself prescribes* (`03-experiment-learnings.md:75`: "prefer single-view GPU augmentation"), not an EXP-008 repeat.

### Pick: **CutMix-Complementary GPU Cutout (idea-01)**

It is the only candidate whose claimed mechanism, cited effect size, and diagnosed limiter line up. It attacks representation-level salient-feature overreliance — precisely the "low-overhead representation or calibration mechanism" that `00-navigate.md:5` set as the search intent and that `011/04-analysis.md` rates medium confidence — while adding no second view, no extra forward, no CPU loader work, and no change to CutMix, SAM, or EMA dose. It is additive rather than substitutive (EXP-006), single-view GPU-side (the explicit EXP-008 remedy), and RNG-isolated in a way I verified against the actual global-stream drop-path draw. Its ~0.81 pp literature effect is the only one in the set of the right order of magnitude for a +0.22 *stable* requirement.

It wins over idea-02 because idea-02's mechanism, once the algebra is done, is an effective-LR increase in disguise with an unsourced coefficient and a falsification ladder aimed at the wrong hypothesis — fix #1 and #2 and it becomes a strong candidate for EXP-013, but not as currently written. It wins over idea-03 because idea-03 asks for a variance increase in a max-selected metric while leaving SWA's own BatchNorm precondition unhandled, and because two-thirds of its headline mechanism never executes.

**Conditions on the pick** — fix before planning: mask geometry to `U[−15,31]` with clipping (#3), the `randint(out=, device=)` defect (#10), latency gate to 1.01× (#12), a `std==(1,1,1)` assertion (#11), and restate the hypothesis against the 95.493 stable tail with an honest sub-0.81 expected effect (#8, #15). Decide the clean-early-batch question deliberately (#9) — full dose is defensible, but say so.
