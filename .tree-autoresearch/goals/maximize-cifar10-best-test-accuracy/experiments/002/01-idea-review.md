# EXP-002 Adversarial Idea Review

## Prioritized Feedback

1. **Sparse EMA Checkpoint Smoothing: the stated limiter is not demonstrated, and the evaluation policy can lower the primary metric.** EXP-001's 94.62% best versus 94.52% final result is one adjacent-epoch observation, not evidence that parameter noise caused the gap (`experiments/001/04-analysis.md`, Results). More importantly, the objective already keeps the best of every epoch, while this proposal makes all online checkpoints after EMA activation invisible. EMA could smooth away a genuine online peak or lag late low-LR improvement, so stabilization need not improve `best_test_acc`. Refine the hypothesis to weight-space ensembling improving generalization, not recovering a 0.10-point best/final gap; cite direct EMA/SWA evidence before selecting it. If tested, predeclare a late activation point, initialize EMA from the online weights at that point, and evaluate a single predetermined model per epoch so cadence remains compliant.

2. **Sparse EMA Checkpoint Smoothing: copying online BatchNorm buffers into averaged weights is a mechanism mismatch.** The proposal acknowledges BatchNorm risk but prescribes online running means/variances for EMA parameters (`01-brainstorm.md`, Sparse EMA summary). Those buffers were accumulated under the changing online network and are not guaranteed to calibrate the averaged weights. This can erase the modest expected gain, especially because `Eval.evaluate` simply evaluates the supplied model and cannot be changed. Prefer averaging floating-point parameters and BatchNorm running buffers with the same sparse EMA, while copying integer counters; alternatively perform a predetermined BN recalibration using training data outside validation, but charge its work to the fixed training budget and specify it up front. Validate state-dict coverage and keep the update before the existing synchronization so its GPU cost is included by `train.py:237-265`.

3. **Early Cutout with Light Label Smoothing: the intervention is underspecified and bundles two unsupported levers.** No mask side length, fill value, per-image versus per-batch policy, or implementation location is defined, so the effective corruption and overhead cannot be reviewed. The only cited paper packet supports early regularization timing, not Cutout or label smoothing, and EXP-001's near-zero training loss does not by itself establish pathological confidence (`experiments/001/04-analysis.md`, Results). Label smoothing also remains active in the nominally clean final quarter, contrary to the cited recommendation to relax regularization late (`knowledge/papers/time-matters-regularization.md`, Relevance). Refine this into one attributable change or supply direct evidence for the combination; specify a CIFAR-appropriate square size, use mean-color fill (zero is mean-color after the normalization at `train.py:174-184`), vectorize per-image masks, and either disable both mechanisms at 75% progress or justify why label smoothing should persist.

4. **Front-Loaded Probabilistic CutMix: strongest limiter match, but the exact recipe is more heuristic than the citations imply.** The history supports a generalization intervention: EXP-001 reached near-zero late training loss and 94.62% test accuracy, and its own analysis names front-loaded CutMix as the high-confidence next step (`experiments/001/04-analysis.md`, Results and Next Steps). The mixed-sample paper supports CutMix as an input-gradient regularizer, and the timing paper supports early application, but neither distillation establishes the specific 0.5 gate, `Beta(1,1)`, or 75% cutoff. These are reasonable fixed hypotheses, not evidence-backed optima; state them as such and avoid accuracy-driven adjustment after the run.

5. **Front-Loaded Probabilistic CutMix: stochastic clean/mixed interleaving is not equivalent to RegMixup's simultaneous clean-plus-mixed objective.** RegMixup retains clean supervision in the same optimization objective, whereas a Bernoulli gate supplies either clean or mixed supervision at each step (`experiments/001/papers/regmixup.md`, Key Contributions). SGD over alternating objectives may approximate an expectation, but it does not preserve the same per-step anchor and has different gradient variance. Keep the one-forward-pass interleaving because the fixed 300-second budget makes the second forward costly, but describe RegMixup only as motivation. Record the realized number of mixed batches so the intended exposure is auditable without using the test result to tune it.

6. **Front-Loaded Probabilistic CutMix: implementation correctness and charged-time throughput are the main concrete failure modes.** `train.py` currently transfers channels-last inputs at lines 244-249 and times all training work through the CUDA synchronization at lines 237-265. Implement the permutation and rectangle on-device after transfer, use a copied patch or safe indexing so source pixels are not overwritten, compute the effective label weight from the clipped rectangle area, and apply `lam * CE(y_a) + (1-lam) * CE(y_b)` from the same logits. A Python loop per image or an extra forward pass would reduce the current 28,790-step exposure under the fixed budget. A single shared rectangle per batch is a defensible low-overhead choice if declared explicitly. Preserve the time-based cutoff and existing schedule so any small throughput change does not shift phases.

7. **Cross-candidate constraint and reward-hacking check.** None of the three ideas is inherently a hard-constraint violation: all can be implemented only in `train.py`, require no dependency, preserve seed 42, and can evaluate once per epoch. CutMix and Cutout are genuine training changes; EMA is a genuine deployable model transform, not evaluator manipulation. The implementation must retain `CUDA_VISIBLE_DEVICES=0` at launch and the frozen `Eval`. Do not choose hyperparameters from intermediate test accuracy or rerun a seed after failure, because the per-epoch CIFAR-10 test evaluations already create overfit pressure. Predeclare the complete recipe and execute once.

## Scored Verdict

### Front-Loaded Probabilistic CutMix

- **Strength of evidence and reasoning: 8/10.** It is directly motivated by EXP-001's measured fit/generalization gap and by two relevant mixed-sample and critical-period mechanisms; the exact probability, alpha, and cutoff remain heuristic, and the RegMixup analogy is imperfect.
- **Potential impact: 8/10.** It can materially change the learned invariances at essentially one-forward-pass cost and has more headroom than merely smoothing checkpoints, though interaction with crop/flip and existing drop path could cause underfitting.

### Sparse EMA Checkpoint Smoothing

- **Strength of evidence and reasoning: 4/10.** The implementation is plausible and cheap, but there is no direct literature support in the packet, a single 0.10-point best/final gap does not identify checkpoint noise, and the proposed BatchNorm handling is inconsistent.
- **Potential impact: 4/10.** EMA may improve generalization, but the proposal itself expects limited upside and evaluating only EMA can discard online peaks from the `best_test_acc` objective.

### Early Cutout with Light Label Smoothing

- **Strength of evidence and reasoning: 4/10.** The general overfitting diagnosis is relevant, but the cited evidence does not establish either component, critical implementation parameters are absent, and two regularizers are bundled without an interaction argument.
- **Potential impact: 6/10.** Input occlusion plus confidence regularization could raise accuracy more than EMA if calibrated well, but redundant regularization with crop/flip and drop path creates a credible underfitting risk in the short fixed-budget run.

## Strongest Idea

**Pick: Front-Loaded Probabilistic CutMix.** It wins because it is the only finalist with both a direct mechanism-to-limiter match and specific external support: EXP-001 demonstrably has optimization capacity left but a large train/test gap, mixed-sample augmentation targets input-level generalization, and early removal preserves the existing low-LR clean refinement. Its weaknesses are refinable implementation and calibration issues rather than a broken objective argument. Keep the recipe fixed at the proposed 0.5 gate, `Beta(1,1)`, and 75% cutoff for this experiment, describe those values as hypotheses, use area-corrected on-device CutMix with one forward pass, and log realized mixed-batch exposure.
