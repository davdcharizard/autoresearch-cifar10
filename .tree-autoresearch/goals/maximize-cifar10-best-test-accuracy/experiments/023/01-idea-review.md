# Adversarial Review — EXP-023 Candidates (base: EXP-001 @ 94.62%, local gate 94.72%)

## Prioritized Feedback

**1. (All candidates) The fork's exit strategy is unstated, and it determines whether a "local improvement" is worth anything.** Any child of EXP-001 that lands between 94.72% and 95.23% earns an `improvement` verdict yet is strictly dominated by EXP-002 on the same envelope — the global best (95.61%, EXP-011) doesn't move. The fork is only justified because SAM (+0.17, EXP-004) and EMA (+0.21, EXP-011) are mechanism-portable add-ons that could be re-stacked on a new branch head. The brainstorm never says this. Fix: preregister the branch decision rule — the new mechanism must beat not just 94.72% but plausibly EXP-002's 95.23% to warrant re-stacking; below that, record the head-to-head learning and abandon the branch. Without this, a 94.9% result reads as threshold-gaming rather than progress toward the goal metric.

**2. (Mixup) Dose-matching vs. best-known-config tension is unacknowledged.** Standard Mixup on CIFAR applies α=1 to *every* batch; the p=0.5/first-75% envelope copies EXP-002 for attribution but halves the canonical dose. That is the right call for a controlled CutMix-vs-Mixup comparison, but it should be stated as a deliberate trade — if Mixup underperforms, the result discredits *dose-matched front-loaded* Mixup, not Mixup. Also, the risk section worries about "dense elementwise work" latency; interpolating a 256×3×32×32 batch is ~1 MiB of elementwise math against a 10 ms step — the paired latency check is fine to keep but this risk is overstated. The real risk is effect size, and the brainstorm should say so plainly: published CIFAR-10 WRN results put Mixup at roughly CutMix's level or slightly below, so the head-to-head is closer to a coin flip than an expected win.

**3. (Label smoothing) The lineage's own evidence against confidence-softening is ignored.** EXP-015 (Poly-1 confidence attenuation) lost 0.27 points and EXP-013 (cosine classifier) lost 0.50 on EXP-011. Those ran atop CutMix's soft targets, so they aren't identical to LS on clean EXP-001 — but the brainstorm cites neither, and the burden is on it to explain why target-softening will succeed here after two related failures elsewhere in the tree. Compounding this, the candidate's own risk section concedes the effect "may be below the 0.10-point gate," and Müller et al.'s gains concentrate on CIFAR-100/ImageNet where baseline error is high; at ~5.4% error the plausible LS delta is at or below the gate. A candidate whose author expects it to miss the necessary condition is a weak use of a run slot. If pursued at all, it belongs as a rider on a stronger candidate's future branch, not a branch founder.

**4. (RICAP) It undercuts the fork's stated rationale and its marginal upside sits inside the noise floor.** The diagnosis demands "a mechanism distinct from CutMix." RICAP is patch replacement with area-weighted labels — the same mixed-sample/patch family the unified analysis groups with CutMix; four sources instead of two is a dose/geometry variant, not a distinct mechanism. Its literature edge over CutMix-class methods on WRN is a few hundredths of a point of error, while EXP-003 showed this pipeline cannot even confirm a CutMix dose change against run variance (~0.1–0.15 points of tail spread per EXP-011's 0.17-point tail). High implementation effort (four crops, four loss terms, clipping/area audits — the candidate's own risk list) buys an expected effect indistinguishable from EXP-002. Additionally, Beta(0.3,0.3) at p=0.5 front-loaded is doubly off-manifold from the paper (which applies RICAP to all batches, full run) — the envelope-transfer assumption is untested and unmentioned.

**5. (Mixup, minor) EXP-006 is correctly distinguished but under-used.** The brainstorm rightly notes manifold mixup ≠ input Mixup. It should also harvest EXP-006's one relevant warning: hidden interpolation *worsened final test loss* when swapped against CutMix — a hint that interpolation-style mixing may trade off against patch-style mixing's area-adjusted label pressure in this recipe. A preregistered secondary check on final test loss (not just accuracy) would make the Mixup-vs-CutMix comparison more informative either way.

**6. (Housekeeping) Lineage claims check out.** EXP-001's only child being EXP-002, the 94.72% gate, the EXP-012 Cutout under-dose, and the closed-directions list (widths 288/320 = EXP-022/014 latency rejects; FP32 gates = EXP-009; dual-view CPU loader = EXP-008) all match the TSV and analyses. No hard-constraint or scope violations in any candidate; all keep one backbone forward, GPU-side batch ops, and fixed seeds. No fatal flags.

## Scored Verdict

**Front-Loaded Probabilistic Mixup**
- Evidence/reasoning: **8/10** — reuses EXP-002's validated envelope, dose, RNG-isolation, and dual-CE machinery nearly verbatim, and the NeurIPS'22 unified analysis plus Golatkar et al. (which studied Mixup's early-phase criticality directly) give it the best-grounded mechanism argument of the three.
- Potential impact: **6.5/10** — if it beats 95.23% it founds a genuinely distinct branch with proven +0.38 of portable SAM/EMA headroom behind it; if it loses, the dose-matched head-to-head is cheap, clean information the tree explicitly lacks.

**Time-Decayed Label Smoothing**
- Evidence/reasoning: **5/10** — paper support is real but concentrated on harder benchmarks, and it ignores the tree's two failed confidence-softening experiments (EXP-013, EXP-015).
- Potential impact: **3/10** — its own risk section predicts an effect below the 0.10-point gate, and even a pass yields a low-ceiling branch whose mechanism overlaps (per the cited paper itself) with the mixed-sample soft targets any competitive branch will need.

**Early Four-Patch RICAP**
- Evidence/reasoning: **6/10** — direct CIFAR/WRN table evidence, but the p=0.5 front-loaded envelope is doubly off-paper and unaddressed, and its "materially different geometry" claim strains against the fork's distinct-mechanism requirement.
- Potential impact: **5/10** — expected effect ≈ CutMix's, i.e., a wash against EXP-002 within this pipeline's demonstrated noise floor, at the highest implementation risk of the three.

## Selection

**Winner: Front-Loaded Probabilistic Mixup.** It is the only candidate that simultaneously (a) attacks the diagnosed generalization gap with a mechanism the unified analysis certifies as genuinely distinct from the incumbent CutMix branch, (b) inherits an already-validated envelope and near-complete code path from EXP-002, minimizing correctness risk, and (c) produces decision-relevant information under *both* outcomes — a win founds a re-stackable branch, a loss closes the whole-image-interpolation question the tree left open since EXP-002's own next-steps. RICAP costs more to build for an expected effect the pipeline cannot distinguish from CutMix; label smoothing is expected by its own author to miss the gate. Condition the selection on feedback item 1: preregister that the branch is only continued (SAM/EMA re-stacking) if Mixup exceeds EXP-002's 95.23%, and add final-test-loss as a secondary comparison metric per item 5.
