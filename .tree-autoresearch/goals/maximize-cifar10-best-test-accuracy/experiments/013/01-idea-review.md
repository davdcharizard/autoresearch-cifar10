# Adversarial Review — EXP-013 Candidates

## Prioritized feedback

**1. [Poly-1] The proposal is self-admittedly underpowered for the diagnosed limiter — this is close to fatal for its expected value.** The system understanding (`02-system-understanding.md`) says a credible child "needs a plausible stable effect near 0.25–0.30 points, not a micro-change." The proposal's own preregistered expectation is "+0.05–0.20 points; a stable +0.25 is an upside case rather than the median expectation" (`proposals/idea-01.md`, Hypothesis section). Its median case fails even the +0.10 formal threshold. The stability-derived ε=0.25 is accuracy-blind by design, but the PolyLoss paper explicitly says the coefficient is task-dependent and its reported gains used tuned, typically larger ε. An "engineering bound" divorced from any accuracy evidence is not conservatism — it is choosing a dose with no reason to believe it's therapeutic. **Fix:** either justify ε from the paper's nearest reported operating point (accepting transfer risk honestly), or accept this is a low-power probe and stop framing it as a plateau-lifting candidate.

**2. [Poly-1] In the regime where it matters, the mechanism degenerates toward a uniform LR bump — the causal story is weaker than written.** During the first 75%, high LR and CutMix keep `p_t` low, so the multiplier sits near 1.0–1.1 (the proposal concedes this at `p_t=0.1` → 1.025). In the clean tail, most training examples have `p_y → 1`, so the multiplier saturates near a *uniform* ×1.25 — approximately a 25% effective-LR increase on the tail, not "confidence-weighted decision-boundary refinement." **Fix:** the audit already logs multiplier min/mean/max per phase; preregister an interpretation rule — if tail multiplier dispersion is small (e.g., p10–p90 within ~0.05), any result must be attributed to effective-LR scaling, not example reweighting.

**3. [EMA horizon] The lag diagnosis is not just unverified — the parent's own data pattern leans against it.** If the shadow were lagging a still-improving online tail, later EMA checkpoints (weighting recent states more) should trend up; instead epochs 118–133 are flat around 95.49 and *end* at 95.46, below the mean (`experiments/011/04-analysis.md`). That is more consistent with a plateaued/noisy online tail than with lag, in which case shortening the half-life from 18.75s to 12.5s removes smoothing (ESS 115→77) and buys variance, not signal. The proposal lists this as a risk but still builds the whole bet on the weaker interpretation. EXP-011's own next-steps rated a horizon variant "low confidence" absent a strong mechanistic rationale; none has been added since. **Fix:** the identification gap could be attacked directly — e.g., alternating live/EMA evaluation sources across tail epochs in one run (protocol permits one eval per epoch; only the *source* alternates) would measure the live-tail level and settle lag-vs-variance, at the cost of halving EMA tail checkpoints. As written, this is a coin flip on an unidentified mechanism.

**4. [EMA horizon] Expected effect sits inside known noise — it is exactly the "micro-change that shifts the selected maximum" the diagnosis warns against.** The proposal's own prediction band is 95.62–95.76, straddling the 95.71 threshold, while historical max/tail variation reaches 0.29 points and the EXP-011 tail alone spans 0.17. "Six half-lives fit exactly in the 75-second tail" is numerology, not evidence. Honest and cheap, but low information either way: a pass could be noise and a fail wouldn't reject the horizon family.

**5. [Cosine classifier] The +0.29 anchor carries winner's-curse selection bias — treat the transferred effect as smaller than cited.** The ICCV paper reports fixed scales 1–60 ranging "from failed optimization to competitive accuracy," and its own learned-scale method (95.49) *lost* to baseline (95.56) (`papers/temperature-cosine-softmax.md`). That means scale 40's 95.85 is plausibly the argmax of the paper's own sweep — importing it is inheriting a max-selected effect. The proposal calls 40 a "literature-anchored midpoint," which overstates it. **Fix:** keep scale 40 (it is still the only evidence-anchored point available), but revise the expected-gain band downward from "+0.19 to +0.39" and make the falsification bins reflect that a smaller true effect is the base case.

**6. [Cosine classifier] Unaddressed geometry mismatch: feature dimension 256 vs the paper's ResNet-34 (512-d).** Initial cosine spread scales as `1/sqrt(d)`; at d=256 the proposal's own estimate gives logit std ≈ 2.5, versus ≈ 1.77 in the paper's setting at the same scale 40. The transferred operating point is effectively *sharper* here than in the cited evidence, compounding the CutMix-vs-sharp-softmax risk in the first 75%. Also note the evidence regime mismatch: the paper trains under an epoch budget; this goal is a hard 300-second wall clock, so any early-optimization slowdown from sharp mixed-target softmax is paid in lost effective training, not just slower curves. **Fix:** acknowledge the dimension mismatch explicitly in the proposal; add an early-progress audit (e.g., training-loss trajectory checkpoint vs parent at 25%/50% progress) so a slow-start failure mode is distinguishable from a flat-transfer failure post hoc.

**7. [Cosine classifier] Weight-decay radial dynamic is named but unmanaged.** With logits invariant to row norm, WD shrinks `fc.weight` norms monotonically while gradients are tangent — effective classifier LR grows as norm falls, and EMA then averages raw (shrinking) vectors. Over ~26k steps this is probably mild, but the proposal only logs *final* row norms. **Fix:** log row-norm min/mean/max per epoch (free, already synchronized at eval boundaries) so a norm-collapse trajectory is observable; preregister no mitigation, just observability.

**8. [All three] Implementation contracts check out against `train.py` — no fatal integration flaws found.** I verified: the cosine bias freeze is compatible with the `requires_grad` filter feeding `sam_parameters` (train.py:732) and with `sam_perturb`'s all-gradients assertion (train.py:177–179); the Poly-1 helper's separate FP32 softmax composes correctly with autograd and consumes no RNG; the EMA change is genuinely one constant plus the startup assertion at train.py:677 (which must be updated — idea-02 covers this). No hard-constraint violations, no frozen-protocol tampering, no retreads of the rejected approaches (EXP-012's spatial erasure, overlap batches, etc.) in any candidate.

## Scored verdict

Scale: /10.

| Idea | Evidence & reasoning | Potential impact |
|---|---|---|
| Bounded soft-target Poly-1 | **6** — exact gradient algebra and audit design are exemplary, but the coefficient has zero accuracy evidence and the mechanism degenerates to ~uniform tail LR scaling (points 1–2). | **3** — its own median expectation (+0.05–0.20) fails the +0.10 threshold's stable-effect intent; ceiling is modest even if everything works. |
| Six-half-life responsive EMA | **5** — impeccably specified and cheapest to run, but built on a lag hypothesis the parent's own tail pattern leans against (point 3). | **3** — predicted band straddles the threshold inside known noise; even success is a ~0.1-point max shift, not a plateau lift (point 4). |
| Fixed-scale-40 cosine classifier | **7** — same-dataset, same-order-of-accuracy published result with a coherent geometric mechanism; docked for winner's-curse anchoring and the unaddressed 256-vs-512-d mismatch (points 5–6). | **8** — the only candidate whose cited effect (+0.29) matches the diagnosed 0.25–0.30 stable-gain requirement, and it targets the plateau (boundary geometry), not the checkpoint-selection maximum. |

**Pick: Fixed-scale-40 cosine-normalized classifier (idea-03).**

It is the only candidate that attacks the diagnosed limiter — a stable generalization gain above the 95.49 plateau — with an effect size that, if it transfers even at half strength, still clears 95.71. Poly-1 and the EMA horizon are both better-specified than they are powerful: each concedes in its own preregistration that the median outcome misses the threshold, which makes them low-information runs regardless of outcome. The cosine classifier is a genuine exploratory bet with real transfer risk (scale sensitivity, dimension mismatch, CutMix interaction under a wall-clock budget), but it is falsifiable in an informative way: a clean failure rejects classifier-geometry leverage on this stack, while either loss-tweak failing tells you almost nothing. Before execution, the proposal should incorporate points 5–7: temper the expected-gain band for selection bias, state the dimension mismatch, and add per-epoch classifier norm and early-progress observability.
