## Prioritized Feedback

**1. All three candidates rest on an unverified transfer of the variance diagnosis (shared, most important).** The "late-iterate variance" evidence is EXP-006's final-four span of 0.15 points and EXP-003's selection reversals — both from *other* nodes. The parent EXP-004's own trajectory shows final = best = 95.40 with improved loss (`experiments/004/04-analysis.md`), i.e. the one trajectory being averaged never exhibited the oscillation the mechanism targets. All three proposals list this as a risk, but none prices it into the hypothesis. Improvement: state the prior honestly (the mechanism is a bet that EXP-004's per-epoch tail history — now deleted — oscillated like EXP-006's), and have the audit's consecutive-sample L2 distances double as the diagnostic that settles this question for future nodes even if accuracy is flat. That salvage value is real and should be preregistered as a named secondary outcome.

**2. Horizon-derived clean-tail EMA has an unacknowledged sampling bias that collapses its distinction from the SAM-pulse variant.** Its eligibility is `next_step % 32 == 0`; SAM fires on even one-based steps (`train.py:165-166`), so *every* EMA sample is a post-SAM state — it is exactly a 1-in-16 subsequence of the SAM-pulse candidate's samples. The proposal presents itself as neutral trajectory sampling while implicitly criticizing the pulse-conditioned idea for "stroboscopic" bias; that framing is wrong. Fix: either make the cadence odd (e.g. 31 — the actual-elapsed-time decay formula in idea-01 already handles uneven intervals correctly, so this is a one-character change that alternates post-ordinary/post-SAM samples), or keep 32 and explicitly attribute the package as "post-SAM-sampled EMA." Either is fine; silence is not.

**3. Epoch-end SWA applies its cited mechanism in precisely the regime its own source says breaks it — and preregisters an undetectable effect.** The SWA note (`knowledge/papers/stochastic-weight-averaging.md`) ties useful averaging to constant/cyclic-LR trajectory diversity; the parent's cosine tail collapses 0.034 → 0.002, and the proposal deliberately refuses the flat-LR tail to avoid bundling. Uniform weighting then gives the high-LR, nonzero-drop-path early-tail samples equal weight, dragging the mean behind the final solution — and its five-sample activation switches all evaluations to that stale mean from ~progress 0.79 onward, hiding every late online checkpoint. Its own expected effect is +0.05 to +0.20, which the goal's learnings classify as below single-run noise ("prioritize candidates with a plausible effect near 0.3 points", `03-experiment-learnings.md`). This is not a hard-constraint violation, but it is a candidate whose honest self-assessment predicts an unresolvable result. Improvement path if pursued anyway: a late-window start (the brainstormed "Uniform SWA + late-window start" combination) removes the stale-early-sample problem, but was not carried into the proposal.

**4. SAM-pulse-conditioned EMA buys its denser sampling at ~16× the charged cost for negligible statistical benefit.** ~2,449 foreach updates over ~160 tensors inside the charged timer versus 153 for the horizon variant, with an effectively equivalent smoothing horizon (512 pulses ≈ 15.7 s vs 18.75 s half-life). Successive tail iterates at LR → 0.002 are highly correlated, so extra sampling density adds almost nothing to the average while directly taxing the validated SAM step budget (its own gate tolerates dropping to 24,800 steps from 25,560). Given finding 2 — the horizon variant samples the same post-SAM subsequence anyway — this candidate's only real differentiators are cost and complexity, both unfavorable. The pulse-count decay parameterization is fine, but not an advantage over actual-elapsed-time decay.

**5. The horizon EMA's expected range 95.70–95.95 is overclaimed relative to its evidence.** The justification ("center the predictor within SAM's late flat basin") imports SWA-paper gain magnitudes from the diverse-LR regime into a collapsed-cosine regime where the same proposal's own risk list says the effect may vanish. A +0.30 to +0.55 expectation on top of a parent whose final already equals its best is not supported by any cited source. Keep the 95.50/95.70 threshold structure, but the stated realistic expectation should be tempered (roughly +0.05 to +0.30) so the tree records an honest prior. This does not change the decision — it changes how a 95.5x result is interpreted.

**6. Minor, shared: linear averaging of `running_var` across weight states systematically understates the averaged model's activation variance** (it ignores the between-state mean shift). Practical EMA implementations do this and survive, and recalibration is correctly prohibited as uncharged training, so this is acceptable — but the audit should log per-BN averaged-vs-live variance ratios so a BN-mismatch failure is diagnosable rather than mysterious.

**7. Compliance check — no fatal issues found in any candidate.** All three keep one evaluator call per epoch with preregistered source routing, charge averaging work inside the timer, prohibit uncharged BN recalibration, use parent-relative gates (per the EXP-008 learning), avoid the EXP-005/008/009 failure modes, and follow EXP-006's explicit "add EMA" recommendation. No reward-hacking smell: the eval-source switch hides checkpoints rather than adding selection pressure. The exact-restore/`finally` machinery in all three is necessary and adequately specified.

## Scored Verdict

Scale 1–10; scores account for the feedback above.

**Epoch-end full-state SWA**
- Evidence/reasoning: **5** — unusually honest about itself, but the honesty amounts to admitting the cited mechanism's precondition (LR diversity) is absent and the expected effect is sub-noise.
- Potential impact: **4** — self-declared ceiling of +0.20 sits below the goal's own detectability bar; stale-mean activation adds real downside.

**SAM-pulse-conditioned EMA**
- Evidence/reasoning: **6** — sound cadence-aware EMA design with strong invariants, but the conditioning claim is admitted as unproven and turns out not to differentiate it from the cheaper variant.
- Potential impact: **6** — same mechanism ceiling as the horizon EMA, discounted slightly by the charged-overhead tax on validated SAM exposure and medium-high effort.

**Horizon-derived clean-tail EMA**
- Evidence/reasoning: **7** — cleanest instantiation of both cited papers: time-derived decay directly implements the EMA-scaling horizon principle, activation-by-copy avoids SWA's stale-mean problem, and overhead is near zero; docked for the unacknowledged post-SAM sampling alignment (finding 2) and the overclaimed range (finding 5).
- Potential impact: **6.5** — same upside as the pulse variant at 1/16 the charged cost, preserving the full validated step budget; a plausible path to ≥+0.30 exists if the parent tail does oscillate, and the diagnostic salvage value (finding 1) is highest here.

**Pick: Horizon-derived clean-tail EMA** (`proposals/idea-01.md`). All three are the same bet on unmeasured parent tail variance, so the winner is the version that maximizes upside per unit of risk to the validated recipe — and that is the horizon EMA: it smooths the same post-SAM subsequence the pulse variant targets while charging ~153 updates instead of ~2,449, its actual-elapsed-time decay is the most faithful reading of the EMA-scaling reference under this wall-clock protocol, and its EMA-starts-as-copy activation avoids the SWA candidate's stale-mean exposure window. Refine before planning: (a) resolve the cadence-parity issue — switch to an odd cadence or explicitly attribute the package as post-SAM-sampled (finding 2); (b) temper the expected range to ~+0.05–0.30 while keeping the 95.50/95.70 thresholds (finding 5); (c) preregister the consecutive-sample and EMA-to-live distance audits as a named secondary outcome so even a flat accuracy result resolves the open question of whether EXP-004's tail has any variance left to average (finding 1).
