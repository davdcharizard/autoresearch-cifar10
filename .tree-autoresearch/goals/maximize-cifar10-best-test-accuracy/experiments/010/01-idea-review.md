## Prioritized Feedback

1. **The two scaling candidates bet against their own diagnosis (WRN-22-4, WRN-16-5).** `02-system-understanding.md` names detectable generalization — not capacity or memory — as the limiter, and the WRN-22-4 proposal itself concedes "near-zero parent training loss also suggests capacity is not the main limiter." Both candidates pay ~33–36% of optimizer updates and unique-image exposure (7.15M → ~4.6–5.25M appearances) for capacity of unproven marginal value, while EXP-005 showed that merely halving new identities at *constant* steps cost 0.12 points. Neither proposal quantifies why +56% parameters should outweigh that exposure loss; the expected ranges (95.45–95.75, 95.5–95.8) are asserted, not derived. Fix: treat these ranges as low-confidence and treat the a-priori acceptance of ~18–20k steps — not the latency gate — as the real go/no-go decision.

2. **The 1-2-3 proposal's literature support is looser than presented.** PyramidNet's actual finding is *gradual widening* with per-block projections; 1-2-3 implements back-loaded stage depth, which the paper never tests. The proposal admits the exact allocation is exploratory, but the 95.53–95.80 point estimate has no source that sizes the effect at ≥0.30. Fix: keep the preregistered ≥0.30 falsification threshold, but the winning argument for this idea is its *bounded downside* (equal MACs, ~equal steps, 1.05× gate), not its point estimate — the brainstorm's Chosen Idea rationale should say so explicitly.

3. **1-2-3's key unmitigated assumption: one 64-channel block at 32×32 suffices for local feature composition.** The risk is named ("later capacity cannot recover lost spatial detail") but nothing probes it. Cheap fix that changes no protocol: log the early-epoch train-loss/accuracy trajectory against the parent's, so a failure can be attributed post-hoc to early-feature starvation rather than left as an unexplained miss.

4. **WRN-16-5's preflight gate is nearly non-binding and shouldn't be framed as protection.** MAC arithmetic already predicts a 1.554× ratio against a 1.60× gate — the gate only catches pathological kernel selection and does nothing about the central exposure-loss risk. Contrast the 1-2-3 proposal's genuinely binding 1.05×. Not a fatal flaw, but the proposal's "valuable only if preflight confirms" framing overstates what the gate verifies.

5. **WRN-22-4 is dominated by WRN-16-5 within this slate.** Both are ~1.56–1.58× MAC, +56%-parameter capacity bets; classic WRN evidence (Zagoruyko & Komodakis — notably uncited) shows width beats depth at matched parameters on CIFAR, and the proposal's own ICLR-2024 citation warns the LR optimum does not transfer across depth changes — untunable under the fixed single-run recipe. Depth adds risk without adding distinct upside over the width variant.

6. **Minor, unaccounted headwind for both scaling ideas: fewer max-over draws.** `best_test_acc` is a max over per-epoch evaluations with a ~0.15-point tail spread; dropping from 144 to ~92–105 epochs removes ~40 draws from that max — a small systematic penalty neither expected-range accounts for. 1-2-3 keeps ~136–144 evals.

7. **Minor confound in 1-2-3, worth one sentence in the plan:** reallocating blocks shifts the per-position drop-path rates (stage 1's sole block gets 0.0133 vs the parent's 0.0133/0.0267 pair), so regularization dose moves with architecture. Attribution is package-level for all three; just state it.

8. **What's sound (no invented flaws):** all three preregister ≥0.30 detectable-effect targets consistent with the 0.14–0.29 noise learning, use same-harness parent-relative gates that correctly absorb the EXP-008 absolute-floor error, forbid post-hoc tuning/seed selection, and stay within hard constraints (train.py-only, GPU 0, budget preserved). No reward-hacking smell in any candidate; the exact-count verification sections are exemplary. No fatal disqualifiers found.

## Scored Verdict

| Idea | Evidence & reasoning | Potential impact |
|---|---|---|
| Back-loaded 1-2-3 stage depth | **8/10** — exact, verified complexity accounting; uniquely attacks the diagnosed generalization limiter while preserving the exposure history says matters; docked for the stretched PyramidNet citation and unvalidated exact allocation. | **7/10** — moderate ceiling (~0.3–0.6) with near-zero structural downside: equal MACs, ~equal steps, tightly gated at 1.05×. |
| Wider WRN-16-5 | **6/10** — width is the strongest classical CIFAR prior of the three and the 16-aligned dense-kernel argument is sound, but the fixed-epoch literature doesn't transfer to a fixed-wall-clock regime and the 1.60× gate is non-binding. | **6.5/10** — highest raw ceiling if capacity binds, discounted by the ~35% exposure loss, ~40 fewer max-over evals, and inherited-LR risk. |
| Deeper WRN-22-4 | **5/10** — honest but self-undermining: concedes capacity isn't the limiter and that the LR optimum may not transfer with depth, then fixes LR anyway. | **6/10** — same cost as WRN-16-5 with the weaker scaling axis; must overcome a 33% exposure deficit before any gain registers. |

**Pick: Back-loaded 1-2-3 stage depth (idea-01).** It is the only candidate whose mechanism is aligned with the diagnosed bottleneck — spending the *same* convolutional budget on more class-specific features — rather than a capacity bet that must first pay back a one-third exposure loss the lineage's own evidence (EXP-005, the step-preserving pattern behind every prior gain) says is expensive. Its accounting is exact and verified, its preflight is the only genuinely binding one, and its failure mode is cheap: if the allocation is null, the run costs nothing structurally and yields a clean negative on an untested axis EXP-009 explicitly recommended. The scaling ideas' higher raw ceilings are speculative precisely where the tree has the most contrary evidence. Refinements before planning: reframe the win condition around bounded downside (feedback 2), add the early-trajectory diagnostic (feedback 3), and note the drop-path redistribution confound (feedback 7).
